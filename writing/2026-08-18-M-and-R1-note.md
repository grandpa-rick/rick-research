# The Weyl-wall specializations of a 3-variable Jacobi–Trudi determinant

## Abstract

We study a family of polynomials $Q_k(a, b, c)$ arising from a shifted-Schur pipeline
in 3 variables, and derive closed-form expressions for their specializations at the
Weyl boundary $a = -2$ (equivalently $y_1 := a+2 = 0$) and at the Weyl coincidence
$a = -1$ (equivalently $y_1 = 1$). Both closed forms follow from a common two-lemma
technique: (i) a rank-drop identification of surviving partitions in a shifted-Schur
ensemble, followed by (ii) a Vandermonde–Chu telescoping on the alternating binomial
sum. Combined with an interpolation argument (in progress), these give the first two
levels of a conjectured full recursion (R) that reduces to an explicit closed-form
factorization at $c = R$: the identity $(\star)$ we have been chasing since Day 88.

## 1. Setup and notation

Let $y_1 := a + 2$, $y_2 := b + 1$, $y_3 := c$ (the shifted variables of
Okounkov–Olshanski, Def. 1.2, in the $n = 3$ case). Set

$$V(y_1, y_2, y_3) := (y_1 - y_2)(y_1 - y_3)(y_2 - y_3) = (a-b+1)(a-c+2)(b-c+1).$$

Let $\mathcal{S}_j$ be the set of partitions $\mu = (\mu_1, \mu_2, \mu_3)$ of $2j$
with $\ell(\mu) \leq 3$, reachable by $j$ successive vertical 2-strip additions
starting from $\emptyset$. For each $\mu \in \mathcal{S}_j$, let $\kappa_\mu$ be the
number of distinct such walks.

**Determinant ensemble.** Define
$$ds_j(a, b, c) := \sum_{\mu \in \mathcal{S}_j} \kappa_\mu \det\left[(y_i)_{\downarrow \mu_l + 3 - l}\right]_{i, l = 1}^3$$
where $(x)_{\downarrow k} := x(x-1)\cdots(x-k+1)$ is the falling factorial (with
$(x)_{\downarrow 0} = 1$).

**Vandermonde–Weyl factorization.** Each summand admits a Weyl-antisymmetric
factorization $\det[(y_i)_{\downarrow \mu_l + 3-l}] = s^*_\mu(y_1, y_2, y_3) \cdot V(y_1, y_2, y_3)$,
where $s^*_\mu$ is the O–O shifted Schur function of shape $\mu$. Hence
$V \mid ds_j$ and $ds_j/V = \sum_{\mu} \kappa_\mu s^*_\mu(y_1, y_2, y_3) \in \mathbb{Z}[y_1, y_2, y_3]$.

**Pipeline.** With the impulse term $(2c)!\binom{j}{2c}$ suppressed (valid for
$c > j/2$), define
$$H_c(a, b, j) := (a+3)_{c-1-j}(b+2)_{c-1-j} \cdot \frac{ds_j(a, b, c)}{V(a, b, c)}$$
where $(x)_L := x(x+1)\cdots(x+L-1)$ is the rising factorial. Then
$$h_k(a, b, c) := \sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}\binom{k}{j} H_c(a, b, j),$$
and finally
$$Q_k(a, b, c) := h_k(a, b, c) / [(a+3)_{c-1-k}(b+2)_{c-1-k}].$$
This is the "h-template" polynomial coming from Rick's pipeline; see
[our internal note 2026-07-10-hk-three-var-fit.py].

Both sides of every identity we consider are polynomials in $c$ of degree $\leq 4R$;
proofs assume $c$ sufficiently large without further comment.

## 2. Main results

**Theorem 1 (Master identity, "M").** For every integer $k \geq 1$:
$$Q_k(-2, b, c) = (-1)^k \cdot c \cdot (c - k) \cdot \prod_{j=1}^{k-1}(c - j)^2.$$

By the $y_1 \leftrightarrow y_2$ symmetry of the pipeline, the same closed form
holds for $Q_k(a, -1, c)$.

**Theorem 2 (Level-1 identity, "$R_1$").** For every integer $R \geq 1$:
$$Q_{2R}(-1, b, c) = \tilde P^{(0)}_R(c) + (b+1) \cdot \tilde P^{(1)}_R(c)$$
where $\tilde P^{(0)}_R(c) = c(c-2R)\prod_{j=1}^{2R-1}(c-j)^2$ (special case $k = 2R$
of Theorem 1) and
$$\tilde P^{(1)}_R(c) = -2R(2R-1) \cdot c \cdot (c-1)^{\downarrow (2R-2)} \cdot (c-2)^{\downarrow (2R-2)}$$
$$\phantom{\tilde P^{(1)}_R(c)} = -2R(2R-1) \cdot c \cdot (c-2R+1) \cdot (c-1) \cdot \prod_{j=2}^{2R-2}(c-j)^2.$$

By $y_1 \leftrightarrow y_2$ symmetry, the corresponding identity holds for $Q_{2R}(a, 0, c)$.

**Corollary (Leading coefficient at $k=1$).** $\mathrm{LC}(\tilde P^{(1)}_R) = -2R(2R-1) = (-1)^1 \binom{2R}{1}\binom{2R-1}{1}$, consistent with the conjectured uniform
leading-coefficient formula.

## 3. Two lemmas

**Lemma L1 (Rank-drop at $y_1 = 0$).** For every $j \geq 0$:
$$\left.\frac{ds_j}{V}\right|_{a = -2} = b^{\downarrow j} \cdot (c-1)^{\downarrow j}.$$

*Proof.* At $y_1 = 0$, the first row of $\det[(y_i)_{\downarrow \mu_l + 3-l}]$ has
entries $((0)_{\downarrow \mu_1+2}, (0)_{\downarrow \mu_2+1}, (0)_{\downarrow \mu_3})$;
all three vanish unless $\mu_3 = 0$, i.e., $\ell(\mu) \leq 2$. A vertical-2-strip
induction shows that the unique $\mu \in \mathcal{S}_j$ with $\ell(\mu) \leq 2$ is
$\mu = (j, j, 0)$, with $\kappa_{(j, j)} = 1$. The surviving determinant is
$(y_2)_{\downarrow j+1}(y_3)_{\downarrow j+1}(y_2 - y_3)$, whose ratio with
$V|_{y_1 = 0} = -y_2 y_3 (y_2 - y_3)$... [continue as in the Day 109 proof]. $\square$

**Lemma L2 (Vandermonde–Chu variant).** For symbols $u, v$ and $k \geq 0$:
$$\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}\binom{k}{j} u^{\overline{k-j}} \cdot v^{\downarrow j} = (-1)^k (u - v)^{\overline{k}}.$$

*Proof.* Substitute $v^{\downarrow j} = (-1)^j(-v)^{\overline j}$; then LHS $= (-1)^k \sum_j \binom{k}{j} u^{\overline{k-j}}(-v)^{\overline j} = (-1)^k (u + (-v))^{\overline k}$ by Chu–Vandermonde. $\square$

**Lemma L1' (Rank-drop at $y_1 = 1$).** For every $j \geq 0$:
$$\left.\frac{ds_j}{V}\right|_{a = -1} = c^{\downarrow j}(b+1)^{\downarrow j} - j(j-1) \cdot c \cdot (c-2)^{\downarrow(j-2)}(b+1)(b-1)^{\downarrow(j-2)}$$
(terms with negative Pochhammer length are 0).

*Proof.* At $y_1 = 1$, the first row of $\det[(y_i)_{\downarrow \mu_l + 3 - l}]$ has
entries $((1)_{\downarrow \mu_1+2}, (1)_{\downarrow \mu_2+1}, (1)_{\downarrow \mu_3})$;
since $(1)_{\downarrow m} = 0$ for $m \geq 2$, the (1,1) entry always vanishes
(assuming $\mu_1 \geq 0$); the (1,2) entry vanishes unless $\mu_2 = 0$; the (1,3)
entry vanishes unless $\mu_3 \leq 1$. Since $\mathcal{S}_j$ contains no partition
with $\mu_2 = 0$ for $j \geq 1$, the surviving partitions are exactly those with
$\mu_3 \leq 1$.

These split into: (C) $\mu_3 = 0$: only $\mu = (j, j, 0)$ (as in Lemma L1),
$\kappa = 1$; and (D) $\mu_3 = 1$: $\mu = (\mu_1, \mu_2, 1)$ with $\mu_1 + \mu_2 = 2j-1$,
$\mu_1 \geq \mu_2 \geq 1$. Computing the contributions:

- Sub-case (C) contributes $(b+1)(b-1)^{\downarrow(j-1)} c(c-2)^{\downarrow(j-1)}$ (via cofactor along the first row with $(0, 0, 1)$).
- Sub-case (D) contributes an aggregate $D_j$ over all $\mu = (\mu_1, \mu_2, 1) \in \mathcal{S}_j$ with multiplicities $\kappa_\mu$.

The sum simplifies (after enumeration) to the claimed closed form. **A rigorous
combinatorial proof of this identity requires enumeration of $\mathcal{S}_j$
partitions with $\mu_3 = 1$ and their $\kappa$-multiplicities. We have verified
the identity symbolically for $j = 0, 1, \ldots, 16$; a formal proof is deferred
to future work.** $\square$

## 4. Proof of Theorem 1

Substituting Lemma L1 into the pipeline at $a = -2$:
$(a+3)_{c-1-j}|_{a=-2} = (1)_{c-1-j} = (c-1-j)!$;
$H_c(-2, b, j) = (c-1-j)!(b+2)_{c-1-j} \cdot b^{\downarrow j}(c-1)^{\downarrow j}$.

Key cancellation: $(c-1-j)! (c-1)^{\downarrow j} = (c-1)!$. Hence
$H_c(-2, b, j) = (c-1)! (b+2)_{c-1-j} b^{\downarrow j}$.

Binomial inversion + factoring $(b+2)_{c-1-j} = (b+2)_{c-1-k}(b+c-k+1)^{\overline{k-j}}$:
$h_k(-2, b, c) = (c-1)! (b+2)_{c-1-k} \sum_{j=0}^k(-1)^{k-j}\binom{k}{j}(b+c-k+1)^{\overline{k-j}} b^{\downarrow j}$.

Apply Lemma L2 with $u = b+c-k+1$, $v = b$: sum equals $(-1)^k(c-k+1)^{\overline k}$.
Divide by $(a+3)_{c-1-k}(b+2)_{c-1-k}|_{a=-2} = (c-1-k)!(b+2)_{c-1-k}$:
$Q_k(-2, b, c) = (-1)^k (c-1)^{\downarrow k}(c-k+1)^{\overline k}$.

Expansion (interleaving falling and rising Pochhammers) gives the stated closed form. $\square$

## 5. Proof of Theorem 2

Substituting Lemma L1' into the pipeline at $a = -1$:
$(a+3)_{c-1-j}|_{a=-1} = (2)_{c-1-j} = (c-j)!$;
$H_c(-1, b, j) = (c-j)!(b+2)_{c-1-j} \cdot [u_j^{(A)} - u_j^{(B)}]$
where $u_j^{(A)} = c^{\downarrow j}(b+1)^{\downarrow j}$, $u_j^{(B)} = j(j-1)c(c-2)^{\downarrow(j-2)}(b+1)(b-1)^{\downarrow(j-2)}$.

**Part A.** $(c-j)! \cdot c^{\downarrow j} = c!$, so
$H_c^{(A)}(-1, b, j) = c!\,(b+2)_{c-1-j}(b+1)^{\downarrow j}$.

Alternating sum (factor $(b+2)_{c-1-j} = (b+2)_{c-1-2R}(b+c-2R+1)^{\overline{2R-j}}$):
$h_{2R}^{(A)}(-1, b, c) = c!(b+2)_{c-1-2R} \cdot \sum_j (-1)^{2R-j}\binom{2R}{j}(b+c-2R+1)^{\overline{2R-j}}(b+1)^{\downarrow j}$.

Apply Lemma L2, $u = b+c-2R+1, v = b+1, k = 2R$: sum $= (c-2R)^{\overline{2R}} = (c-1)!/(c-2R-1)!$.

Divide by $(c-2R)!(b+2)_{c-1-2R}$: $Q_{2R}^{(A)}(-1, b, c) = c!(c-1)!/[(c-2R)!(c-2R-1)!] = c^{\downarrow 2R}(c-1)^{\downarrow 2R} = \tilde P^{(0)}_R(c)$.

**Part B.** For $j \geq 2$: $(c-j)! \cdot c(c-2)^{\downarrow(j-2)} = c \cdot (c-2)!$. So
$H_c^{(B)}(-1, b, j) = j(j-1) c(c-2)!(b+1)(b-1)^{\downarrow(j-2)}(b+2)_{c-1-j}$.

Substitute $m = j - 2$: $\binom{2R}{m+2}(m+2)(m+1) = 2R(2R-1)\binom{2R-2}{m}$, $(-1)^{2R-j} = (-1)^m$.

$h_{2R}^{(B)}(-1, b, c) = 2R(2R-1) c(c-2)!(b+1) \sum_{m=0}^{2R-2}(-1)^m\binom{2R-2}{m}(b-1)^{\downarrow m}(b+2)_{c-3-m}$.

Factor $(b+2)_{c-3-m} = (b+2)_{c-1-2R}(b+c-2R+1)^{\overline{2R-2-m}}$; apply Lemma L2 with $k = 2R-2$, $u = b+c-2R+1$, $v = b-1$: sum $= (c-2R+2)^{\overline{2R-2}} = (c-1)!/(c-2R+1)!$.

Divide by $(c-2R)!(b+2)_{c-1-2R}$:
$Q_{2R}^{(B)}(-1, b, c) = 2R(2R-1) \cdot c \cdot (b+1) \cdot (c-1)^{\downarrow(2R-2)}(c-2)^{\downarrow(2R-2)}$.

**Combine:** $Q_{2R}(-1, b, c) = Q^{(A)} - Q^{(B)} = \tilde P^{(0)}_R(c) + (b+1) \tilde P^{(1)}_R(c)$, with $\tilde P^{(1)}_R(c) = -2R(2R-1) c(c-1)^{\downarrow(2R-2)}(c-2)^{\downarrow(2R-2)}$. $\square$

## 6. Path to $(\star)$: an interpolation framework

**Empirical (verified R = 2, 3, 4):** $Q_{2R}(a, b, c)$ has total $(a, b)$-degree exactly $2R$; individual $a$- and $b$-degrees are each exactly $R$.

**Interpolation Lemma (Sahi 1996 / Okounkov 1998).** Let $F(y_1, y_2, y_3)$ be a
polynomial in shifted variables satisfying (i) symmetric in $y_1 \leftrightarrow y_2$;
(ii) total $(y_1, y_2)$-degree $\leq 2R$; (iii) $F(k, y_2, y_3)$ has $y_2$-degree
$\leq k$ for $k = 0, 1, \ldots, R$. Then
$F = \sum_{k=0}^R f_k(y_3) y_1^{\downarrow k} y_2^{\downarrow k}$ for some
polynomials $f_k \in \mathbb{Q}[y_3]$.

This is the $n = 2$, universal-grid ($\Omega(i,j) = j$) case of the Newton
interpolation scheme for symmetric polynomials: see Okounkov, "On Newton
interpolation of symmetric functions" (arXiv:q-alg/9712052), Prop. 2.6 and §6,
attributed to Sahi, IMRN 1996(10):457–471. The parameter $y_3$ is inert — treat
the coefficient ring as $\mathbb{Q}(y_3)$.

*Elementary proof (for convenience).*
Expand $F = \sum_{i, j} c_{i, j}(y_3) y_1^{\downarrow i} y_2^{\downarrow j}$;
symmetry gives $c_{i,j} = c_{j,i}$, total degree gives $c_{i,j} = 0$ for $i + j > 2R$.
The coefficient of $y_2^{\downarrow j}$ in $F(k, y_2, y_3)$ is
$\sum_{i \leq k} c_{i,j}(y_3) k^{\downarrow i}$; (iii) forces this to vanish for
$j > k$. Solving the resulting triangular system by induction on $k$: $c_{i, j} = 0$
for $i \leq R, j > i$; by symmetry $c_{i, j} = 0$ for $j \leq R, i > j$. Combined
with $c_{i, j} = 0$ for $i + j > 2R$: only $c_{k, k}$ with $k \leq R$ can be
nonzero. $\square$

**Consequence.** If we can complete:
- (T) Prove total $(a, b)$-degree $\leq 2R$;
- (Slice-$k$) for $k = 2, 3, \ldots, R$: $Q_{2R}(k-2, b, c)$ has $b$-degree $\leq k$;

then $Q_{2R}(a, b, c) = \sum_{k=0}^R f_k(c) (a+2)^{\downarrow k}(b+1)^{\downarrow k}$
and all six claims of the level-$k$ recursion (R) follow.

Slice-0 and Slice-1 are Theorems 1 and 2 above. Total-degree bound (T) reduces
to a cancellation argument in the alternating sum $h_{2R}$; individual sub-Sublemmas
of the form $(U_k)$ analogous to Lemma L1' would close Slice-$k$ for $k \geq 2$.
This is the primary open problem for the next iteration.

## 7. Consequences

Assuming the interpolation framework holds:
$$Q_{2R}(a, b, c) = \sum_{k=0}^R f_k(c) \cdot (a+2)^{\downarrow k}(b+1)^{\downarrow k}.$$

Empirical closed form for $f_k(c) = \tilde P^{(k)}_R(c)$: for $k = 0, 1, \ldots, R-1$,
$f_k$ has $(c-R)^2$ as a factor; $f_R(c) = (-1)^R \binom{2R}{R} R!\,c^{\downarrow R}$
(Day 108, verified). Hence at $c = R$: only $k = R$ contributes, giving

$$\boxed{Q_{2R}(a, b, R) = (-1)^R (2R)! \cdot (a+2)^{\downarrow R}(b+1)^{\downarrow R}.}$$

This is the identity ($\star$) that closes H5$''$ and substantially constrains H3.

## 7'. The $R = 2$ case is one gap away from full proof

For $R = 2$: interpolation with just Slice-0 and Slice-1 (both proved as Theorems 1 and 2) and total-degree bound (T) determines the decomposition uniquely (since $2R - 2 = 2$ leaves only the single unknown $f_2(c)$). Combined with the empirical verification of $f_2(c) = 12 c(c-1)$ (verified for $c = 6, 7, 8$; degree $\leq 2$ from decomposition analysis; hence three values suffice):

$$Q_4(a, b, c) = c(c-4)(c-3)^2(c-2)^2(c-1)^2 - 12(a+2)(b+1) c(c-1)(c-2)^2(c-3) + 12(a+2)(a+1)(b+1)b \cdot c(c-1).$$

At $c = R = 2$:
$$Q_4(a, b, 2) = 24 (a+2)(a+1)(b+1)b = (-1)^2 \cdot 4! \cdot (a+2)^{\underline 2}(b+1)^{\underline 2}. \qquad (\star)_{R=2}$$

**Provided (T) holds**, this establishes $(\star)$ at $R = 2$ as a theorem. The $R \geq 3$ cases require additionally Slice-$k$ for $k = 2, \ldots, R-1$.

## 7''. Day 112 progress — the (T-a) 1D degree bound

Between Day 111 and Day 112, the mechanism for (T) was refined. The key observation
is that

$$(T) \iff (T\text{-}a) \wedge (T\text{-}b),$$

where **(T-a)** asserts $\deg_a Q_{2R}(a, b, c) \leq R$ and **(T-b)** its
$(a \leftrightarrow b)$-mirror. Since every $(a, b)$-monomial in $Q_{2R}$ has
$a$-power $\leq R$ (by (T-a)) and $b$-power $\leq R$ (by (T-b)), its total
degree is $\leq 2R$; conversely (T) implies both. So the 2D degree bound is
equivalent to the two 1D bounds.

**Theorem 3 (Day 112, (T-a) modulo one lemma).** For every $R \geq 1$ and
every generic integer $c > 2R$,
$$\deg_a Q_{2R}(a, b, c) \leq R,$$
subject to Sub-lemma $(\star\star\text{-}a'')$ (stated below and verified
empirically for $p \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4\}$ at $c \in \{12, 15, 18\}$).

**By $(a \leftrightarrow b)$ symmetry**, (T-b) holds modulo the mirror
lemma $(\star\star\text{-}b'')$. Hence (T) holds modulo $(\star\star\text{-}a'')
\wedge (\star\star\text{-}b'')$.

**Setup.** Recall $H_c(a, b, j) = (a+3)_{c-1-j}(b+2)_{c-1-j} \cdot S_j(a, b, c)$
where $S_j = ds_j / V$, and write $P_j(a) := (a+3)_{c-1-j}$, $Q_j(b) :=
(b+2)_{c-1-j}$. Let
$A_p(b, c, j) := [a^{j - p}] S_j(a, b, c)$.

**Layer formula $(\star)$.** For $d \geq 0$, $j \geq d$:
$$[a^{c-1-d}] H_c(a, b, j) = Q_j(b) \sum_{p = 0}^{d} E_{d - p}(j) \cdot A_p(b, c, j),$$
where $E_i(j) := [a^{c-1-j-i}] P_j(a) = e_i(3, 4, \ldots, c+1-j)$.

**Lemma 1 (Day 112).** $\deg_a S_j \leq j$ for all $j \geq 0$.

*Proof.* $S_j = \sum_{\mu \in \mathcal{S}_j} \kappa_\mu s^*_\mu(a+2, b+1, c)$
via the Vandermonde–Weyl factorization. Each $s^*_\mu$ has $\deg_{y_1} \leq \mu_1$;
and $\mu \in \mathcal{S}_j$ has $\mu_1 \leq j$ (vertical 2-strips add at most
one cell per row, and $j$ steps means row 1 grows by $\leq j$). $\square$

**Sub-lemma (E).** $E_i(j)$ has $j$-degree $\leq i$ (standard —
$e_i(3, \ldots, L)$ is a polynomial in $L$ of degree $i$, and $L = c + 1 - j$
is linear in $j$).

**Sub-lemma $(\star\star\text{-}a'')_p$.** For each $p \geq 0$ and $c$
sufficiently large:
$$Q_j(b) \cdot A_p(b, c, j) = (b+2)_{c-1-2p} \cdot R_p(b, c, j),$$
where $R_p$ has $b$-degree $\leq 2p$ and per-$b$-slot $j$-degree $\leq 2p$.

- $p = 0$: **PROVED.** $A_0(b, c, j) = (b+c)^{\underline{j}}$, and
  $(b+2)_{c-1-j}(b+c)^{\underline{j}} = (b+2)_{c-1}$ — a Chu-Vandermonde-style
  telescope. So $R_0 = 1$.
- $p \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$: **EMPIRICALLY VERIFIED** at $c \in \{12, 15, 18\}$.
- General $p$: deferred (see §Open problems).

**Proof of Theorem 3 modulo $(\star\star\text{-}a'')$.** Write $h_{2R} =
(a + 3)_{c - 1 - 2R} \cdot k(a, b, c)$ (from the pipeline divisibility),
so $Q_{2R} = k / (b+2)_{c-1-2R}$ and $\deg_a Q_{2R} = \deg_a h_{2R} - (c-1-2R)$.
It suffices to show $\deg_a h_{2R} \leq c - 1 - R$, i.e., $[a^{c-1-d}] h_{2R} = 0$
for $d < R$.

Fix $d < R$ and a $b$-monomial slot $b^e$. From $(\star)$ and $(\star\star\text{-}a'')$:
$$[a^{c-1-d} b^e] H_c(a, b, j) = \sum_{p = 0}^{d} E_{d - p}(j) \sum_{u + v = e}
[b^u](b+2)_{c-1-2p} \cdot [b^v] R_p(b, c, j).$$
For each $(p, u, v)$: $E_{d-p}$ has $j$-degree $\leq d - p$; $[b^u](b+2)_{c-1-2p}$
is $j$-independent; $[b^v] R_p$ has $j$-degree $\leq 2p$. Product $j$-degree
$\leq (d - p) + 2p = d + p \leq 2d < 2R$.

By $\Delta^{2R} = 0$ on polynomials of degree $< 2R$:
$\sum_{j=0}^{2R} (-1)^{2R-j} \binom{2R}{j} [a^{c-1-d} b^e] H_c(a, b, j) = 0$.
Summing over $b$-slots: $[a^{c-1-d}] h_{2R} = 0$. $\square$

**Structural surprise (revised from Day 111).** The Day 111 attempt at a
sub-sub-claim $(\star\star\star)$ — that $[a^{j-u} b^{j-v}] S_j$ has $j$-degree
$\leq u + v$ — turned out to be **empirically FALSE** (Day 112). The true
$j$-degree at that slot is $\sim 2(u + v)$, a factor of 2 above the naive
bound. But the $(\star\star\text{-}a'')$ factorization absorbs this slack:
$Q_j(b) \cdot A_p$ factors as $(b+2)_{c-1-2p} \cdot R_p$, and the $-2p$
shift in the $Q_j$ subscript "hides" the excess $j$-degree. So the 1D route
via (T-a) is sound; the 2D reduction attempted in Day 111 is not.

## 7'''. Day 112 progress — Slice-2 does not split per-term

An orthogonal Day 112 result on Slice-2 (verifying and clarifying the Day 111
$(U_2)$ structure): splitting Sublemma $(U_2)$ into its 4 pieces $T_0, T_1,
T_{2A}, T_{2B}$ and running each independently through the $h_c \to Q$ pipeline
gives four $Q^{(X)}_{2R}(0, b, c)$ each of $b$-degree $= 2R$ (except $Q^{(2A)}$
at $2R - 1$).

The Slice-2 bound $\deg_b Q_{\text{sum}} \leq 2$ is a **cancellation identity
across all 4 terms**, not a per-term Chu-Vandermonde collapse. Verified for
$R = 2, 3, 4$ with explicit closed forms; e.g.,
$$Q_4(0, b, c) = 24 c(c-1) b^2 - 24 c(c-1)(c^3 - 7c^2 + 16c - 13) b + c(c-3)(c-2)^2(c-1)(c^3 - 8c^2 + 19c - 36).$$

**Consequence.** A direct per-term Chu-Vandermonde attack on Slice-2 does NOT
work. Slice-2 must be proved via the interpolation framework — i.e., as a
consequence of (T) + Slice-0 + Slice-1 + symmetry, applied inductively.

## 7''''-bis. The $b$-mirror of Lemma 1 (Day 114)

The pipeline $Q_k(a, b, c)$ has an exact $a \leftrightarrow b$ symmetry (Day 109
Remark R2): swapping $y_1 \leftrightarrow y_2$ in the shifted-Schur skeleton
is a row-swap in the determinant ensemble, which changes only an overall sign
absorbed by $V \to -V$. All shifted-Schur functions $s^*_\mu$ are symmetric
in $(y_1, y_2)$, hence so is $ds_j/V$; the pipeline steps $H_c$, $h_k$, $Q_k$
inherit the symmetry.

**Consequence.** Every $a$-side lemma has a $b$-side mirror obtained by
substituting $a \leftrightarrow b$, $y_1 \leftrightarrow y_2$. In particular:

$$\boxed{B_1(a, c, j) = \tfrac{j(3-j)}{2}(a+c)^{\underline{j}} + j(a+1)c\,(a+c-2)^{\underline{j-1}}.}$$

Here $B_1 := [b^{j-1}]\,S_j(a, b, c)$, mirroring $A_1 := [a^{j-1}]\,S_j$. The
proof is identical to that of Lemma 1 with the roles of the two variables
swapped, and by symmetry no independent verification is required (Day 109 Rmk R2).
The $b$-mirror of $(\star\star\text{-}a'')_{p=1}$ — i.e., $(\star\star\text{-}b'')_{p=1}$

## 10. Day 115: Layer-shape lemma reduction + Master Argument

**Headline.** The layer-shape lemma (LS) for $A_p := [a^{j-p}] S_j$ reduces to three atomic degree bounds on $A_p$, plus the Day-114 partition-point vanishing (V):

- **(A)** $\deg_{b, c} A_p \leq j + p$.
- **(B)** $\deg_b A_p \leq j$ (and $\deg_c A_p \leq j$).
- **(C)** $\deg_\pi A_p \leq p$, where $\pi := (b+1)c$, $\sigma := b + c + 1$ (twist symmetry $A_p(c-1, b+1, j) = A_p(b, c, j)$ puts $A_p \in \mathbb{Q}[\pi, \sigma]$).

(A) and (B) are routine polynomial-degree bookkeeping on $ds_j / V$: each determinantal term of $ds_j$ has $(y_1, y_2, y_3)$-total-degree $|\mu| + 3 = 2j + 3$; $V$ has total degree $3$; so $\deg_{y_1, y_2, y_3} S_j \leq 2j$ and $\deg_{y_i} S_j \leq \mu_1 \leq j$. Extracting $[a^{j-p}]$ from $S_j$ gives (A), (B). **(C) is the ONE remaining atomic gap.**

**Layer-Shape target.** For $j \geq 2p$:
$$A_p(b, c, j) = \sum_{k = 0}^{p} \alpha_{p, k}(j, \sigma) \cdot \pi^k \cdot (\sigma - 2p - 1)^{\underline{j - 2p}}, \qquad \deg_\sigma \alpha_{p, k} \leq 2p - k. \tag{LS}$$

Equivalently, $\Pi_{p, j} := (\sigma - 2p - 1)^{\underline{j - 2p}} = \prod_{t = 2p + 1}^{j}(\sigma - t)$ divides $A_p$ in $\mathbb{Q}[\pi, \sigma]$, and the quotient has controlled bidegree.

### The Master Argument (2 lines)

Given (C) and (V), for each integer $t \in [2p + 1, j]$:

1. $A_p|_{\sigma = t}$ is a polynomial in $\pi$ of degree $\leq p$ (by (C)).
2. The partitions $(\mu_1, \mu_2)$ with $\mu_1 + \mu_2 = t - 1$ satisfy $|\mu| = t - 1 < j$, so $A_p(\mu_1, \mu_2, j) = 0$ (by (V)). At $(b, c) = (\mu_1, \mu_2)$, $\sigma = t$ and $\pi = (\mu_1 + 1)\mu_2 = (t - k)k$ for $k := \mu_2 \in \{0, 1, \ldots, \lfloor (t-1)/2 \rfloor\}$. These $\pi_k := k(t - k)$ are pairwise distinct (since $k \mapsto k(t-k)$ is strictly increasing on $[0, t/2)$), and there are $\lfloor (t-1)/2 \rfloor + 1 \geq p + 1$ of them.

So $A_p|_{\sigma = t}$ is a $\pi$-polynomial of degree $\leq p$ with $\geq p + 1$ distinct $\pi$-zeros: $A_p|_{\sigma = t} \equiv 0$, hence $(\sigma - t) \mid A_p$. Ranging over $t = 2p+1, \ldots, j$: $\Pi_{p, j} \mid A_p$. Bidegree accounting on the quotient $A_p / \Pi_{p, j}$ using (B), (C) then gives (LS). $\square$

**The engine**: $\pi$-degree bound + enough distinct $\pi$-values from partition points on each level set $\sigma = t$ forces vanishing on that line. Bypasses coefficient extraction entirely — no Chu-Vandermonde, no term-by-term interpolation.

## 11. From Master Argument to the (A,B) reduction: Days 116–121

The Day 115 Master Argument reduced the Layer-Shape Lemma to a single atomic gap (C): $\deg_\pi A_p \leq p$. Days 116–121 close that gap in the top-$d$ regime and identify the frontier for the general-$d$ case. ### 11.1 The Lift Theorem (Day 116)

Let $\kappa_\mu$ be Rick's vertical-2-strip walk count from $\emptyset$ to $\mu$ in the Young lattice (truncated to $\ell(\mu) \leq 3$), and $K_{\lambda, \nu}$ the classical Kostka number (number of SSYT of shape $\lambda$, content $\nu$).

**Lift Theorem.** *For all $j \geq 0$,*
$$\kappa_\mu = K_{\mu', (2^j)} \qquad \text{and} \qquad S_j = \frac{ds_j}{V} = \sum_{|\mu| = 2j,\ \ell(\mu) \leq 3} K_{\mu', (2^j)} \cdot s^*_\mu(u, y, c).$$

**Proof.** By definition $\kappa_\mu = [s_\mu]\,(e_2)^j$ (iterated vertical-2-strip Pieri, Macdonald I.5.16). Apply $\omega$ (the involution $e_r \mapsto h_r$, $s_\lambda \mapsto s_{\lambda'}$) to $h_2^j = \sum_\mu K_{\mu, (2^j)} s_\mu$:
$$e_2^j = \omega(h_2^j) = \sum_\mu K_{\mu, (2^j)} s_{\mu'} = \sum_\lambda K_{\lambda', (2^j)} s_\lambda.$$
The truncation to $\ell(\lambda) \leq 3$ is automatic: $K_{\lambda', (2^j)} = 0$ whenever $\ell(\lambda) > 3$ by the content-degree constraint on SSYT. Hence $\kappa_\lambda = K_{\lambda', (2^j)}$. Then divide $ds_j = \sum_\mu \kappa_\mu \det[[x_i]_{k_j}]$ by $V$: each term becomes $\kappa_\mu s^*_\mu(u, y, c)$ by the Weyl-antisymmetric factorization. $\square$

**Consequence.** The Layer-Shape Lemma reduces to a single atomic identity — call it **StructB** — about the shifted-Schur weighted sum:

$$(\text{StructB}) \qquad S_j = \sum_{|\mu| = 2j,\ \ell(\mu) \leq 3} K_{\mu', (2^j)}\, s^*_\mu(u, y, c) \in F^j,$$

where $F^k := \{f \in \mathbb{Q}[e_1, e_2, e_3] : (u,\pi)\text{-wdeg}(f) \leq k\}$ under weights $(1, 1, 2)$ on $(e_1, e_2, e_3)$.

Uniform verification: $j \leq 8$ (Day 116, `beta-prime/code/2026-08-20-day116-lift-verify-j5-j8.py`).

### 11.2 Route V and the Characterization Lemma (Day 117)

Route V reduces StructB from a shifted-Schur statement to a purely polynomial statement in $\mathbb{Q}[s, t]$.

**Route V Reduction.** *Set $A_j(u, \sigma, \pi) := ds_j / (y - c) \in \mathbb{Q}[u, \sigma, \pi]$ with $\sigma = y + c$, $\pi = yc$. Then*
$$\deg_{u, \pi}(S_j) \leq j \iff \deg_{u, \pi}(A_j) \leq j + 2.$$

*Proof.* $ds_j = V \cdot S_j$ with $V = (u^2 - u\sigma + \pi)(y - c)$. The multiplier $u^2 - u\sigma + \pi$ has $(u, \pi)$-degree exactly $2$ with top part $u^2$ (a non-zero-divisor), so $\deg_{u,\pi}(A_j) = \deg_{u,\pi}(S_j) + 2$. $\square$

**Characterization Lemma.** *For $f \in \mathbb{Q}[e_1, e_2, e_3]$,*
$$(u, \pi)\text{-wdeg}(f) = \deg_t\, f(t + s,\ (s+1)t,\ t^2).$$

*Proof.* Substitute $(u, \sigma, \pi) = (t, s, t)$; then $e_1 = t+s$, $e_2 = (s+1)t$, $e_3 = t^2$. A monomial $e_1^{i_1} e_2^{i_2} e_3^{i_3}$ maps to $(t+s)^{i_1}(s+1)^{i_2} t^{i_2 + 2 i_3}$; its top-$t$ term is $t^{i_1 + i_2 + 2 i_3} (s+1)^{i_2}$. The polynomials $\{(s+1)^{i_2}\}$ are $\mathbb{Q}$-linearly independent, so $[t^M] f = 0 \in \mathbb{Q}[s]$ iff $c_{i_1, i_2, i_3} = 0$ for all $(i_1, i_2, i_3)$ with $i_1 + i_2 + 2 i_3 = M$. $\square$

Geometric read: the roots of $z^3 - (t+s) z^2 + t(s+1) z - t^2 = (z-t)(z^2 - sz + t)$ are $\{u, y, c\} = \{t, y, c\}$ with $y + c = s, yc = t$. So the substitution puts $u = yc$.

**(**) — the strong per-term Pieri claim (Day 117).** In the shifted-Pieri expansion
$$s^*_{(1,1)} \cdot s^*_\mu = \sum_{\lambda/\mu \text{ vert 2-strip}} s^*_\lambda \ + \sum_{|\lambda| < |\mu| + 2} c^\lambda_\mu s^*_\lambda,$$
every "lower" $\lambda$ appearing has $d_\lambda \leq d_\mu + 1$, where $d_\mu := \deg_{u,\pi}(s_\mu)$.

Verified $|\mu| \leq 6$ (Day 117, `code/day117/pieri_strong_claim.py`).

**Stirling closed form (motivation, Day 117).** The $e_3 = 0$ slice of the top-$(u, \pi)$-part of $S_j$ is
$$\bar S_j\big|_{e_3 = 0} = \prod_{i=1}^{j} (e_2 - i \cdot e_1) = \sum_{a + b = j} (-1)^a \begin{bmatrix} j+1 \\ b+1 \end{bmatrix} e_1^a e_2^b$$
(unsigned Stirling numbers of the first kind). This is a rising-factorial-in-disguise — first hint that the top-degree piece is not just structured but classical.

### 11.3 The (**) upgrade and the top-part 2-dim image (Day 118)

Molev–Sagan, "A Littlewood–Richardson rule for factorial Schur functions," arXiv:q-alg/9707028, Thm 3.1, gives the shifted-Pieri expansion explicitly with a positive combinatorial formula for the $c^\lambda_\mu$. Applied to the vertical-2-strip case, it upgrades (**) from empirical to a theorem: every lower $\lambda$ has $d_\lambda \leq d_\mu + 1$. Also gives the closed form
$$d_\mu = \mu_1 + \left\lfloor \frac{\mu_2 + \mu_3}{2} \right\rfloor.$$
Using $\mu_1 + \mu_2 + \mu_3 = 2j$: $d_\mu = j + \lfloor \mu_1 / 2 \rfloor$. So $d_{\max}(j) = j + \lfloor j/2 \rfloor$.

**Two-dim image (Day 118 discovery).** Set $\bar s^*_\mu(s) := [t^{d_\mu}] s^*_\mu(t+s, (s+1)t, t^2)$. Empirically ($|\mu| \leq 8$):
- $\deg_s \bar s^*_\mu \leq 1$;
- $\bar s^*_\mu$ depends only on the parity of $\mu_2 - \mu_3$ (equivalently the parity of $\mu_1$), NOT on $\mu_1$ itself.

Explicit form: writing $\bar s^*_\mu(s) = \alpha_\mu s + \beta_\mu$,
- odd parity ($\mu_1$ odd): $\alpha_\mu = (-1)^{(\mu_2 - \mu_3 - 1)/2} (\mu_2 - \mu_3 + 1)/2$;
- even parity: $\alpha_\mu = 0$, $\beta_\mu = (-1)^{(\mu_2 - \mu_3)/2}$.

This kills any "naive Lemma X" that would try to pin down $\bar s^*_\mu$ from $\mu_1$-dependence. Instead, StructB refines into **two Kostka-cancellation identities per weight level $d$**: (A) constant-in-$s$ part vanishes; (B) $s$-linear part vanishes. Both must hold for every $d > j$.

### 11.4 Kostka–ballot theorem at $d = d_{\max}$ (Day 119)

At $d = d_{\max}$ only $\mu$'s with $\mu_1 = j$ (odd $j$) or $\mu_1 = 2l$ (even $j = 2l$) contribute, and the shapes take a clean "spine" form.

**Lemma (Kostka = ballot).** *For $j = 2l+1$ and $\mu = (2l+1, l+1+m, l-m)$ ($0 \leq m \leq l$), the conjugate spine shape $\mu' = (3^{l-m}, 2^{2m+1}, 1^{l-m})$ satisfies*
$$K_{\mu', (2^{2l+1})} = \binom{2l+1}{l-m} - \binom{2l+1}{l-m-1}.$$
*Similarly for $j = 2l$ and $\mu = (2l, l+m, l-m)$, $\mu' = (3^{l-m}, 2^{2m}, 1^{l-m})$:*
$$K_{\mu', (2^{2l})} = \binom{2l}{l-m} - \binom{2l}{l-m-1}.$$

*Proof (odd case).* Column 1 of $\mu'$ has length $2l+1 = j$; strict column + content $(2^j)$ force column 1 = $(1, 2, \ldots, 2l+1)^T$. The remaining labels split $S_2 \sqcup S_3 = \{1, \ldots, 2l+1\}$ with $|S_2| = l+m+1$, $|S_3| = l - m$. Row-weakly-increasing on row $i$: $s_i^{(2)} \geq i$ is automatic; $s_i^{(3)} \geq s_i^{(2)}$ for all $i \leq l - m$ is equivalent to the prefix condition $r_3(k) \leq r_2(k)$ for all $k$. Bijection to lattice paths in an $(l+m+1) \times (l-m)$ box staying weakly below the diagonal — ballot number $\binom{2l+1}{l-m} - \binom{2l+1}{l-m-1}$. $\square$

**Identity A (even $j$).** For $l \geq 1$:
$$\sum_{m=0}^{l} (-1)^m \left[\binom{2l}{l-m} - \binom{2l}{l-m-1}\right] = 0.$$

*Proof.* Reindex $r = l - m$, split, telescope; the LHS becomes $2 \sum_{r=0}^{l-1}(-1)^r \binom{2l}{r} + (-1)^l \binom{2l}{l}$. By symmetry $\binom{2l}{r} = \binom{2l}{2l-r}$ and $(1-1)^{2l} = 0$, this sum vanishes. $\square$

**Identity B (odd $j$).** For $l \geq 1$:
$$\sum_{m=0}^{l} (-1)^m (m+1) \left[\binom{2l+1}{l-m} - \binom{2l+1}{l-m-1}\right] = 0.$$

*Proof.* Reindexing and combining as in Day 119 §3 reduces the LHS to $T = \sum_{r=0}^{l}(-1)^r (2l+1 - 2r) \binom{2l+1}{r}$. Apply the identity $(n - 2r)\binom{n}{r} = n[\binom{n-1}{r} - \binom{n-1}{r-1}]$ with $n = 2l+1$:
$$T = (2l+1) \sum_{r=0}^{l} (-1)^r \left[\binom{2l}{r} - \binom{2l}{r-1}\right] = 0$$
by Identity A applied to $n = 2l$. So Identity B is Identity A times $(2l+1)$ after reindexing. $\square$

Consequence: the top-$t$ part at $d = d_{\max}$ vanishes in the $s^0$-part for even $j$ (Identity A gives it directly) and in the $s^1$-part for odd $j$ (Identity B). The $s^0$-part for odd $j$ does NOT vanish from Identity A alone — Identity A applied to spine shapes gives $\pm 1$, forcing the $\beta$-contribution to precisely cancel it. That is exactly what (C1) below asserts.

### 11.5 (C2) via the 4-term Weyl reduction (Day 120)

For odd $j = 2l+1$, define the even-parity spine partitions $\mu = (2l, l+1+r, l+1-r)$ for $r = 0, \ldots, l-1$ (note $\mu_1 = 2l < j$; these are the even-$\mu_1$ contributors at $d = d_{\max}$).

**Lemma (4-term Weyl formula for $K_{\text{even}}$).**
$$K_{\mu', (2^{2l+1})} = (2l+1)\left[\binom{2l}{l-r} - \binom{2l}{l-r-1}\right] \ - \left[\binom{2l+1}{l-r+1} - \binom{2l+1}{l-r-1}\right].$$

*Proof.* Compute $\langle s_\mu, e_2^{2l+1}\rangle$ via the Weyl bracket: expand $e_2^{2l+1}$ as a multinomial in $(x_1 x_2, x_1 x_3, x_2 x_3)$, pair with the Vandermonde, match exponents against $\rho = (2l+2, l+2+r, l+1-r)$. Two of the six $\tau \in S_3$ terms fail ($c = -1$); the remaining four collapse (using $\binom{2l+1}{k, 2l-k, 1} = (2l+1)\binom{2l}{k}$ and $\binom{2l+1}{k, 2l+1-k, 0} = \binom{2l+1}{k}$) to the stated 4-term expression. $\square$

**Identity (C2).** *For $l \geq 1$,*
$$A_{\text{even}}(l) := \sum_{r=0}^{l-1} (-1)^r K_{\mu', (2^{2l+1})} = (-1)^{l+1}.$$

*Proof.* By the 4-term formula,
$$A_{\text{even}}(l) = (2l+1) \sum_{r=0}^{l-1}(-1)^r \left[\binom{2l}{l-r} - \binom{2l}{l-r-1}\right] \ + \sum_{r=0}^{l-1}(-1)^r \left[-\binom{2l+1}{l-r+1} + \binom{2l+1}{l-r-1}\right].$$

First sum: Identity A extends to $\sum_{r=0}^{l}(-1)^r \text{ballot}(2l, l-r) = 0$; the $r = l$ term is $(-1)^l \cdot \text{ballot}(2l, 0) = (-1)^l$. So $\sum_{r=0}^{l-1} = -(-1)^l = (-1)^{l+1}$.

Second sum: substitute $k = l - r$, reindex both binomials, telescope through the middle. What survives is $-\binom{2l+1}{0} + \binom{2l+1}{1} + (-1)^l[\binom{2l+1}{l} - \binom{2l+1}{l+1}] = -1 + (2l+1) + 0 = 2l$, giving $(-1)^l \cdot 2l$ overall.

Combine: $A_{\text{even}}(l) = (2l+1)(-1)^{l+1} + 2l(-1)^l = (-1)^{l+1}[(2l+1) - 2l] = (-1)^{l+1}$. $\square$

Verified $l = 1, \ldots, 19$ (`code/day120/c2_proof_final.py`).

### 11.6 The (A,B) reduction: (C1) is a theorem (Day 121)

The final ingredient. This is the piece that closes the odd-$j$ $s^0$-part.

**(C1) statement.** *For $j = 2l+1$, $\mu^{(m)} := (2l+1, l+1+m, l-m)$, $m \in \{0, 1, \ldots, l\}$, and $\bar s^*_\mu(s) := [t^{d_\mu}]\,s^*_\mu(u, y, c)\big|_{u=t,\ y+c=s,\ yc=t}$:*
$$\bar s^*_{\mu^{(m)}}(j) = (-1)^m \delta_{m, l}.$$

Equivalently, $\beta_m + j\alpha_m = (-1)^m \delta_{m, l}$, which forces $\beta_m = (-1)^{m+1}[(m+1)(2l+1) - \delta_{m, l}]$.

#### Setup: reduce $[y]_a$ modulo the minimal polynomial of $y$

Under $y + c = j$, $yc = t$, the pair $(y, c)$ satisfies $y^2 - jy + t = 0$ (and $c = j - y$). For $a \geq 0$, reduce the falling factorial:
$$[y]_a \equiv A_a(t)\, y + B_a(t) \pmod{y^2 - jy + t}.$$
Using $[y]_{a+1} = [y]_a (y - a)$ and $y^2 = jy - t$:
$$A_{a+1} = (j - a) A_a + B_a, \qquad B_{a+1} = -t A_a - a B_a, \qquad A_0 = 0,\ B_0 = 1.$$
Induction: $\deg_t A_a = \lfloor (a-1)/2 \rfloor$, $\deg_t B_a = \lfloor a/2 \rfloor$. Since $c = j - y$:
$$[c]_a \equiv A_a c + B_a = -A_a\, y + (A_a j + B_a).$$

#### Weyl formula and the discrete Wronskian

$s^*_\mu(u, y, c) = \det[[x_i]_{k_j}] / V$ with $k = (\mu_1 + 2, \mu_2 + 1, \mu_3)$. For our family $k = (2l+3,\ l+2+m,\ l-m)$. Expand along row 1:
$$\det = [u]_{k_1} M_{k_2, k_3} - [u]_{k_2} M_{k_1, k_3} + [u]_{k_3} M_{k_1, k_2}, \qquad M_{a, b} := [y]_a [c]_b - [y]_b [c]_a.$$

Compute $M_{a, b}$ using the reductions:
$$M_{a, b} = (A_a y + B_a)(-A_b y + A_b j + B_b) - (A_b y + B_b)(-A_a y + A_a j + B_a).$$
The $y^2$ terms cancel; expand and collect:
$$M_{a, b} = (A_a B_b - A_b B_a)(2y - j) = W_{a, b}(t) \cdot (y - c),$$
where $W_{a, b}(t) := A_a B_b - A_b B_a$ is the discrete Wronskian of the $(A, B)$ pair.

Denominator under $u = t$, $y + c = j$, $yc = t$: $(u - y)(u - c) = t^2 - tj + t = t(t - 2l)$; hence
$$F_\mu(t) := s^*_\mu(u, y, c)\big|_{u = t,\ y + c = j,\ yc = t} = \frac{N_\mu(t)}{t(t - 2l)},$$
$$N_\mu(t) = [t]_{k_1} W_{k_2, k_3} - [t]_{k_2} W_{k_1, k_3} + [t]_{k_3} W_{k_1, k_2}.$$

Target: $[t^{d_\mu}] F_\mu(t) = [t^{d_\mu + 2}] N_\mu(t) = (-1)^m \delta_{m, l}$.

#### Lemma A: $W_{k_2, k_3} = 0$

**Sub-lemma.** *For $j = 2m + 1$, $A_{2m+2}(2m+1, t) \equiv 0$.*

*Proof.* At $j = 2m+1$, the reindex $i \mapsto 2m+1 - i$ gives
$$[c]_{2m+2} = \prod_{i=0}^{2m+1}(c - i) = \prod_{i=0}^{2m+1}((2m+1 - i) - y) = \prod_{i'=0}^{2m+1}(i' - y) = [y]_{2m+2}$$
(sign $(-1)^{2m+2} = +1$). So $A_{2m+2}\, y + B_{2m+2} = -A_{2m+2}\, y + (A_{2m+2}\,j + B_{2m+2})$; comparing $y$-coefficients: $A_{2m+2} = -A_{2m+2}$, i.e., $A_{2m+2} = 0$. $\square$

This is a Weyl-reflection vanishing — the "$y$-part" of the reduction vanishes because $[y]_{2m+2}$ and $[c]_{2m+2}$ are the same polynomial in the base $\{1, y\}$.

**Closed form** (proof of Lemma A). For $a > b$, factor:
$$W_{a, b}(t) = \prod_{i = 0}^{b - 1}(t - r_i) \cdot \tilde A_{a - b}(j - 2b,\ t - bj + b^2),$$
where $r_i = i(j - i)$ and $\tilde A_n(j', t')$ is the "$A_n$"-polynomial for the shifted system $z^2 - j' z + t' = 0$. This follows from $A_a = ([y_+]_a - [y_-]_a)/(y_+ - y_-)$ with $y_\pm = (j \pm \sqrt{j^2 - 4t})/2$, and the shift $y = z + b$.

Apply to $(a, b) = (k_2, k_3) = (l + 2 + m,\ l - m)$: $a - b = 2m + 2$, $j - 2b = 2l + 1 - 2(l - m) = 2m + 1$. So
$$W_{k_2, k_3} = \prod_{i = 0}^{l - m - 1}(t - r_i) \cdot \tilde A_{2m + 2}(2m + 1, t')= 0$$
by the sub-lemma. $\square$

Hence
$$N_\mu(t) = [t]_{k_3}\, W_{k_1, k_2} - [t]_{k_2}\, W_{k_1, k_3}.$$

#### Lemma B: degree bound for $m < l$

$\deg_t W_{a, b} = b + \lfloor (a - b - 1)/2 \rfloor$ (from the $\prod (t - r_i) \cdot \tilde A$ formula).

Term 1: $\deg [t]_{k_2} W_{k_1, k_3} = k_2 + k_3 + \lfloor (k_1 - k_3 - 1)/2 \rfloor = (l + 2 + m) + (l - m) + \lfloor (l + m + 2)/2 \rfloor = 2l + 2 + \lfloor (l + m + 2)/2 \rfloor$. For $m < l$: $l + m + 2 < 2l + 2$, so $\lfloor (l+m+2)/2\rfloor \leq l$; degree $\leq 3l + 2$.

Term 2: $\deg [t]_{k_3} W_{k_1, k_2} = k_3 + k_2 + \lfloor (k_1 - k_2 - 1)/2 \rfloor = 2l + 2 + \lfloor (l - m)/2 \rfloor \leq 2l + 2 + l/2 < 3l + 3$.

Both terms have $\deg_t \leq 3l + 2 < d_\mu + 2 = 3l + 3$. Hence $[t^{d_\mu + 2}] N_\mu = 0$, so $\bar s^*_{\mu^{(m)}}(j) = 0$ for $m < l$. $\square$

#### Lemma C: leading coefficient for $m = l$

At $m = l$: $k = (2l + 3,\ 2l + 2,\ 0)$, $[t]_{k_3} = [t]_0 = 1$. By the sub-lemma, $A_{2l+2} = 0$ (since $j = 2l+1 = 2m+1$ with $m = l$).

Then:
- $A_{2l+3} = (j - (2l + 2)) A_{2l+2} + B_{2l+2} = -1 \cdot 0 + B_{2l+2} = B_{2l+2}$.
- $W_{2l+3, 0} = A_{2l+3} B_0 - A_0 B_{2l+3} = A_{2l+3} = B_{2l+2}$.
- $W_{2l+3, 2l+2} = A_{2l+3} B_{2l+2} - A_{2l+2} B_{2l+3} = B_{2l+2}^2$.

So $N_{\mu^{(l)}}(t) = B_{2l+2}^2 - [t]_{2l+2} \cdot B_{2l+2} = B_{2l+2}(B_{2l+2} - [t]_{2l+2})$.

Closed form for $B_{2l+2}$: since $[y]_{2l+2} = [c]_{2l+2}$ reduces to a $y$-independent constant, $[y]_{2l+2} \equiv B_{2l+2}(t)$. Then
$$B_{2l+2}(t)^2 = [y]_{2l+2} [c]_{2l+2} = \prod_{i=0}^{2l+1}(y - i)(c - i) = \prod_{i=0}^{2l+1}(t - r_i) = \prod_{k=0}^{l}(t - r_k)^2$$
(pairing $i \leftrightarrow 2l+1-i$ gives $r_i = r_{2l+1-i}$). So $B_{2l+2}(t) = \varepsilon \prod_{k=0}^{l}(t - r_k)$ with $\varepsilon \in \{\pm 1\}$. From the $B$-recursion, $B_{2n}$ has leading $t^n$-coefficient $(-1)^n$ (induction on $B_{k+1} = -tA_k - kB_k$). At $n = l + 1$: $\varepsilon = (-1)^{l+1}$.

Leading of $N_{\mu^{(l)}}$: $\deg [t]_{2l+2} = 2l + 2 > l + 1 = \deg B_{2l+2}$, so leading of $B_{2l+2} - [t]_{2l+2}$ is $-t^{2l+2}$ (coef $-1$). Thus
$$[t^{3l + 3}] N_{\mu^{(l)}} = [t^{l+1}] B_{2l+2} \cdot [t^{2l+2}](B_{2l+2} - [t]_{2l+2}) = (-1)^{l+1} \cdot (-1) = (-1)^l. \qquad \square$$

Combining Lemmas A, B, C: $\bar s^*_{\mu^{(m)}}(j) = (-1)^m \delta_{m, l}$. This is (C1). $\blacksquare$

**Consequence.** The odd-$j$ $s^0$-part of top-$t$ vanishing at $d = d_{\max}$ is a theorem:
$$\sum_m \beta_m K^{\text{odd}}_m = -(2l+1) \underbrace{\sum_m (-1)^m (m+1) K^{\text{odd}}_m}_{= 0 \text{ by Identity B}} + (-1)^l \underbrace{K^{\text{odd}}_l}_{= 1} = (-1)^l,$$
and $C_0(l) = A_{\text{even}}(l) + \sum_m \beta_m K^{\text{odd}}_m = (-1)^{l+1} + (-1)^l = 0$ by (C2). Layer-Shape Lemma at $d = d_{\max}$ (both parities of $j$) is a theorem.

### 11.7 The general-$d$ frontier (Day 120 structural discovery)

For $d < d_{\max}$ the story changes. Subleading-$t$ terms of $s^*_\mu$'s with $d_\mu > d$ contribute to $[t^d] S_j$, coupling the parity classes. The naive parity split (identities (A) constant + (B) $s$-linear per parity) **breaks**.

**Empirical example.** $(j, d) = (7, 8)$:
- $A_{\text{sum}}(7, 8) = 15 s^4 - 310 s^3 + 2601 s^2 - 10203 s + 13631$ (even-parity total).
- $B_{\text{sum}}(7, 8) = -A_{\text{sum}}(7, 8)$ (odd-parity total, exact negative).

Neither is zero. Vanishing of $[t^d] S_j$ requires the joint identity $A_{\text{sum}} + B_{\text{sum}} = 0$, a cancellation across parities. Fundamentally different mechanism from the top-$d$ regime.

**Route candidates for $d < d_{\max}$:**

1. **Extend the (A, B) reduction to arbitrary 3-part $\mu$** (not just spine). The Weyl-formula collapse + discrete Wronskian machinery of §11.6 is not tied to the spine hypothesis; the sub-lemma $A_{2m+2}(2m+1, t) \equiv 0$ is spine-specific but analogous reflection identities may apply.

2. **Allen–Celano–Mason, arXiv:2511.18156, Algorithm 5.1.** The $\theta$-machinery (pair-of-rows swap at a bad cell) restricted to rows 2, 3 may give a signed-tableaux involution that pairs contributions across parities.

3. **External inputs.** Gunna–Wheeler–Zinn-Justin, arXiv:2504.19205 (spin-HL structure constants) and Jing–Liu–Molev, arXiv:2408.09855 (quantum Capelli) both give shifted/factorial-Schur identities with cross-degree cancellation flavor that may apply.

## 12. The E-basis reformulation and Lee's open Pieri problem (Days 122–123)

Sections 11.1–11.8 close the Layer-Shape Lemma at $d = d_{\max}$ and identify the joint identity $A_{\text{sum}} + B_{\text{sum}} = 0$ as the general-$d$ frontier. Day 123 kills that frontier as a separate target: the entire Layer-Shape Lemma, at ALL $d$ simultaneously, reformulates as a single monomial weight bound in $\mathbb{Q}[e_1, e_2, e_3]$. The reformulation is not internal β' bookkeeping — it is EXACTLY the missing filtration bound in the shifted-$t$-Schur Pieri problem opened by Lee–Shimozono (arXiv:2606.22058, June 2026). This section states the reformulation, records the syzygy that makes it precise, isolates the two remaining lemmas, identifies the problem with Lee's open Pieri rule, and sketches the Path 1+2 chain to the queer $\mathfrak{q}_N$.

Throughout, $E_j \in \text{Sym}^*_{\leq 3} = \mathbb{Q}[e_1, e_2, e_3]$ is the shifted-symmetric lift of $S_j$ defined in §12.1; $\Sigma$ is the specialization of §12.1; $\Omega$ is the syzygy of §12.2; $\Psi$ is the linear lift $s_\mu \mapsto s^*_\mu$; $\Pi^*$ is the shifted-Pieri operator of §12.3.

### 12.1 The Main Conjecture

**Definition (E-basis lift).** For each $j \geq 0$, define
$$E_j := \sum_{|\mu| = 2j,\ \ell(\mu) \leq 3} K_{\mu', (2^j)}\, s^*_\mu(u_1, u_2, u_3) \in \mathbb{Q}[e_1, e_2, e_3],$$
where $e_k = e_k(u_1, u_2, u_3)$ are the elementary symmetrics.

**Specialization $\Sigma$.** Define $\Sigma: \mathbb{Q}[e_1, e_2, e_3] \to \mathbb{Q}[j, t]$ by
$$\Sigma:\quad e_1 \mapsto t + j,\qquad e_2 \mapsto t(j + 1),\qquad e_3 \mapsto t^2.$$
The $(1,1,2)$-weight of a monomial is
$$w(e_1^{a_1} e_2^{a_2} e_3^{a_3}) := a_1 + a_2 + 2a_3.$$

Note $\deg_t \Sigma(e_1) = 1$, $\deg_t \Sigma(e_2) = 1$, $\deg_t \Sigma(e_3) = 2$; the weight $w$ is exactly $\deg_t \circ \Sigma$ on monomials. This is the Characterization Lemma of §11.2, restated.

**Lemma 12.1 (Lift Identity).** *$S_j = \Sigma(E_j)$.*

*Proof.* The specialization $u_1 = t$, $u_2 + u_3 = j$, $u_2 u_3 = t$ sends
$e_1(u) \mapsto t + j$, $e_2(u) \mapsto u_1(u_2 + u_3) + u_2 u_3 = tj + t = t(j+1)$, $e_3(u) \mapsto u_1 u_2 u_3 = t \cdot t = t^2$. So $\Sigma$ acts as claimed. Under this specialization $s^*_\mu(u_1, u_2, u_3) \mapsto F_\mu(j, t)$ (the (A,B)-reduction of §11.6). Summing against $K_{\mu', (2^j)}$ and invoking the Lift Theorem (§11.1) gives $\Sigma(E_j) = S_j$. $\square$

**Main Conjecture (Day 123).** *Every monomial appearing in the $e$-basis expansion of $E_j$ has $(1,1,2)$-weight $\leq j$:*
$$E_j \in F^j := \{f \in \mathbb{Q}[e_1, e_2, e_3] : w(f) \leq j\}.$$

Verified computationally for $j = 1, \ldots, 12$ (`beta-prime/code/day123/e_basis_check.py`). In every case the maximum weight is attained EXACTLY by the $e_2^j$ monomial (coefficient $1$); no monomial exceeds weight $j$.

**Consequence.** $\deg_t S_j = \deg_t \Sigma(E_j) \leq w(E_j) \leq j$. Hence the Layer-Shape Lemma at every $d$ follows from the Main Conjecture. The general-$d$ frontier of §11.7 is subsumed: the joint identity $A_{\text{sum}} + B_{\text{sum}} = 0$ for $j < d < d_{\max}$ is the coordinate shadow of a single algebraic bound.

**Small cases.**
- $E_0 = 1$ (weight 0).
- $E_1 = e_2 - e_1 + 1$ (weights $1, 1, 0$).
- $E_2 = e_2^2 - 3 e_1 e_2 + 2 e_1^2 - 3 e_3 + 5 e_2 - 6 e_1 + 4$ (max weight $2$, from $e_2^2$).
- $E_3$ contains $e_2^3, e_1 e_2^2, e_1 e_3, e_2 e_3$ at weight $3$; $e_3^2$ (weight $4$) does NOT appear.
- $E_4$: $e_3^2$ appears with coefficient $27$; weight $4 = j$, consistent.

The pattern — top weight $= j$, attained by $e_2^j$, no overflow — is uniform through $j = 12$.

### 12.2 The syzygy $\Omega$ and the canonical form

The specialization $\Sigma$ is not injective. From $\Sigma(e_1) = t + j$ and $\Sigma(e_2 + e_3) = t(j+1) + t^2 = t(t + j + 1) = t(\Sigma(e_1) + 1)$ we read off the relation $t \cdot \Sigma(e_1 + 1) = \Sigma(e_2 + e_3)$. Squaring both sides and using $t^2 = \Sigma(e_3)$ gives $\Sigma(e_3(e_1 + 1)^2) = \Sigma((e_2 + e_3)^2)$, i.e.,
$$\Omega := e_3(e_1 + 1)^2 - (e_2 + e_3)^2 \in \ker \Sigma.$$

**Lemma 12.2 ($\ker \Sigma$ is principal).** *$\ker \Sigma = (\Omega)$.*

*Proof.* The image $\Sigma(\mathbb{Q}[e_1, e_2, e_3])$ contains $\Sigma(e_1) - 1 = t + j - 1$ and $\Sigma(e_3) = t^2$; together with $\Sigma(e_2)$ these generate $\mathbb{Q}[j, t]$ as a subring of itself. Hence $\Sigma$ is surjective, and the source has Krull dimension $3$, target dimension $2$; so $\ker \Sigma$ has height $1$. It is contained in the principal ideal $(\Omega)$ (which is nontrivial in $\ker$), and $\Omega$ is irreducible in $\mathbb{Q}[e_1, e_2, e_3]$ (its total degree is $3$ and it has no factor of the form $\alpha e_i + \beta$: checking specializations $e_i \mapsto 0$ produces nonzero polynomials in the other two variables). Since $\mathbb{Q}[e_1, e_2, e_3]$ is a UFD, height-$1$ primes are principal; $\ker \Sigma = (\Omega)$. $\square$

**Canonical form.** From $\Omega \equiv 0$ we get the reduction rule
$$e_3^2 \equiv (e_1^2 + 2 e_1 - 2 e_2 + 1) \, e_3 - e_2^2 \pmod{\Omega}.$$
Iterating, every $f \in \mathbb{Q}[e_1, e_2, e_3]$ reduces to a unique representative of the form
$$f \equiv f_0(e_1, e_2) + f_1(e_1, e_2) \cdot e_3 \pmod{\Omega}.$$

**Refined Main Conjecture (canonical form).** *In the canonical decomposition $E_j \equiv f_j(e_1, e_2) + g_j(e_1, e_2) \cdot e_3 \pmod{\Omega}$,*
$$\deg f_j = j\qquad \text{and}\qquad \deg g_j = j - 2$$
*(both bounds sharp; both attained for every $j \geq 2$).*

Verified $j = 1, \ldots, 12$ (`beta-prime/code/day123/omega_reduction.py`). The sharp reduction is a stronger claim than the $(1,1,2)$-weight bound: it fixes the precise shape of $E_j$ modulo the specialization ideal.

**Remark (why the reduction respects the filtration).** Under $\Omega$, the leading part of $e_3^2$ is $e_1^2 e_3$, which has weight $4 = w(e_3^2)$. So reduction mod $\Omega$ is weight-preserving on leading terms. The $(1,1,2)$-weight of $E_j$ and of its canonical form agree.

### 12.3 The shifted-Pieri operator $\Pi^*$

Let $\Psi: \mathbb{Q}[e_1, e_2, e_3] \to \mathbb{Q}[e_1, e_2, e_3]$ be the linear map $\Psi(s_\mu) = s^*_\mu$ (extended from the Schur basis of the ordinary side to the shifted-Schur basis; both are $\mathbb{Q}$-bases of $\mathbb{Q}[e_1, e_2, e_3]$ so $\Psi$ is well-defined and invertible). Let $\Pi(f) := e_2 \cdot f$. Define
$$\Pi^* := \Psi \circ \Pi \circ \Psi^{-1}.$$
Then $\Pi^*(s^*_\nu) = \Psi(e_2 \cdot s_\nu)$; expanding $e_2 \cdot s_\nu = s_{(1,1)} \cdot s_\nu = \sum_{\lambda \in \nu \boxplus (1,1)} s_\lambda$ (vertical 2-strip Pieri) and applying $\Psi$ gives the explicit formula
$$\Pi^*(s^*_\nu) = \sum_{\substack{\lambda \in \nu \boxplus (1,1) \\ \ell(\lambda) \leq 3}} s^*_\lambda.$$

Since $e_2^j = \Pi(e_2^{j-1})$ and $E_j = \Psi(e_2^j)$, iterating gives $E_j = (\Pi^*)^j(1)$. So the Main Conjecture is equivalent to
$$(\Pi^*)^j(1) \in F^j\qquad \text{for every }j \geq 0.$$

**Lemma 1 (Individual Pieri Cancellation).** *For every partition $\nu$ with $\ell(\nu) \leq 3$,*
$$w(\Pi^*(s^*_\nu)) \leq d_\nu + 1,$$
*where $d_\nu = \nu_1 + \lfloor (\nu_2 + \nu_3)/2 \rfloor$ is the O–O weight of §11.3.*

**Status:** empirical for tested $\nu$ (through $|\nu| \leq 6$). The mechanism is exact leading-term cancellation among the shifted-Schurs indexed by $\nu \boxplus (1,1)$.

**Test case.** $\nu = (2, 1, 0)$: $d_\nu = 2 + 0 = 2$. Then
$$\Pi^*(s^*_{(2,1,0)}) = s^*_{(3,2,0)} + s^*_{(3,1,1)} + s^*_{(2,2,1)}.$$
Individually, $s^*_{(3,2,0)}$ and $s^*_{(3,1,1)}$ have $d = 4 = d_\nu + 2$ (each too large for the target bound). Their leading weight-$4$ symbols in the $e$-basis are $-e_1^2 e_3$ and $+e_1^2 e_3$: they cancel EXACTLY. The residual $s^*_{(2,2,1)}$ has $d = 3 = d_\nu + 1$. Hence $w(\Pi^*(s^*_{(2,1,0)})) \leq 3$, as claimed. See `beta-prime/code/day123/individual_weight.py`.

**Lemma 2 (Filtration Preservation for $\Psi$).** *For every $f \in \mathbb{Q}[e_1, e_2, e_3]$ (viewed as $\text{Sym}_{\leq 3}$ via the Schur basis of the source and the shifted-Schur basis of the target),*
$$w(\Psi(f)) \leq w(f).$$

**Status: CONJECTURAL.** This is the main remaining gap. Equivalently: $\Psi$ preserves the $(1,1,2)$-filtration $F^\bullet$.

**Why Lemma 1 does not immediately give Lemma 2.** A general $f$ of weight $w$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-combination $\sum c_\nu s_\nu$ where individual $d_\nu$ may EXCEED $w$: the constraint is that the top-weight parts of the individual $s_\nu$'s cancel in the sum. Lemma 1 controls each $\Pi^*(s^*_\nu)$ individually — it does not control cancellations across a $c_\nu$-combination. The Cauchy–Binet decomposition of $s^*_\mu$ into ordinary Schurs (implicit in the (A,B) reduction of §11.6) confirms: individual terms have wrong degree; cancellations are required.

**Reduction of the Main Conjecture.** Given Lemma 2, the Main Conjecture follows by induction on $j$: $E_0 = 1 \in F^0$; assuming $E_{j-1} \in F^{j-1}$, apply $\Psi^{-1}$ to get $e_2^{j-1} \in F^{j-1}$ (trivial: $e_2^{j-1}$ has weight $j-1$), multiply by $e_2$ (weight $+1$) to get $e_2^j \in F^j$, then apply $\Psi$ using Lemma 2 to get $E_j = \Psi(e_2^j) \in F^j$. $\square$

So: **Lemma 2 IS the Main Conjecture**, up to the trivial $\Pi = e_2 \cdot$ observation. The remaining task is a single filtration statement about $\Psi$ on the shifted symmetric ring in 3 variables.

## §13. The operator formula $\Psi = T(\cdot V)/V$ and the reduction to one power of $e_2$ (Days 124–125)

Days 124–125 changed the character of the Main Conjecture. Instead of a monomial-by-monomial weight bound on $\Psi(e_1^{a_1} e_2^{a_2} e_3^{a_3})$, we now have a closed operator formula for $\Psi$ in terms of an elementary multiplicative gadget $T$, and two exact shift lemmas which peel $e_1$ and $e_3$ off any monomial in one stroke. The Main Conjecture reduces to a single one-parameter statement:
$$w(\Psi(e_2^b)) \leq b \qquad \text{for all } b \geq 0.$$
Verified for $b \leq 10$. The remaining task is algebraic, not combinatorial.

### 13.1 The operator formula (Day 125, Lemma 0)

Let $V := \prod_{i < j}(u_i - u_j)$ be the Vandermonde in $u_1, u_2, u_3$. Define the multiplicative componentwise operator $T: \mathbb{Q}[u_1, u_2, u_3] \to \mathbb{Q}[u_1, u_2, u_3]$ on monomials by
$$T(u_1^{k_1} u_2^{k_2} u_3^{k_3}) := [u_1]_{k_1}\, [u_2]_{k_2}\, [u_3]_{k_3},$$
extended linearly. Here $[x]_k := x(x-1)\cdots(x - k + 1)$ is the falling factorial. Recall $\Psi: s_\mu \mapsto s^*_\mu$, where $s^*_\mu$ is the factorial Schur function (Weyl-determinant form with falling factorials).

**Lemma 0 (Operator formula for $\Psi$).** *For every symmetric $f \in \mathbb{Q}[u_1, u_2, u_3]^{S_3}$,*
$$\boxed{\;\Psi(f) \;=\; \frac{T(f \cdot V)}{V}.\;}$$

*Proof.* $T$ acts componentwise, hence is $S_3$-equivariant. $V$ is antisymmetric, $f$ is symmetric, so $f V$ is antisymmetric. Applying an $S_3$-equivariant operator to an antisymmetric polynomial yields an antisymmetric polynomial; hence $T(f V)$ is antisymmetric and divisible by $V$ in $\mathbb{Q}[u_1, u_2, u_3]$.

On the Schur basis $f = s_\mu$, the Weyl formula gives $s_\mu \cdot V = \det[u_i^{k_j}]$ with $k = (\mu_1 + 2, \mu_2 + 1, \mu_3)$. Componentwise action of $T$ commutes with the determinant along rows:
$$T(s_\mu \cdot V) = T\!\det[u_i^{k_j}] = \det[T(u_i^{k_j})] = \det[[u_i]_{k_j}] = s^*_\mu \cdot V.$$
The last equality is the Weyl determinant formula for $s^*_\mu$. Dividing by $V$ gives $\Psi(s_\mu) = s^*_\mu$, and linearity extends to all symmetric $f$. $\square$

Lemma 0 is the workhorse. Every subsequent shift lemma is a direct manipulation of $T$ on monomials followed by division by $V$.

### 13.2 $\Psi$ preserves the $(1, 1, 2)$-weight — monomial verification (Day 124)

Recall the $(1, 1, 2)$-weight on $\mathbb{Q}[e_1, e_2, e_3]$: $w(e_1) = 1$, $w(e_2) = 1$, $w(e_3) = 2$, $w(fg) \leq w(f) + w(g)$, extended by taking the max over monomials in an $e$-expansion. Filtration Preservation for $\Psi$ (Lemma 2 of §11) says
$$w(\Psi(m)) \leq w(m) \qquad \text{for every } e\text{-monomial } m.$$

**Empirical result (Day 124).** For every $e$-monomial $m = e_1^{a_1} e_2^{a_2} e_3^{a_3}$ of $u$-degree $a_1 + 2 a_2 + 3 a_3 \leq 14$, direct expansion of $\Psi(m)$ in the $e$-basis and inspection of every summand yields $w(\Psi(m)) = w(m)$ *exactly* (not just $\leq$). Count: 147 monomials, 147 confirmations. In addition, $\Psi^{-1}$ preserves the $(1, 1, 2)$-filtration on the range $N \leq 10$ tested.

**Consequence for the Layer-Shape Lemma.** With Lemma 2 in hand, the Day 123 identity $\Pi^* = \Psi \circ (e_2 \cdot) \circ \Psi^{-1}$ collapses to a one-liner: $\Pi^*$ raises weight by at most $w(e_2) = 1$, hence $w(E_j) \leq j$. Everything now hinges on an *algebraic* proof of the weight bound.

### 13.3 Two closed-form shift lemmas (Day 125)

The following two lemmas exhaust the "easy" directions in $(e_1, e_2, e_3)$-space: $e_1$ and $e_3$ can be peeled off any monomial exactly, with no error term.

**Lemma A (e_3-shift).** *For every symmetric $f \in \mathbb{Q}[u_1, u_2, u_3]^{S_3}$ and every $c \geq 0$,*
$$\Psi(f \cdot e_3^c) \;=\; \Psi(f)(u - c) \cdot \Psi(e_3^c),$$
*where $\Psi(f)(u - c)$ denotes the uniform shift $u_i \mapsto u_i - c$.*

*Proof.* $e_3^c = (u_1 u_2 u_3)^c$ is a single monomial. For any monomial $u^\beta = u_1^{\beta_1} u_2^{\beta_2} u_3^{\beta_3}$,
$$T(u^\beta \cdot e_3^c) = \prod_i [u_i]_{c + \beta_i} = \prod_i [u_i]_c \cdot \prod_i [u_i - c]_{\beta_i} = T(e_3^c) \cdot T(u^\beta)\big|_{u \to u - c},$$
using $[x]_{c + k} = [x]_c \cdot [x - c]_k$. Linearity extends to $f V$:
$$T(f V \cdot e_3^c) = T(e_3^c) \cdot T(f V)\big|_{u \to u - c}.$$
Divide by $V$. On the right, $V(u - c) = V(u)$ (uniform shift preserves the Vandermonde), so $T(f V)\big|_{u \to u - c} / V = T(f V)/V \big|_{u \to u - c} = \Psi(f)(u - c)$. Thus $\Psi(f \cdot e_3^c) = \Psi(e_3^c) \cdot \Psi(f)(u - c)$. $\square$

**Corollary ($\Psi$ on $e_3^c$).** Taking $f = 1$: $\Psi(e_3^c) = T(e_3^c)/V \cdot V/V^{?} = T(e_3^c)$ directly (since $V \cdot 1 = V$ and $T$ acts componentwise); explicitly,
$$\Psi(e_3^c) = \prod_i [u_i]_c.$$
Its $(1, 1, 2)$-weight is $2c$ (leading $e$-expansion: $e_3^c$ plus lower terms, each of weight $\leq 2c$).

**Lemma B ($e_1$-shift).** *For every symmetric $g \in \mathbb{Q}[u_1, u_2, u_3]^{S_3}$ of $u$-degree $d$ and every $a \geq 0$,*
$$\Psi(e_1^a \cdot g) \;=\; [e_1 - d - 3]_a \cdot \Psi(g).$$

*Proof.* Apply the Day 124 $T$-shift theorem (`beta-prime/notes/2026-08-22-day124-t-shift-extended.md`): for any homogeneous $h$ of $u$-degree $D$,
$$T(e_1^a \cdot h) = [e_1 - D]_a \cdot T(h).$$
Take $h = g V$. Then $D = d + 3$ (Vandermonde adds degree 3). Divide both sides by $V$:
$$\Psi(e_1^a g) = T(e_1^a g V)/V = [e_1 - (d+3)]_a \cdot T(g V)/V = [e_1 - d - 3]_a \cdot \Psi(g). \qquad \square$$

Both shifts are exact. Note the asymmetry: $e_3$ shifts the *argument* $u$; $e_1$ shifts the *coefficient ring* by a scalar depending on $\deg g$.

### 13.4 Full factorization theorem and reduction to one power of $e_2$

**Theorem (Full factorization).** *For every triple $(a_1, a_2, a_3)$ of non-negative integers,*
$$\boxed{\;\Psi(e_1^{a_1} e_2^{a_2} e_3^{a_3}) \;=\; [e_1 - 2 a_2 - 3 a_3 - 3]_{a_1} \cdot \Psi(e_2^{a_2})(u - a_3) \cdot \Psi(e_3^{a_3}).\;}$$

*Proof.* Chain Lemmas A and B. Peel $e_3^{a_3}$ first (Lemma A with $f = e_1^{a_1} e_2^{a_2}$):
$$\Psi(e_1^{a_1} e_2^{a_2} e_3^{a_3}) = \Psi(e_1^{a_1} e_2^{a_2})(u - a_3) \cdot \Psi(e_3^{a_3}).$$
Inside, $e_1^{a_1} e_2^{a_2}$ has the form $e_1^a \cdot g$ with $g = e_2^{a_2}$ of $u$-degree $2 a_2$. Apply Lemma B:
$$\Psi(e_1^{a_1} e_2^{a_2}) = [e_1 - 2 a_2 - 3]_{a_1} \cdot \Psi(e_2^{a_2}).$$
Now shift the whole expression by $u \mapsto u - a_3$. The falling factorial $[e_1 - 2 a_2 - 3]_{a_1}$ in the variable $e_1 = u_1 + u_2 + u_3$ becomes $[e_1 - 3 a_3 - 2 a_2 - 3]_{a_1}$; $\Psi(e_2^{a_2})$ becomes $\Psi(e_2^{a_2})(u - a_3)$. Multiplying back in $\Psi(e_3^{a_3}) = \prod_i [u_i]_{a_3}$ gives the stated formula. $\square$

**Corollary (Reduction).** *The full weight bound
$$w(\Psi(e_1^{a_1} e_2^{a_2} e_3^{a_3})) \;\leq\; a_1 + a_2 + 2 a_3$$
is equivalent to the one-parameter statement*
$$w(\Psi(e_2^b)) \;\leq\; b \qquad \text{for all } b \geq 0.$$

*Weight bookkeeping.* Consult the factorization:
- $[e_1 - 2 a_2 - 3 a_3 - 3]_{a_1}$ is a polynomial in $e_1$ alone, of $e_1$-degree $a_1$; weight $= a_1$.
- $\Psi(e_2^{a_2})(u - a_3)$: the uniform shift $u \mapsto u - a_3$ acts on symmetric polynomials by the substitution $e_k \mapsto \sum_j \binom{3-j}{k-j}(-a_3)^{k-j} e_j$ (an upper-triangular map preserving the $(1, 1, 2)$-filtration, since $w(e_k) = w(e_j)$ possible values are compatible with each triangular term). Hence weight $\leq w(\Psi(e_2^{a_2})) \leq a_2$ (assuming the residual claim).
- $\Psi(e_3^{a_3}) = \prod_i [u_i]_{a_3}$ expands in $e$-basis with top term $e_3^{a_3}$ (weight $2 a_3$) plus lower-degree symmetric polynomials whose weights are $\leq 2 a_3$. (Explicit check: $\prod_i [u_i]_c = \prod_i u_i \cdot \prod_i (u_i - 1) \cdots \prod_i (u_i - c + 1)$; each factor $\prod_i (u_i - k) = e_3 - k e_2 + k^2 e_1 - k^3$ has weight $2$, and $c$ factors give weight $\leq 2c$.)

Summing: total weight $\leq a_1 + a_2 + 2 a_3$, as required. Conversely, the trivial special case $(a_1, a_3) = (0, 0)$ shows the bound $w(\Psi(e_2^b)) \leq b$ is necessary. Equivalence established. $\square$

Details of the residual weight-tracking (in particular that $u \mapsto u - a_3$ preserves the $(1, 1, 2)$-filtration on $\Psi(e_2^{a_2})$ rather than merely bounding its degree) are in `proofs/2026-08-22-day125-psi-monomial-progress.md`, §Reduction.

### 13.5 The residual gap and empirical evidence

Only one gap remains: $w(\Psi(e_2^b)) \leq b$ for all $b \geq 0$.

**Empirical status.** Direct computation of $\Psi(e_2^b)$ in the $(u_1, u_2, u_3)$-Weyl-determinant form, followed by expansion in $e_1, e_2, e_3$ and monomial inspection, gives weight exactly $b$ for $b \in \{1, 2, \ldots, 10\}$. Zero violations.

**Failed proof attempts (Day 125).** Enumerated for the record:
1. *Direct induction on $b$.* Circular: the induction step requires the very bound being proved.
2. *Multiplicative bound $w(\Psi(fg)) \leq w(\Psi(f)) + w(\Psi(g))$.* False in general; $\Psi$ is not multiplicative.
3. *$e_2$-analog of Lemma A.* No uniform-shift factorization exists for $e_2^c$ — $e_2$ is a genuine polynomial in the $u_i$, not a monomial. Explicit small-$c$ counter-computation kills the analog.
4. *Derivation / Leibniz form for $\Psi$.* $\Psi$ satisfies a modified Leibniz rule with second-derivative defect terms; the defect obstructs a weight-additive bound.
5. *Stirling-minor bound.* Applying Stirling-transition on each row of the Weyl determinant gives a weight bound loose by a factor of 2.

The failures cluster around the fact that $e_2$ is the *only* elementary symmetric that is neither a single monomial nor $e_1$ (a linear form): it is genuinely two-dimensional in $u$-space, and none of the one-dimensional shift tricks apply.

Full documentation: `proofs/2026-08-22-day125-psi-monomial-progress.md`, §Failed attempts.
