## Corrections to our notes
1. **f_w is defined in §3 (Def 3.2), not §7.** §7 ("Categorical interpretation by coherent sheaves on G/P") defines a different basis g_w through the affine KL basis (Def 7.3). Prop 7.4 identifies g_w with the K-classes of the irreducible noncommutative-Springer (exotic) modules E_w in K_0(Coh^G(T*(G/B))). Lemma 7.15 shows f_w ≡ g_w in the cell subquotient N_c. The "coherent" reading belongs to g_w, and it agrees with f_w only modulo lower cells.
2. **"Steinberg" means Steinberg's 1975 basis e_w = w·exp(ϖ_{I(w)}) of Z[T] over Z[T]^W** ([24], "On a theorem of Pittie"). It has nothing to do with the Steinberg variety. The paper does not use the Steinberg variety.
3. f_w is a basis of the free module **N = C[T] over A = C[T]^W** (Cor 3.5). It is not a basis of C[T]/C[T]^W. The coinvariant quotient N/A_+ ≅ H^•(G/B) appears only in the proof of Lemma 3.7.
4. The paper uses the **group algebra Z[W]**, i.e. KL bases at q=1: "C_s = s − 1, C′_s = s + 1" (§2.1).

## Key verbatim quotes
- **Def 3.2** (p.7): "For any w ∈ W , let f_w = 1/|W_{I(w)}| C′_w(exp(ϖ_{I(w)})), f^w = 1/|W_{I(w)}| C^w(exp(ϖ_{I(w)}))." Here I(w) is the set of simple roots s_i with ℓ(ws_i) < ℓ(w), i.e. the right descents. ϖ_I is the sum of the fundamental weights over the complement of I. C^w is the dual basis under (a,b) = tr(a·i(b)), so "C^w = w0 C′_{w0 w}".
- **Lemma 3.7(3)** (p.8): "Define ξ : A[W]^c → N_Z^c by ξ(C′_w) = f_w. Then ξ is a W-equivariant isomorphism." Here N_Z^c = N_Z^{≤c}/N_Z^{<c} = ⊕_{y∈c} A_Z f_y (Def 3.6). Z[W]^c is the two-sided cell subquotient spanned by C′_w, w ∈ c (§2.1).
- The proof of Lemma 3.7 (p.8) contains: "For any v, w in a left cell c_L ⊂ c, we have I(v) = I(w) by [15, Section 8.6]. ... the map Z[W]^{c_L} → N_Z^{c_L} given by C′_w ↦ f_w, is W-equivariant."
- **Prop 3.8**: "{f_w} ... satisfies the conditions of Lemma 3.1 for the partial order ≤_LR."
- **Lemma 7.15** (p.24): "The images of the elements f_w and g_w agree in N_c, where c is the two-sided cell containing w."

## Why this answers Q70
In {f̄_w} (cell-graded) the W-action is, by Lemma 3.7(3), exactly the left-multiplication action of W on the C′-basis of the q=1 cell module Z[W]^c. That action is the q=1 W-graph representation in the C′ convention, left cells, with μ-edges. This is immediate from the definition. f_w is "C′_w applied to one vector exp(ϖ_I)", and the 1/|W_I| normalisation is constant on left cells. So the S_3/S_4/B_2 computation would only confirm a stated, proved lemma.
- Negative control (T_w ↔ Steinberg e_w): the paper says outright that the Steinberg basis's "C-span" is not W-invariant (§3.1). That is the stated reason f_w was introduced.
- Lemma 7.15 transfers the same statement to g_w = [E_w] (exotic irreducibles). So the "coherent reading of μ" (Crown V′) holds only on the associated graded, and there it is a citation (BFKM Lemmas 3.7 + 7.15, via Bezrukavnikov–Mirković [4]). It is not new.

## What is NOT stated (residue, low value)
- The full, unfiltered W-matrix of {f_w} or {g_w} over A, including the lower-cell tails with A-coefficients. sC′_w produces C′_y(exp ϖ_{I(w)}) for lower y, and these must be re-expanded in f_y over A. This is computable with their SageMath package [22] (github.com/caldermf/modular-reduction) if it is ever wanted. Grade: hunch that it carries nothing beyond the W-graph plus filtration data. Not recommended as a question.
- Non-crystallographic W is not covered. The setup is a Weyl group of an almost simple simply connected G, with p good.
