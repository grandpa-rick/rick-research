## The synthesis
- **The same B(∞) carries several bases.** Components give the semicanonical basis, IC sheaves the canonical basis, MV cycles the MV basis.
  All three are polite/biperfect, and **BKK 1905.08460 Thm 2.4 (first-hand): the bicrystal is unique.** The polytopes Pol(b) are MV polytopes for all of them
  (Baumann §3.3; for semicanonical, BKT 1110.3661, from memory). So **Φ_θ, ε, φ and the whole Q60 story are blind to which basis you hold.**
- **The frontier cluster studies exactly where they differ.** A reducible characteristic cycle means canonical ≠ semicanonical at that element.
  "Rigid component / real simple / □-irreducible" (KKKO 1412.8106 hub; Lapid–Mínguez) is the locus where they agree. Lee–Park 2610.32040's *imaginary
  elements* (recovering Leclerc 2002) are the first places they come apart.
- **MO 500059** (Browse 226: is P_{v,w}(1)>1 equivalent to conormal containment in the singular support of an IC Schubert sheaf?) is the Schubert/Hecke-side version
  of the same question. It touches our own **P^{J,q}=1 locus = Boolean seam** (Q55, Crown N). The trivial-KL locus is where nothing can go wrong.

## Seed payoff
- **Seed Q4** (q=0 for CHAs strips the linear algebra): this sharpens it. What q=0 strips, *precisely*, is the choice among polite bases.
  The crystal and every factorisation datum survive, and what dies is the distinction measured by characteristic cycles. A "q=0 CHA" would be a structure
  that can't tell canonical from semicanonical. That's a test any candidate must pass.
- **Seed Q2** (read KL from the crystal): this explains in one line why a crystal *alone* can't do it. KL data is IC-sheaf data, and the crystal is shared with
  the semicanonical basis, whose structure constants differ. So Q2 needs an extra datum beyond the crystal. Our Boolean/P=1 results are exactly the
  places where no extra datum is needed. That's a structural reason the Crown N results stopped at the Boolean locus.
