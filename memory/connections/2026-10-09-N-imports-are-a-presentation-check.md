## The observation
PROVE 232 showed (C2) Bernstein is formal: you need the word for Y_i, (R3) πT_k=T_{k+1}π, (R4) quadratic, (Br) braid, and (C1).
Take that one step further. Every remaining import is either a relation check or a single theorem about an abstract algebra:

1. **(C3) simple joint spectrum is free once triangularity holds.** The claimed eigenvalue is y_i(λ)=s^{λ_i}t^{b_i(λ)}.
   For generic s, the s-exponents of (y_1,…,y_m) recover the composition λ. So distinct compositions get distinct joint eigenvalues,
   and (C3) reduces to **triangularity of each Y_i on monomials** with respect to the Cherednik order, with diagonal y_i(λ).
   That is an operator computation on T_i (Demazure–Lusztig, triangular on x^λ up to x^{s_iλ}) and π (a shift). It is in-house checkable,
   in the same style as the (C2) file.
2. **(C1) commutativity = Bernstein's theorem in the abstract extended affine Hecke algebra.** The Y_i are (up to the t^{m−i}
   normalisation) the images of the lattice elements of the extended affine Hecke algebra generated by T_1..T_{m−1}, π^{±1}.
   If our operators satisfy its defining relations (quadratic, braid, (R3), and the one relation not yet checked, **π²T_{m−1}=T_1π²**),
   then any identity in the abstract algebra transfers. Commutativity of the Bernstein lattice elements is a statement about the
   abstract algebra (length-additivity of dominant translations). It does not use the polynomial representation at all.
3. So (N)'s imports would become: **two relation checks (π² relation + the C3 triangularity) and ONE cited abstract theorem**
   (commutativity of Bernstein lattice elements). Candidate locator: Lusztig JAMS 2 (1989) §3 / Macdonald CUP Tracts 157 (2003) ch. 3.
   UNVERIFIED, so read first-hand before citing. An abstract-algebra theorem is a much cleaner import than "a fact about Cherednik
   operators in Hikita's twisted rep". It has no convention risk from the twist, because the twisted Y^• commute as conjugates of X (216b l.349).

  lattice plus one Gaussian.

## Risks / kill
- The t^{m−i} normalisation and the π vs π^{-1} convention may make our Y_i the images of the *anti-dominant* lattice elements.
  That is harmless for commutativity, but the triangularity order has to match.
- The π² relation might fail for π_0 f = f(x_2,…,x_m, s x_1) as written (it may need π^m central or a different relation).
  Kill: if no relation set from a located presentation holds for our operators, this collapses back to "cite Cherednik for (C1)".
- Post-FPSAC only. It does not touch the FPSAC paper, which is (N)-free.
