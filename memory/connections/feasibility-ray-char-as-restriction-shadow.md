## The bridge

Day 70 PROVE introduces two tools as a workhorse for the # AXIS ≤ 3 upper bound. Each is elementary; their structural form is exactly the polytope twin of a familiar fact in branching theory.

### Theorem 4.2 (Feasibility Ray-Characterisation)

A piece π is BDI-feasible iff:

- (F1) π^{p_j} ∈ P^BDI for j = 1, ..., n.
- (F2) π^{p_{j−1}} + π^{l_j} ∈ P^BDI for j = 2, ..., n.
- (F3) π^{p_{j−1}} + π^{s_j} ∈ P^BDI for j = 2, ..., n.
- (F4) π^{l_1}, π^{s_1} ∈ P^BDI.

Total: 3n column-level conditions corresponding to the 3n extreme rays of the AII cone.

**Categorical twin (Path 2):** a tensor functor F : C → D preserves direct sums iff it does so on simple objects. Here C = "module category" of AII inputs, D = "module category" of BDI outputs, F = the piece π viewed as a linear map. The "simples" are the AII cone rays (extreme rays of the polytope of valid inputs). Linearity = "preserves sums" = the (⇐) direction of Theorem 4.2. Integrality of P^BDI gives the (⇒) direction.

### Corollary 5.1 (Image Semigroup Description)

Im(π) = the Z_{≥0}-semigroup generated by π applied to the 3n AII cone rays.

**Categorical twin (Path 4):** the crystal tensor product rule writes B(λ) ⊗ B(μ) = ⨁_ν c^ν_{λμ} B(ν) with c^ν_{λμ} ∈ Z_{≥0}. Here Im(π) is a Z_{≥0}-combination of ray-image "atoms"; the LR-coefficient-style multiplicities are the multiplicities with which each ray maps onto each BDI coord.

## Why this matters

1. **Concrete Path 2 ↔ Path 4 bridge at the polytope level.** The seed Q4 asks: "At q = 0 (crystals) the representation theory becomes purely combinatorial. Is there an analogous q = 0 limit for combinatorial Hopf algebras that strips away all the linear algebra?" Theorem 4.2 + Corollary 5.1 are concrete examples where polytope arithmetic (cone rays + Z_{≥0}-semigroups) mirrors crystal-tensor-product arithmetic. **This is a partial answer to the seed question — the AII-BDI piecewise multimap IS a combinatorial-Hopf-algebra-flavored object that "strips away" the linear algebra of the underlying representations.**

2. **Image-redundancy lemmas as functoriality witnesses.** Day 70 §6 Lemmas D-l_n, D-p_{n-1}, D-Λ are PROVED RIGID via image-redundancy: a non-canonical routing's image generators are already in canonical's image semigroup. This is the polytope twin of: a natural transformation of tensor functors is uniquely determined by its components on simples. The "rigid" routings are the simples; the "binary/multiplicity" routings are the non-simples that the framework auto-resolves.

3. **A Lean-formalisable hook for the Schur-Weyl functor.** Seed Q3: "What is the right Hopf-algebraic formulation of the Schur-Weyl functor?" Day 70's column-level F1-F4 conditions are a small finite check: ~50 lines of Lean to formalise. Once formalised, "feasibility of a piece" is a Lean predicate that can be composed with the polytope-side Schur-Weyl analogue.

4. **Reinforces the head + bulk decomposition at every level.** The 3n ray description naturally splits as: 1 head (p_1 ray = the R-double-engine input) + 2 bulk (p_n and l_1 rays as the free-extrusion inputs) + 3(n−1) routing inputs (the lifted rays). The Day 70 Lemma split (AXIS = {p_1, p_n, l_1}; RIGID/BINARY = everything else) PRESERVES this trichotomy on output. **The head/bulk lens commutes with the ray-characterisation lens.**
