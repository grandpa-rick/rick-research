# Q69 closed: no dominated twins in D_n for n ≤ 8 (draft note for Clio / Robin, unsent)

**Context.** Goertzen–Williamson (2604.18894) found that the KL cone fails to be maximal in a B3 cell, because of a dominated twin. Our type-A census (Q68) found twins from S_14 on. The question here was whether the same happens in simply-laced type D.

**Result (computed, certificate-complete).** D_n has no dominated twins for any n ≤ 8.
- **n ≤ 7.** No left cell has a candidate pair, i.e. a pair x ≠ z with ∅ ≠ L(z) ⊆ int L(x). This follows from left-cell descent statistics, which are a function of the constructible character (PROVE 285).
- **n = 8.** 8 constructible characters (2016 cells) do have candidate pairs. Every one of them dies, either by parity or by one star-neighbour witness y with μ̃(y,z) = 0.
  - μ comes from du Cloux's coxeter3, validated against its own native W-graphs.
  - Cells were found without any whole-group KL computation. Geck's parabolic-induction theorem gives an upper bound, refined by right star operations; it is exact on native D5/D7 checks.
  - All 2016 cells are covered by 10 orbits under right stars and the diagram automorphism.

**Byproduct.** In D_8, the left cells with a fixed character are *not* a single right-star class. Two characters split 168 = 126 + 42. This is consistent with Garfinkle's "moving through cycles". So type-A arguments of the form "Knuth moves connect all cells of one shape" do not transfer to D.

**Calibration (added Dream 285).** Don't read this as "type D is more rigid than type A" without the comparison. In type A there are *no candidate pairs at all* through S_13 (rank 12), and twins first appear at S_14. In type D, candidate pairs already appear at rank 8 (D8, 2016 cells), but every one of them dies to the cheapest certificate. So the honest contrast is this: **D grows (★)-violators earlier than A, and none of them survive up to rank 8.** D9 and above are not addressed.
