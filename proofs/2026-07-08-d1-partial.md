## 2. Lemma 1 (Δβ closed form) — proved

**Statement.** For all c ≥ 2,

    Δβ(c) := β(c) − β(c-1) = 1 + v₂(c-1).

**Proof.** Write n = c-1 (so c ≥ 2 ⟺ n ≥ 1). Set v = v₂(n). We claim
the identity of Kummer

    (K)     s₂(n) − s₂(n-1) = 1 − v.

Proof of (K): let n = 2^v · m with m odd. In binary, n ends in v
trailing zeros followed by a 1 at position v. Subtracting 1 turns those
v trailing zeros into v trailing ones and flips the position-v bit from
1 to 0. Net change to the digit sum: (v new ones on the right) − (1
old one at position v) = v − 1. So s₂(n-1) − s₂(n) = v − 1, i.e.,
s₂(n) − s₂(n-1) = 1 − v. ✓

Now

    Δβ(c) = 2(c-1) − s₂(c-1) − [2(c-2) − s₂(c-2)]
          = 2 − [s₂(n) − s₂(n-1)]
          = 2 − (1 − v)                   [by (K)]
          = 1 + v_2(c-1).                                    ∎

## 3. Lemma 2 (Mod-8 corollary from D1) — proved conditionally

**Statement.** Assume Conjecture D1: for odd c ≥ 3,

    Δβ'(c) = 1 − max(2, v_2(c-1)).

Then the dimer law Δβ'(c) = −1 holds if and only if c ≢ 1 (mod 8).

**Proof.** Δβ'(c) = −1
    ⟺ 1 − max(2, v_2(c-1)) = −1
    ⟺ max(2, v_2(c-1)) = 2
    ⟺ v_2(c-1) ≤ 2
    ⟺ 8 ∤ (c-1)
    ⟺ c ≢ 1 (mod 8).                                        ∎

**Three-case breakdown:**

- **c ≡ 3 (mod 4).** Then v₂(c-1) = 1, so Δβ'(c) = 1 − 2 = −1. Dimer.
- **c ≡ 5 (mod 8).** Then v₂(c-1) = 2, so Δβ'(c) = 1 − 2 = −1. Dimer.
- **c ≡ 1 (mod 8).** Then v₂(c-1) ≥ 3, so Δβ'(c) = 1 − v₂(c-1) ≤ −2.
  Dimer breaks. In particular:
  - c = 9  (v₂=3): Δβ' = −2 ← matches data.
  - c = 17 (v₂=4): Δβ' = −3 ← predicted.
  - c = 33 (v₂=5): Δβ' = −4 ← predicted.
  - c = 4·2^t + 1 (v₂=t+2): Δβ' = −(t+1) ← predicted family.

Both branches agree at v₂(c-1) = 2, which is what makes the corollary
clean: the transition from "dimer holds" to "dimer breaks" is exactly
where v₂ climbs above 2, i.e., at c ≡ 1 mod 8.

## 4. Theorem 3 (4-period identity) — proved from D1 + (E)

**Empirical anchor (E).** β'(4k) = β(4k) for all k ≥ 1
(equivalently, D(4k) = 0).

Data: D(4) = 0, D(8) = 0. Extension conjecture: D(4k) = 0 for all
k ≥ 1. Structural rationale: at c ≡ 0 mod 4, the ambient Kummer floor
and the heavy-quotient floor coincide — the internal 2-adic slack in
H_c that opens at odd c ≡ 1 mod 8 closes back up cleanly at the next
multiple of 4.

**Theorem 3.** Assume D1 (Δβ'(c) = 1 − max(2, v₂(c-1)) for odd c ≥ 3)
and (E). Then for all k ≥ 1,

    Δβ'(4k+2) + Δβ'(4k+4) = 9 + 2 v₂(k).

**Proof.** By (E),

    β'(4(k+1)) − β'(4k) = β(4(k+1)) − β(4k)
                       = Σ_{i=1}^4 Δβ(4k+i).

By Lemma 1, Δβ(4k+i) = 1 + v₂(4k+i-1). Two of the four (i=2, i=4)
have 4k+i-1 odd, contributing 1 each. The other two contribute
1 + v₂(4k) + 1 + v₂(4k+2) = 2 + (2+v₂(k)) + 1 = 5 + v₂(k). So

    β'(4(k+1)) − β'(4k) = 2 + (5 + v₂(k)) = 7 + v₂(k).                (*)

Now split the LHS into the four Δβ'(4k+i):

    Σ Δβ'(4k+i) = 7 + v₂(k).                                            (**)

The two odd-c terms are computable via D1:

    Δβ'(4k+1) = 1 − max(2, v_2(4k)) = 1 − max(2, 2+v₂(k)) = −1 − v₂(k),
    Δβ'(4k+3) = 1 − max(2, v_2(4k+2)) = 1 − max(2, 1)     = −1.

Sum of the odd contributions:

    Δβ'(4k+1) + Δβ'(4k+3) = −2 − v₂(k).                                 (***)

Subtracting (***) from (**):

    Δβ'(4k+2) + Δβ'(4k+4) = (7 + v₂(k)) − (−2 − v₂(k)) = 9 + 2 v₂(k). ∎

**Verification against data.**

| k | c=4k+2 | Δβ'(4k+2) | c=4k+4 | Δβ'(4k+4) | sum | 9 + 2 v₂(k) |
|---|--------|-----------|--------|-----------|-----|-------------|
| 1 |   6    |     4     |   8    |     5     |  9  |     9       |
| 2 |  10    |     5     |  12    |     6*    | 11  |    11       |

(* Δβ'(12) = 6 is predicted, not measured; needs Clio's H_12.)

## 5. Theorem 4 (complete closed form under D1 + (E) + D2)

**Conjecture D2.** For all k ≥ 1, β'(4k+2) = β(4k+2) − 1 − v₂(k).

Data: β'(6) = 8 − 1 − 0 = 7 ✓, β'(10) = 16 − 1 − 1 = 14 ✓.

D2 is not implied by D1 + (E) — those only fix the *sum*
Δβ'(4k+2) + Δβ'(4k+4). But data at c = 6, 10 pin down the split at
k = 1, 2, and D2 is the natural interpolation.

**Theorem 4.** Under D1 + (E) + D2, for all c ≥ 4,

    β'(c) = β(c) − D(c),

where D is piecewise closed in (c mod 4) and v₂ of a shifted quotient:

    D(4k)   = 0
    D(4k+1) = 4 + 2 v₂(k)        (k ≥ 1)
    D(4k+2) = 1 + v₂(k)          (k ≥ 1)
    D(4k+3) = 4 + v₂(k)          (k ≥ 1).

**Proof.**
- D(4k) = 0 is exactly (E).
- D(4k+2) = 1 + v₂(k) is exactly D2.
- D(4k+1) = D(4k) + [ΔD(4k+1)] where ΔD(4k+1) = Δβ(4k+1) − Δβ'(4k+1)
  = (1 + v₂(4k)) − (−1 − v₂(k)) = 2 + (2+v₂(k)) + v₂(k) = 4 + 2v₂(k).
  So D(4k+1) = 0 + 4 + 2v₂(k) = 4 + 2v₂(k).
- D(4k+3) = D(4k+2) + [ΔD(4k+3)] where ΔD(4k+3) = Δβ(4k+3) − Δβ'(4k+3)
  = (1 + v_2(4k+2)) − (−1) = 1 + 1 + 1 = 3.
  So D(4k+3) = 1 + v₂(k) + 3 = 4 + v₂(k).                             ∎

## 6. Structural anatomy at c = 5 and Conjecture S

### Anatomy of the c = 5 minimum

The value β'(5) = 3 is realised at (a, b, j) = (3, 0, 2). Expanding
H_5 at (3, 0, 2):

    H_5(3, 0, 2) = h_0(3,0) · C(2,0)
                 + h_1(3,0) · C(2,1)
                 + h_2(3,0) · C(2,2),

with:

- **h_0(3,0) · 1** = 6·7·8·9 · 2·3·4·5 = 362880 = 9!. v₂ = **7**.
- **h_1(3,0) · 2** = (−20)·6·7·8 · 2·3·4 · 2 = −322560. v₂ = **10**.
- **h_2(3,0) · 1** = (−10) · 6·7 · 2·3 · [(3+2)(0+1) − 24]
                   = (−10) · 42 · 6 · (5 − 24)
                   = (−10) · 42 · 6 · (−19)
                   = 47880. v₂ = **3**.

Sum = 362880 − 322560 + 47880 = 88200 = 2³ · 3² · 5² · 7². v₂ = **3**. ✓

**Key observation.** The floor v₂ = 3 is carried by the h_2·C(2,2)
term alone. The other two terms have strictly higher v₂ (7 and 10)
and cannot lower the sum's v₂ below 3. This is a "sharp cancellation
via a single term" structure.

**Decomposition of v₂(h_2 at (3,0)):**

    v₂(−10) = 1
    v₂((a+3)(a+4)) at a=3 = v₂(6·7) = 1
    v₂((b+2)(b+3)) at b=0 = v₂(2·3) = 1
    v₂((a+2)(b+1) − 24) at (3,0) = v₂(5·1 − 24) = v₂(−19) = 0
    ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
    Total v₂(h_2(3,0)) = 3.

The `(a+2)(b+1) − 24` factor is critical: at (3, 0) it equals −19,
which is *odd*. If we had chosen (a, b) with (a+2)(b+1) even, this
factor would have contributed at least 1 more, driving v₂ above 3
(bad — β' would need a different witness). If we had chosen (a, b)
with (a+2)(b+1) ≡ 24 mod 8, we'd get a *deeper* drop — but the
minimising choice is exactly the "cusp" where (a+2)(b+1) = 5 sits
odd but small.

**Structural mnemonic.** 24 = 4! = (c-1)!. The polynomial factor is
`(a+2)(b+1) − (c-1)!` at c = 5. So h_2 has the form

    h_2 = −C(c,2) · Π_{i=3}^{c-1}(a+i) · Π_{i=2}^{c-2}(b+i)
                  · [ (a+2)(b+1) − (c-1)! ]

(this reduces to the Clio h_2 at c = 5 with `-C(5,2) = -10`; the
run lengths are c-3 on each side; for c=5 that's 2 terms each,
matching (a+3)(a+4) and (b+2)(b+3)).

### Conjecture S (structural)

For each odd c with v₂(c-1) ≤ 2, the minimum β'(c) = β(c) − 4 is
realised at (a*, b*, j*) with:

- j* = 2 (so C(j*, k*) picks up the "central" coefficient h_{k*}
        where k* is the h_k that carries the sharp cancellation);
- (a*, b*) chosen so that (a+2)(b+1) is a small odd number, killing
  the polynomial factor's v₂;
- run products (a+3)…(a+c-3) and (b+2)…(b+c-4) contribute exactly
  min-v₂ of a length-(c-3) run of consecutive integers.

Under Conjecture S, β'(c) = β(c) − 4 − 2·v₂(k) at c = 4k+1 for
k ≥ 1 is a direct consequence of the h_2·C(j*,2) structure plus
the way (a+2)(b+1) − (c-1)! interacts with high v₂(c-1). The v₂(k)
term appears because (c-1)! = (4k)! carries an extra v₂(k) beyond
its s₂ contribution, and the polynomial factor (a+2)(b+1) − (4k)!
can pick up this extra 2-adic slack when v₂(k) ≥ 1.

Conjecture S is currently a **hunch**. Elevating it to `sketched`
would require:

1. A general H_c(a,b,j) with the analogous 9 → (2c−1)-term structure
   for c > 5 and a coefficient pattern matching the c=5 case;
2. Verification that the minimising (a*, b*, j*) at each odd c
   indeed sits at (a+2)(b+1) small-odd, in the "central" h_k*·C(j*,k*)
   term.

Alternatively, Track B (build a general H_c engine from Clio's
Lemma 1 template) would let us test S computationally at c = 6,
7, ..., 17.

    H_c(a, b, 0)_predicted = M_0 · c! · (a + c + 1) · ∏_{i=1}^c (b+i)
