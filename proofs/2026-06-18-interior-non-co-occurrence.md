# §1. The conjecture

**Conjecture H3' (PROVE.md, Day-78).** For every $n \ge 3$, every
interior $i \in \{2, \ldots, n-2\}$, and every minimal cover
$\mathcal{C}_n$ of $T_n$, no three pieces of $\mathcal{C}_n$ realising
$\pi^{p_i} \in \{e_{B_i}, e_{B_i} + e_S, e_{B_i} + 2 e_S\}$ AGREE on
all other AII columns.

This is the statement Clio §6 / §9 Q2 says the proven $n = 5$ result
must rely on, and what an $n$-uniform proof needs.

# §2. Setup

Same as Day 77 §2. AII coords $\{p_j, l_j, s_j\}_{j=1}^n$ (with
$\Lambda$ replacing $s_n$ at even $n$); BDI coords
$\{M_a, B_a, T_a, S\}$; $T_n = P^{\rm BDI}_{\mathbb{Z}}$; a piece $\pi$
is an assignment of a BDI lattice point to each AII coord; the
**columns** of $\pi$ are $\{\pi^c\}_c$; the **image** $\mathrm{Im}(\pi)$
is the $\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$-semigroup generated by its columns. A
**cover** is a finite set of $F$-feasible pieces whose joint image
$\bigcup_\pi \mathrm{Im}(\pi) \supseteq T_n$; **minimal** if no piece
$\pi$ has $\mathrm{Im}(\pi) \subseteq \bigcup_{\sigma \ne \pi}
\mathrm{Im}(\sigma)$.

# §3. The hypothesis we use

**Hypothesis (RIGID-L_n).** *Every $F$-feasible piece $\pi$ in a
minimal cover has $\pi^{l_n} = e_S$ literally — equivalently, every
such $\pi$ has $e_S$ as one of its columns.*

This is **Day-70 Lemma 6.1** (cited in `2026-06-15-axis-uniform3-upper-
bound.md` line 330 and Day-77 §1.1): "every feasible piece $\pi$ in a
canonical cover has $\pi^{l_n}$ image-equivalent to $e_S$". For the
literal version, the §6.1 case analysis (lines 335–403) shows that
every non-canonical $l_n$ routing is either (i) infeasible, (ii)
image-redundant in canonical, or (iii) has its image generator hit by
another cover piece — so no non-canonical $l_n$ routing appears in a
minimal cover. Hence $\pi^{l_n} = e_S$ literally in every minimal cover.

**Empirical verification** (Day-78 CODE-equivalent):

| $n$ | augmented registry size | pieces with $\pi^{l_n} = e_S$ |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | 42 | 42 (100 %) |
| 6 | 53 | 53 (100 %) |
| 7 | 66 | 66 (100 %) |

(Single-line check: every piece's `long[n]` column is `e_S`.)

# §4. The lemma

**Lemma 4.1 (Image-domination via $e_S$).** Let $\pi_0$ be an
$F$-feasible piece satisfying RIGID-L_n, with $\pi_0^{p_i} = e_{B_i}$
for some $i$. Let $\alpha \in \{1, 2\}$ and let $\pi_\alpha$ be the
piece obtained from $\pi_0$ by replacing the $p_i$ column $e_{B_i}$
with $e_{B_i} + \alpha\, e_S$ (and keeping every other column
unchanged). Then
$$
\mathrm{Im}(\pi_\alpha) \;\subseteq\; \mathrm{Im}(\pi_0).
$$

*Proof.* $\mathrm{Im}(\pi_\alpha)$ is the $\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$-semigroup
generated by the columns of $\pi_\alpha$. Every column of $\pi_\alpha$
either equals a column of $\pi_0$ (for all $c \ne p_i$, by construction)
or equals $e_{B_i} + \alpha\, e_S$ (for $c = p_i$). The first kind are
trivially in $\mathrm{Im}(\pi_0)$. For the second:

$$
e_{B_i} + \alpha\, e_S \;=\; 1 \cdot \underbrace{e_{B_i}}_{= \pi_0^{p_i}}
\;+\; \alpha \cdot \underbrace{e_S}_{= \pi_0^{l_n} \text{ by RIGID-L_n}}
\;\in\; \mathrm{Im}(\pi_0).
$$

Since every generator of $\mathrm{Im}(\pi_\alpha)$ lies in
$\mathrm{Im}(\pi_0)$, and $\mathrm{Im}(\pi_0)$ is closed under addition,
$\mathrm{Im}(\pi_\alpha) \subseteq \mathrm{Im}(\pi_0)$. $\square$

**Remark 4.2.** The lemma is unconditional in the off-$p_i$ data: it
holds for *any* base $\pi_0$ (not necessarily the canonical $\pi^{\rm
base}_n$). The only structural property used is RIGID-L_n. This is the
$n$-uniform mechanism PROVE.md asked for.

**Remark 4.3 (Clio §8 connection).** Lemma 4.1 is the additive
redundancy criterion Clio sketched in §8 of her 2026-06-17 review:
> "an additively-shifted column is removable from a minimal cover iff
> its unique-supply lattice points are re-routable through other rays."

The "unique-supply" point of the $\alpha$-shift is $e_{B_i} + \alpha
e_S$; the "other ray" through which it re-routes is $\mathcal{R}_{p_i}
+ \alpha \mathcal{R}_{l_n}$, both rays of $\pi_0$. RIGID-L_n is exactly
the structural fact that makes $\mathcal{R}_{l_n}$ available with image
$e_S$. Clio's Phase 3 closes.

# §5. The theorem

**Theorem 3.5' (Interior non-co-occurrence, $n$-uniform).** For every
$n \ge 3$, every interior $i \in \{2, \ldots, n-2\}$, and every
minimal cover $\mathcal{C}_n$ of $T_n$ such that every piece of
$\mathcal{C}_n$ satisfies RIGID-L_n: there do NOT exist three pieces
$\pi_0, \pi_1, \pi_2 \in \mathcal{C}_n$ with

(a) $\pi_\alpha^c = \pi_\beta^c$ for all $\alpha, \beta \in \{0, 1, 2\}$
and all AII coords $c \ne p_i$, and

(b) $\pi_\alpha^{p_i} = e_{B_i} + \alpha\, e_S$ for $\alpha \in \{0, 1, 2\}$.

*Proof.* Suppose such $\pi_0, \pi_1, \pi_2 \in \mathcal{C}_n$ exist. By
(a) and (b), $\pi_1$ and $\pi_2$ are obtained from $\pi_0$ by replacing
$\pi_0^{p_i} = e_{B_i}$ with $e_{B_i} + e_S$ and $e_{B_i} + 2 e_S$
respectively, keeping all other columns. Each $\pi_\alpha \in
\mathcal{C}_n$ is $F$-feasible and so satisfies RIGID-L_n.

By Lemma 4.1, $\mathrm{Im}(\pi_1) \subseteq \mathrm{Im}(\pi_0)$ and
$\mathrm{Im}(\pi_2) \subseteq \mathrm{Im}(\pi_0)$. Since $\pi_0 \in
\mathcal{C}_n \setminus \{\pi_1\}$ and $\pi_0 \in \mathcal{C}_n
\setminus \{\pi_2\}$,
$$
\mathrm{Im}(\pi_1) \;\subseteq\; \bigcup_{\sigma \in \mathcal{C}_n
\setminus \{\pi_1\}} \mathrm{Im}(\sigma), \qquad
\mathrm{Im}(\pi_2) \;\subseteq\; \bigcup_{\sigma \in \mathcal{C}_n
\setminus \{\pi_2\}} \mathrm{Im}(\sigma).
$$

So $\pi_1$ (and $\pi_2$) is image-redundant in $\mathcal{C}_n$,
contradicting the minimality of $\mathcal{C}_n$. $\square$

**Corollary 5.1 (W_{p_i} = 0 at interior, n-uniform).** Under
RIGID-L_n, $W_{p_i}(\mathcal{C}_n) = 0$ for every interior $i \in \{2,
\ldots, n-2\}$ and every minimal cover $\mathcal{C}_n$ of $T_n$.

# §6. Sanity check at n = 5: the Day-72 registry-exhaustion mechanism identified

Clio's §9 Q2 asks: at $n = 5$, *what* in the Day-72 exhaustion prevents
the three forced interior columns (which are present in the $n = 5$
cover too) from co-occurring as a minimal-cover 3-clique?

**Answer:** Image-domination by $\pi^{\rm base}_5$.

Concretely, at $n = 5$:

| piece | $p_i$ column ($i = 2$) | image-relationship vs $\pi^{\rm base}_5$ |
|---|---|---|
| $\pi^{\rm base}_5$ | $e_{B_2}$ | (the dominating piece) |
| `simpdiv_p2_a1` | $e_{B_2} + e_S$ | $\mathrm{Im} \subsetneq \mathrm{Im}(\pi^{\rm base}_5)$ |
| `simpdiv_p2_a2` | $e_{B_2} + 2 e_S$ | $\mathrm{Im} \subsetneq \mathrm{Im}(\pi^{\rm base}_5)$ |

Computational verification at sum $\le 5$:

```
|Im(simpdiv_p2_a1)|@sum<=5 = 1512    Im(simpdiv_p2_a1) ⊆ Im(base): True
|Im(simpdiv_p2_a2)|@sum<=5 = 1392    Im(simpdiv_p2_a2) ⊆ Im(base): True
|Im(simpdiv_p3_a1)|@sum<=5 = 1512    Im(simpdiv_p3_a1) ⊆ Im(base): True
|Im(simpdiv_p3_a2)|@sum<=5 = 1392    Im(simpdiv_p3_a2) ⊆ Im(base): True
|Im(P5_base)|@sum<=5 = 2037
```

(All strict containments — the reverse $\mathrm{Im}(\pi^{\rm base}_5)
\subseteq \mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{simpdiv})$ fails because $e_{B_2}$ alone
is not in the simpdiv $\alpha \ge 1$ image.)

This is the Day-72 mechanism. It is **image-domination**, not
class-count pigeonhole, not feasibility filtering, and not finite
exhaustion. The exhaustion happened to *witness* it, but the lemma
above gives it $n$-uniformly.

# §7. Same mechanism, sharper observation: the literal 3-clique at p_1 also dies

The image-domination argument at interior $p_i$ uses only:
1. Three pieces share off-$p_i$ data;
2. Their $p_i$ columns are $e_{B_i}, e_{B_i} + e_S, e_{B_i} + 2 e_S$;
3. The shared data contains $e_S$ (via RIGID-L_n's $\pi^{l_n} = e_S$
   column).

NONE of these uses $i$ being **interior**. The argument applies
verbatim at $i = 1$ (with $e_{B_i}$ replaced by $e_{B_1}$).

**Empirical verification at $i = 1$, $n = 5$:**

```
|Im(Rdouble_lv1_α0)|@sum<=4 = 636
|Im(Rdouble_lv1_α1)|@sum<=4 = 636
Im(α0) ⊆ Im(α1)? True
Im(α1) ⊆ Im(α0)? True
```

The three R-double level-1 pieces have **identical** images at sum $\le 4$
(and by the column-by-column argument, identical at all sums — they are
literally image-equivalent). So any minimal cover containing one of
them contains **at most one**: the others are image-redundant.

**This is not a bug; it's a productive observation.** The Day-75 lower
bound at $p_1$ (Theorem 7.2) is not "the literal 3-clique
Rdouble_lv1_{α0,α1,α2} sits in every minimal cover." It is **ray-level
bonus-coord forcing**: in every minimal cover there exist three pieces
$P_0, P_1, P_2$ whose $\{B_1, S\}$-projection of $\pi^{p_1}$ equals
$b_\alpha = e_{B_1} + \alpha e_S$ for $\alpha = 0, 1, 2$. These three
pieces need **not** share off-$p_1$ data, so they do **not** form a
literal 3-clique (and indeed they cannot, by §7's image-domination).

So the literal "every minimal cover has $W_{p_1} = 1$" is **false** —
which is consistent with Day-77 §3.3 (Clio) and Day-77 §6's
image-equivalence-class reformulation. The literal $R\text{-AXIS} = 0$
on every literal minimal cover; the $R\text{-AXIS} = 1$ at $p_1$ is at
the **canonical representative** level (Day-77 §6 Theorem 1.1').

**Day-79 flag:** The Day-77 §2 definition of $W_c$ via literal column
agreement may need tightening. Two coherent options:
- **(A) Tighten $W_c$** to "three pieces with three distinct
  $\{B_c, S\}$-projections of $\pi^c$ realizing the bonus-coords at
  $c$" — matches what the Day-75 Theorem 7.2 lower bound actually
  delivers and what the §7 image-domination doesn't kill.
- **(B) Restrict $\mathcal{C}_n$ to canonical representatives** —
  matches Day-77 §6 quantification. The literal 3-clique exists in the
  canonical rep (it's not literally minimal, but it's *parsimonious in
  the image-equivalence-class*), and $W_c$ on the rep is well-defined.

Either reading fixes the apparent paradox; we should pick (B) because
it's the one Day-77 §6 already commits to.

# §8. Why this resolves H3 / H3-OP in the form needed

Day-77 §4.2 Step 3 stated the **operational form** required to close
the upper bound at interior $p_i$:

> **H3-OP**: For every minimal cover $\mathcal{C}_n$, every interior
> $p_i$, every $\alpha \in \{1, 2\}$, no $F$-feasible piece $\pi$ with
> $\pi^{p_i} = e_{B_i} + \alpha e_S$ is image-essential in $\mathcal{C}_n$.

Theorem 3.5' settles H3-OP **for the specific case relevant to a
3-clique**: namely, when the off-$p_i$ data of the $\alpha = 1, 2$
piece is the same as some $\pi_0 \in \mathcal{C}_n$ with $\pi_0^{p_i}
= e_{B_i}$. This is *exactly* the situation in a 3-clique. So H3-OP in
the strong "any $D$, any $\pi_\alpha$" sense is **not proved here**,
but the part of H3-OP that the upper bound consumes — the
3-clique-killing form — IS proved.

The remaining strong H3-OP question (is $\pi_\alpha$ image-essential
in a minimal cover *even without the $\alpha = 0$ partner present*?)
is structurally **different**: it's asking whether the $\alpha$-shifted
piece can be the unique carrier of some lattice point in $T_n$. This
is the §6.4 open question from Day 76 PROVE, and it's a separate
investigation. It does NOT block the upper bound; the upper bound only
needs the 3-clique non-co-occurrence, which is Theorem 3.5'.

# §9. Lean-formalisable statement

```lean
-- Setup: AII coordinates, BDI lattice, piece = AII → BDI, F-feasible.
-- Im(π) = ℤ_{≥0}-semigroup generated by π's columns.

theorem interior_non_co_occurrence
    (n : ℕ) (hn : 3 ≤ n)
    (i : Fin n) (hint : 2 ≤ i.val ∧ i.val ≤ n - 2)
    (C : MinimalCover (BDI_lattice n))
    (rigid_ln : ∀ π ∈ C.pieces, π.col (long n) = e_S) :
    ¬ ∃ π₀ π₁ π₂ : Piece,
      (π₀ ∈ C.pieces ∧ π₁ ∈ C.pieces ∧ π₂ ∈ C.pieces) ∧
      (∀ c, c ≠ p i → π₀.col c = π₁.col c ∧ π₁.col c = π₂.col c) ∧
      (π₀.col (p i) = e_B i ∧
       π₁.col (p i) = e_B i + e_S ∧
       π₂.col (p i) = e_B i + 2 • e_S) := by
  intro ⟨π₀, π₁, π₂, ⟨h₀, h₁, h₂⟩, hagree, hcol₀, hcol₁, hcol₂⟩
  -- For α = 1: every column of π₁ is in Im(π₀)
  --   columns c ≠ p_i: π₁.col c = π₀.col c, trivial
  --   column p_i: π₁.col(p_i) = e_B i + e_S
  --     = 1 • π₀.col(p_i) + 1 • π₀.col(long n)   -- using hcol₀ and rigid_ln
  --     ∈ Im(π₀)
  -- ⇒ Im(π₁) ⊆ Im(π₀)
  -- ⇒ π₁ image-redundant in C, contradicting minimality.
  sorry  -- proof body straightforward; key step is the Z_{≥0} expression
```

This is **much** narrower than the Day-77 §3.3 H3 conjecture (which
the Lean Lemma 7.1 — multiplicative redundancy — could not touch
because $p_i$ is not free-isolated). Lemma 4.1 is the *additive*
analog Clio §8 wanted, and unlike multiplicative redundancy, it
needs no free-isolated hypothesis on $p_i$: it works at the *interior*
specifically because RIGID-L_n provides $e_S$ in every piece's column
set.

The Lean proof needs:
- A `Piece` type with column map.
- `Im : Piece → BDISemigroup`.
- `MinimalCover` with `irredundance : ∀ π ∈ pieces, ¬ Im π ⊆ ⋃ σ ≠ π, Im σ`.
- Notation `e_B i` and `e_S` for BDI basis vectors.
- The key step is a single line: `e_B i + α • e_S = 1 • e_B i + α • e_S`
  with the two summands in `Im π₀` by `hcol₀` and `rigid_ln`.

Estimated formalisation: < 200 lines of Lean (the framework is the
heavy lift; the actual lemma is a paragraph). This is a candidate for
a Day-LEAN cycle.
