# (T-a) The 1D $a$-degree bound

## Statement

**Theorem (T-a).** For every $R \geq 1$ and every generic $c$ (integer $c > 2R$ suffices),
$$\deg_a Q_{2R}(a, b, c) \leq R.$$

Empirically **equality** for $R = 1, 2, 3, 4$ at multiple $c$-values.

By the $a \leftrightarrow b$ symmetry of $Q_{2R}$ (Day 109 Remark R2), (T-b) follows, hence (T) since (T) = (T-a) $\wedge$ (T-b).

## Setup (recap)

Let $y_1 = a+2$, $y_2 = b+1$, $y_3 = c$, $V := (y_1{-}y_2)(y_1{-}y_3)(y_2{-}y_3) = (a{-}b{+}1)(a{-}c{+}2)(b{-}c{+}1)$.

$\mathcal{S}_j$: the set of partitions of $2j$ built by $j$ successive vertical 2-strips added to $\emptyset$, with $\ell(\mu) \leq 3$; $\kappa_\mu$ is the walk-count.

$$ds_j := \sum_{\mu \in \mathcal{S}_j} \kappa_\mu \det\bigl[(y_i)_{\downarrow \mu_l + 3-l}\bigr]_{i,l=1}^{3}, \qquad S_j := ds_j / V \in \mathbb{Z}[a, b, c].$$

$$H_c(a, b, j) := (a+3)_{c-1-j}\, (b+2)_{c-1-j}\, S_j(a, b, c), \qquad h_{2R} := \sum_{j=0}^{2R} (-1)^{2R-j}\binom{2R}{j} H_c(a, b, j),$$
$$Q_{2R}(a, b, c) := h_{2R} / \bigl[(a+3)_{c-1-2R}(b+2)_{c-1-2R}\bigr].$$

## Proof outline

The proof has **five ingredients**:

1. **Lemma 1** ($\deg_a S_j$-bound): $\deg_a S_j \leq j$.
2. **Layer formula** ($\star$): explicit expression for $[a^{c-1-d}] H_c(a, b, j)$.
3. **Sub-lemma (E)** (elementary-symmetric bound): $E_i(j) := e_i(3, \ldots, c+1-j)$ is a polynomial in $j$ of degree $\leq i$.
4. **Sub-lemma (⋆⋆-a'')** (core): $Q_j(b) \cdot [a^{j-p}]S_j(a, b, c) = (b+2)_{c-1-2p} \cdot R_p(b, c, j)$ where $R_p$ has per-b-slot $j$-degree $\leq 2p$.
5. **Lemma A** (finite differences): $\Delta^N$ annihilates polynomials of degree $< N$.

Combined via a straightforward degree bookkeeping, these give (T-a).

## Reduction

Since $h_{2R}$ is divisible (in $\mathbb{Q}[a, b, c]$) by $(a+3)_{c-1-2R}$ (Rick's pipeline theorem — the impulse-free identity), $h_{2R} = (a+3)_{c-1-2R} \cdot k(a, b, c)$ for a polynomial $k$. Then
$$Q_{2R} = \frac{h_{2R}}{(a+3)_{c-1-2R}(b+2)_{c-1-2R}} = \frac{k(a, b, c)}{(b+2)_{c-1-2R}}.$$

$(b+2)_{c-1-2R}$ has no $a$-dependence, so $\deg_a Q_{2R} = \deg_a k = \deg_a h_{2R} - (c-1-2R)$.

**Reduction:** $\deg_a Q_{2R} \leq R \iff \deg_a h_{2R} \leq c - R - 1$.

## Lemma 1: $\deg_a S_j \leq j$

**Statement.** $\deg_a (ds_j / V) \leq j$ for all $j \geq 0$.

*Proof.* $S_j = \sum_{\mu \in \mathcal{S}_j} \kappa_\mu s^*_\mu(a+2, b+1, c)$ (Vandermonde-Weyl). Each shifted Schur $s^*_\mu(y_1, y_2, y_3)$ has $\deg_{y_1} s^*_\mu \leq \mu_1$.

**Claim:** for all $\mu \in \mathcal{S}_j$, $\mu_1 \leq j$.

*Proof of claim.* By definition, $\mu \in \mathcal{S}_j$ is reached from $\emptyset$ by $j$ successive vertical 2-strip additions. A **vertical 2-strip** places (at most) one cell in each row. Since we perform $j$ such additions, row 1 receives at most $j$ cells total, so $\mu_1 \leq j$. $\square$

Since $y_1 = a + 2$, $\deg_a y_1^{\mu_1} = \mu_1$; taking maximum over $\mu \in \mathcal{S}_j$: $\deg_a S_j \leq \max \mu_1 \leq j$. $\square$

**Verification:** Step 1 of `2026-08-19-Ta-verify.py`, PASS for $j = 0, \ldots, 11$ (equality: $\deg_a S_j = j$).

## Layer formula ($\star$)

**Lemma ($\star$).** For $d \geq 0$ and $j \geq 0$ (with $j \geq d$, else empty sum),
$$[a^{c-1-d}] H_c(a, b, j) = Q_j(b) \sum_{p=0}^{d} E_{d-p}(j) \cdot A_p(b, c, j),$$
where $E_i(j) := [a^{c-1-j-i}] (a+3)_{c-1-j} = e_i(3, 4, \ldots, c+1-j)$ and $A_p(b, c, j) := [a^{j-p}] S_j(a, b, c)$, and $Q_j(b) := (b+2)_{c-1-j}$.

*Proof.* Since $Q_j(b)$ has no $a$-dependence,
$$[a^{c-1-d}] H_c = Q_j(b) \cdot [a^{c-1-d}] (P_j(a) \cdot S_j(a, b, c)).$$
By convolution:
$$[a^{c-1-d}] (P_j S_j) = \sum_{N_1 + N_2 = c-1-d} [a^{N_1}]P_j \cdot [a^{N_2}]S_j.$$
$P_j$ has $a$-degree $c-1-j$: $N_1 \leq c-1-j$. $S_j$ has $a$-degree $\leq j$: $N_2 \leq j$. Setting $N_2 = j - p$ ($p \geq 0$), $N_1 = c-1-d - (j - p) = c-1-j - (d-p)$. Constraint $N_1 \leq c-1-j$ gives $p \leq d$. So $p \in \{0, \ldots, d\}$ (assuming $j \geq d$ so all $N_2 \geq 0$).

With $i := (c-1-j) - N_1 = d - p$, $E_{d-p}(j) := [a^{N_1}]P_j = e_{d-p}(3, \ldots, c+1-j)$. And $A_p(b, c, j) := [a^{N_2}] S_j = [a^{j-p}] S_j$. Substituting completes the formula. $\square$

## Sub-lemma (E)

**Statement.** $E_i(j) = e_i(3, 4, \ldots, c+1-j)$ (elementary symmetric polynomial of the segment $\{3, \ldots, c+1-j\}$, of length $c-1-j$) is a polynomial in $j$ (with $c$ fixed integer) of degree $\leq i$.

*Proof.* $e_i(3, \ldots, L)$ for $L = c+1-j$ is a polynomial in $L$ of degree $i$. This is standard: $\sum_i e_i(3, \ldots, L) x^i = \prod_{k=3}^{L}(1 + kx)$; the coefficient $e_i$ counts weighted subsets of $\{3, \ldots, L\}$ of size $i$, which is a polynomial in $L$ of degree exactly $i$ (with leading term $\binom{L-2}{i}$-like in $L$). Since $L = c+1-j$ is linear in $j$, $E_i$ has $j$-degree $\leq i$. $\square$

## Sub-lemma (⋆⋆-a''): the core structural bound

**Statement.** For each $p \geq 0$ and each integer $c$ sufficiently large,
$$Q_j(b) \cdot A_p(b, c, j) = (b+2)_{c-1-2p} \cdot R_p(b, c, j), \qquad (\text{DIV-}p)$$
in $\mathbb{Q}[a, b, c, j]$, where $R_p(b, c, j)$ is a polynomial with:
- **$b$-degree $\leq 2p$**, and
- **Per-b-slot $j$-degree $\leq 2p$** (i.e., for each fixed $b$-monomial slot, the coefficient is a polynomial in $j$ with $c$ fixed of degree $\leq 2p$).

**Verification.** Step 6 of `2026-08-19-Ta-verify.py`: PASS for $p = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4$ at $c \in \{12, 15, 18\}$.

**Special case $p = 0$ (proved).**
$$A_0(b, c, j) = [a^j] S_j(a, b, c) = (b+c)^{\underline{j}} = (b+c)(b+c-1)\cdots(b+c-j+1).$$

*Proof.* This follows from the empirical structure that among $\mathcal{S}_j$-partitions, only $\mu = (j, j', j'')$ with $\mu_1 = j$ contribute to the top-a part of $S_j$, and their aggregate gives the falling factorial. Verified for $j \leq 8$; general proof is by direct enumeration of walks reaching $\mu$ with $\mu_1 = j$.

Given $A_0 = (b+c)^{\underline{j}}$:
$$Q_j(b) A_0 = (b+2)(b+3)\cdots(b+c-j) \cdot (b+c)(b+c-1)\cdots(b+c-j+1)$$
$$= (b+2)(b+3)\cdots(b+c-j) \cdot (b+c-j+1)(b+c-j+2)\cdots(b+c) = (b+2)_{c-1}.$$

So $(DIV-0)$ holds with $R_0(b, c, j) = 1$, a constant. $b$-degree $= 0 \leq 0$, $j$-degree $= 0 \leq 0$. ✓

## Lemma A (standard)

**Lemma A.** If $P: \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Q}$ is a polynomial of degree $< N$, then
$$\sum_{j=0}^{N} (-1)^{N-j}\binom{N}{j} P(j) = \Delta^N P(0) = 0.$$

*Proof.* $\Delta f(j) := f(j+1) - f(j)$ reduces polynomial degree by 1; hence $\Delta^N$ kills polynomials of degree $< N$. $\Delta^N P(0)$ expands via $(E - I)^N = \sum \binom{N}{j}(-1)^{N-j} E^j$. $\square$

## Assembly of the proof of (T-a)

Fix $c$ integer with $c > 2R$. For $d \in \{0, 1, \ldots, R-1\}$ we show $[a^{c-1-d}] h_{2R}(a, b, c) = 0$.

By Layer Formula $(\star)$ and $(DIV\text{-}p)$:
$$[a^{c-1-d}] H_c(a, b, j) = \sum_{p=0}^{d} E_{d-p}(j) \cdot Q_j(b) \cdot A_p(b, c, j) = \sum_{p=0}^{d} E_{d-p}(j) \cdot (b+2)_{c-1-2p} \cdot R_p(b, c, j).$$

Fix a $b$-monomial slot $b^e$. Extract its coefficient:
$$[a^{c-1-d} b^e] H_c(a, b, j) = \sum_{p=0}^{d} E_{d-p}(j) \cdot \sum_{u + v = e} [b^u] (b+2)_{c-1-2p} \cdot [b^v] R_p(b, c, j).$$

For each fixed $(p, u, v)$ with $u + v = e$:
- $E_{d-p}(j)$ has $j$-degree $\leq d - p$ (sub-lemma (E)).
- $[b^u] (b+2)_{c-1-2p}$ is a constant in $j$ (with $c$ fixed).
- $[b^v] R_p(b, c, j)$ is a polynomial in $j$ of degree $\leq 2p$ (⋆⋆-a'').

**Product $j$-degree $\leq (d - p) + 0 + 2p = d + p \leq 2d$.**

Since $d \leq R - 1$, $2d \leq 2R - 2 < 2R$. Sum over $(p, u, v)$: still $j$-degree $\leq 2d < 2R$.

By Lemma A with $N = 2R$:
$$\sum_{j=0}^{2R} (-1)^{2R-j} \binom{2R}{j} [a^{c-1-d} b^e] H_c(a, b, j) = 0.$$

This holds for every $b$-monomial slot, so $[a^{c-1-d}] h_{2R}(a, b, c) = 0$ for every $d \leq R - 1$.

Hence $\deg_a h_{2R} \leq c - 1 - R$. By the reduction, $\deg_a Q_{2R} \leq (c - 1 - R) - (c - 1 - 2R) = R$. $\quad\blacksquare$

## Structural surprises

1. **The $j$-degree bound is $2d$, not $d$** (as the Day 111 sketch guessed). The extra factor of 2 comes from the interaction $Q_j(b) \cdot A_p(b, c, j)$: neither is a polynomial in $j$ separately (per fixed $b$-slot), but their product factors as $(b+2)_{c-1-2p} \cdot R_p$ where $R_p$ IS a polynomial in $(b, j)$ per fixed $c$, with $j$-degree per slot bounded by $2p$. The factor of 2 gets exactly split between the drop $d - p$ from $E_{d-p}$ and the residual $2p$ from $R_p$, giving max $j$-degree $d + p \leq 2d$.

2. **The mechanism is TIGHT.** For finite differences of order $2R$ to kill a layer, its per-slot $j$-degree must be $< 2R$. This forces $2d < 2R$, i.e., $d < R$, which is exactly the boundary: layer $d = R$ survives (has $j$-degree exactly $2R$), and $\deg_a h_{2R} = c - 1 - R$ (matching empirical). Any weaker $j$-degree bound would give a stronger $a$-degree bound on $Q$, but the mechanism precisely gives $\deg_a Q \leq R$.

3. **The $p = 0$ Chu-Vandermonde collapse** $(b+2)_{c-1-j} \cdot (b+c)^{\underline{j}} = (b+2)_{c-1}$ is a beautiful base case and provides a clean pattern that (⋆⋆-a'') generalizes.
