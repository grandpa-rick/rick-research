                                               1     Introduction
                                               Quiver varieties, introduced by Nakajima in [Nak94], are a cornerstone of the geometric
                                               representation theory of Kac–Moody algebras. Given a symmetric Kac–Moody algebra g,
                                               one obtains a quiver by adding an orientation to its Dynkin diagram. Nakajima showed
                                               that highest-weight irreducible representations of g can be reconstructed from top-degree
                                               homology groups of so-called nilpotent quiver varieties associated to that quiver [Nak98].
                                               The Kashiwara crystals [Kas91] of these irreducible representations were subsequently
                                               constructed by Saito from irreducible components of the same nilpotent quiver varieties
                                               [Sai02]. These results were further generalised to symmetric Borcherds and Borcherds-
                                               Bozec algebras in [KKS12; Boz16].
                                                   These results are now understood as part of several interrelated geometric constructions
                                               of Kac–Moody algebras. Nilpotent quiver varieties are linked to Ringel-Hall algebras
                                               of quivers via a microlocal construction [Rin90; Lus91; KS97; Hen24; FL25], and the
                                               action of g on their homology extends to an action of a BPS Lie algebra built from the
                                               preprojective cohomological Hall algebra of the quiver [SV13a; YZ18; Dav25; DHS23].
                                               Poincaré polynomials of (nilpotent) quiver varieties are also related to Kac polynomials

    In this paper, we are concerned with geometric realisations of representations of a
symmetrisable Kac–Moody algebra g. Some of the above constructions have been gen-
eralised to the symmetrisable case over the past decade. In order to account for Cartan
data with non-trivial symmetriser, one should consider quivers with additional structure.
Geiss–Leclerc–Schröer obtained a realisation of the enveloping algebra of g by generalising
the Ringel–Hall approach to quivers with multiplicities [GLS16]. Moreover, the construc-
tion of the Kashiwara crystal attached to the quantised universal enveloping algebra of g
[KS97] was extended to the symmetrisable case in several works, using graphs with au-
tomorphisms [Sav05], species [NT18] and quivers with multiplicities [GLS18]. Geometric
realisations of the Yangian and of the (shifted) quantum loop group associated to g were
also provided by Yang–Zhao, Varagnolo–Vasserot and Cao–Okounkov–Zhou–Zhou using
quivers with potential [YZ22; VV23; Cao+26]. These quivers with potential appear in
[HL16; Neg25] in a related context.
    Following Geiss–Leclerc–Schröer’s approach [GLS18], we give a geometric construction
of the crystals of irreducible highest-weight representations of any symmetrisable Kac–
Moody algebra g (see Theorem B below). This builds on the results of Geiss–Leclerc–
Schröer [GLS18] in a similar way to how Saito’s construction [Sai02] expands on his earlier
work with Kashiwara [KS97].
    To carry out our construction, we introduce analogues of Nakajima’s nilpotent quiver
varieties [Nak94] for quivers with multiplicities (see Theorem A below). These quiver
varieties are constructed in the spirit of Geometric Invariant Theory (GIT), as quotients of
stable loci in the representation spaces of a framed quiver with multiplicities associated to
g and a fixed highest weight by a non-reductive group. We identify a stability condition on
quiver representations with multiplicities, which allows us to generalise Saito’s construction
[Sai02] to the symmetrisable case. In our previous work on moduli of quiver representations
with multiplicities [HJV25], we introduced new stability conditions and constructed moduli
spaces of quiver representations with multiplicities in a general setup, using tools from non-
reductive GIT [HHJ]. However, these stability conditions coming from GIT do not fit our
needs for the construction of crystals, so we instead define a new notion of stability and
recursively construct nilpotent quiver varieties using slices. Our construction of slices relies
on so-called Hecke correspondences (see below), which parametrise Hecke modifications of
quiver representations at a vertex and induce Hecke (or crystal) operators on the set of
irreducible components of nilpotent quiver varieties.

    We now explain our results concerning geometric realisations of crystals. One can
attach to (Q, m) a symmetrisable Cartan matrix C and the corresponding Kac–Moody
algebra g. Let λ be the integral dominant weight whose coordinates in the basis of funda-
mental weights are given by f . Then there is an irreducible module L(λ) of highest weight
λ over the quantised universal enveloping algebra Uq (g). Let B(C; λ) be the Kashiwara
crystal attached to L(λ) [Kas91]; this can be viewed as a combinatorial skeleton of L(λ).
Its elements correspond to the vectors of the crystal basis of L(λ) [Kas91; Lus91; GL93].

we obtain a morphism of crystals relating Bfs and the geometric realisation of the crys-

homomorphism Uq− (g) ↠ L(λ). The latter statement is proved in Section 5.
    Since the irreducible components of nilpotent quiver varieties with multiplicities match
the crystals of the representations L(λ), it is natural to ask whether one can construct
the representations themselves from the Borel–Moore homology of quiver varieties in the
symmetrisable case, as Nakajima did in the symmetric case [Nak98]. Some difficulties arise
in the case of quivers with multiplicities due to the lack of a convenient proper pushforward
map in homology. We plan to return to this question in future works.

Relation to other works Varagnolo–Vasserot and Cao–Okounkov–Zhou–Zhou con-
struct certain representations of non-symmetric (shifted) quantum loop groups and Yan-
gians from critical quiver varieties attached to a tripled quiver with potential and a stability
condition from reductive GIT [VV23; Cao+26]. Cyclic derivatives of this potential yield
the defining equations of our nilpotent quiver varieties, as noted in [GLS17]. However,

                                               3
there seems to be no straightforward relation between our quiver varieties (nor between
the topological invariants we study). Indeed, these authors use critical K-theory (or co-
homology), whereas our approach is related to usual (top-degree) Borel–Moore homology
and those do not relate by dimensional reduction in the case of quivers with multiplicities
(see [Dav17; Păd22; VV22] for comparison in the case of quivers without multiplicities).
Moreover, working with locally free representations amounts to prescribing certain Jordan
types for loop arrows in the triple quiver from [VV23; Cao+26], which seems at odds with
usual GIT techniques.
    Let us also mention that there is an analogue of nilpotent quiver varieties in the set-
ting of Higgs bundles on a smooth projective curve: the global nilpotent cone [Lau88].
Irreducible components of the global nilpotent cone and Hecke operators also play an im-
portant rôle in that setting: for instance in the study of CoHAs of Higgs sheaves [Min20;
SS20] and in mirror symmetry [HH22]. Spaces of Hecke modifications can be viewed as
non-reductive quotients in that setup as well, as in the forthcoming thesis [Mar26]. In
this work, the author studies the nilpotent cone in moduli spaces of Higgs bundles, by
considering Hecke modifications of rank two vector bundles on a smooth projective curve,
along a suitably chosen non-reduced divisor.

Notation and conventions Throughout the paper, we work over an algebraically
closed base field k of arbitrary characteristic. Given a scheme X, we call Irr(X) the set of
irreducible components of X. Given an action of an algebraic group G on a scheme X, we
say that X admits a Zariski-locally trivial G-quotient if it admits a categorical quotient (in
the category of schemes) such that the quotient map is a Zariski-locally trivial principal
G-bundle. General definitions in Sections 2.1-2.2 are given for arbitrary quivers Q. From
Section 2.4 on, we always assume that Q has no loop arrows.

4     Prerequisites on crystals
In this section, we collect prerequiste results concerning crystals and their geometric re-
alisations [KS97; Sai02; GLS18]. In particular, we adapt Saito’s strategy for constructing
crystals of irreducible highest-weight representations of Kac–Moody algebras [Sai02] to the
setting of quivers with multiplicities. Recall that we assume Q has no loop arrows.

4.1     Kashiwara crystals
