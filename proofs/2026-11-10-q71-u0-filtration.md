## Conventions

The conventions are those of `wake287/u0filt/RESULTS.md`.

- V = Q^n with letters 0 < 1 < … < n−1. Write V^{⊗N} for the span of the words w = w_1…w_N.
- H = H_N(0) acts on the right (Jung–Oh 2509.26194 (4.5)). With a = w_i and b = w_{i+1}, the generator π̄_i acts on w by: swap if a < b; −w if a > b; 0 if a = b.
- U_0(gl_n) acts by Jung–Oh Def 4.2, with Δe_i = 1⊗e_i + e_i⊗k_i, Δf_i = k_{i+1}⊗f_i + f_i⊗1, Δk_i = k_i⊗k_i. On words this gives:
  - **k_i** multiplies w by [letter i does not occur in w].
  - **f_i** sends w to the sum over positions p with w_p = i and no letter i+1 at any position < p. Each term is w with w_p replaced by i+1.
  - **e_i** sends w to the sum over positions p with w_p = i+1 and no letter i at any position > p. Each term is w with w_p replaced by i.
- A := ρ(U_0) ⊆ End_H(V^{⊗N}). Here A-stable means stable under all e_i, f_i, k_i.

## Theorem

Let n ≥ 2 and N ≥ 3. There is **no** chain 0 = X_0 ⊂ X_1 ⊂ … ⊂ X_m = V^{⊗N} of A-stable H-submodules such that each X_k/X_{k−1} is H-isomorphic to P_k^{⊕d_k}, for a single indecomposable projective P_k.

**Corollary.** V^{⊗N} has no filtration by (U_0(gl_n), H_N(0))-sub-bimodules with layers L_I ⊠ M_I. (As an H-module, L ⊠ M_I ≅ M_I^{⊕ dim L}.) Together with PROVE 287 (⇐), the U_0-version of Q71 holds in the same form as the S_0-version: such a filtration exists iff N ≤ 2.

The S_0-version (PROVE 287) does not imply this one. A ⊊ S_0 in general (19 < 20 at (2,3)), so A-stable submodules form a larger lattice.

## Proof

Throughout, let **S = {0,1}**. Let V′ ⊂ V^{⊗N} be the span of words whose set of letters is **exactly** {0,1}. For 1 ≤ j ≤ N−1, let V_j ⊂ V′ be the span of words with j letters 1 and N−j letters 0. Then V′ = ⊕_{j=1}^{N−1} V_j, and every V_j is an H-submodule, because H preserves letter content.

We reuse the notation of PROVE 287, Lemma 2:
- u_q is the word with a single 1, at position q.
- v_q is the word with a single 0, at position q.
- W = span{u_1,…,u_{N−1}} ⊂ V_1, and V_1 = Q·Σu ⊕ W.
- W′ = span{v_2,…,v_N} ⊂ V_{N−1}, and V_{N−1} = Q·Σv ⊕ W′.

The following facts are from Lemma 2:
- W and W′ are local, generated by u_{N−1} and v_2 respectively.
- top W = C_{\{N−1\}} and top W′ = C_{\{1\}}. These are distinct since N ≥ 3, so W ≇ W′.
- u_1 spans a simple submodule of type C_{\{1\}}. v_N spans a simple submodule of type C_{\{N−1\}}.

**Step 0 (A contains the support projector).**
- p := (1−k_0)(1−k_1)·∏_{i≥2} k_i ∈ A. It acts on a word w by [0 ∈ w][1 ∈ w][no letter ≥ 2 in w], so p is the projector onto V′ along the other words.
- f := p f_0 p ∈ A and e := p e_0 p ∈ A.
- On {0,1}-words, k_1 and k_0 detect only the letters 1 and 0. So f and e act on V′ by the gl_2 rules above, followed by deletion of any term that is all-0 or all-1.

**Step 1 (restrict the chain to V′).** Suppose such a chain exists, and put X′_k := pX_k.
- Since p ∈ A, we get X′_k = X_k ∩ V′. One inclusion is pX_k ⊆ X_k ∩ V′. The other: if x ∈ X_k ∩ V′, then x = px.
- Each X′_k is an H-submodule (p commutes with H) and is stable under f and e.
- p acts on the H-module X_k/X_{k−1} ≅ P_k^{d_k} as an idempotent H-endomorphism. So X′_k/X′_{k−1} = p(X_k/X_{k−1}) is an H-direct summand of P_k^{d_k}. By Krull–Schmidt it is ≅ P_k^{d′_k} for some d′_k ≥ 0.

So (X′_k) is a chain of H-submodules of V′ = X′_m. It is stable under f and e, and its layers are isotypic projective.

Consequences:
- The layers are projective, so each V′/X′_k is projective, and each inclusion X′_k ⊂ V′ splits over H.
- Up to isomorphism, V′ ≅ ⊕_k P_k^{d′_k}.
- W and W′ are indecomposable H-direct summands of V′, because each V_j is a summand of V′. By Krull–Schmidt both types occur among the P_k with d′_k > 0.

**Step 2 (first hook layer).** Let k be minimal such that X′_k has a direct summand isomorphic to W or to W′. Then X′_k ≅ ⊕_{l≤k} P_l^{d′_l}. No P_l with l < k is ≅ W or W′, and P_k is ≅ exactly one of W, W′. There are two cases:
- **Case A:** X′_k has a summand ≅ W and none ≅ W′.
- **Case B:** X′_k has a summand ≅ W′ and none ≅ W.

Write X := X′_k.

**Step 3 (three small facts).**

(i) **End_H W = Q·id and End_H W′ = Q·id.**
- An endomorphism φ of W is fixed by y = φ(u_{N−1}) = Σ_{q≤N−1} c_q u_q.
- y must satisfy yπ̄_{N−1} = −y. But u_qπ̄_{N−1} = 0 for q ≤ N−2 (letters 0,0 at positions N−1, N), and u_{N−1}π̄_{N−1} = −u_{N−1}. Hence y = c_{N−1}u_{N−1}.
- For W′: y = φ(v_2) must satisfy yπ̄_1 = −y. Since v_qπ̄_1 = 0 for q ≥ 3, we get y ∈ Q·v_2.

(ii) **Hom_H(V_j, C_{\{N−1\}}) = 0 for j ≥ 2, and Hom_H(V_j, C_{\{1\}}) = 0 for j ≤ N−2.**
- V_j is generated as an H-module by the sorted word s = 0^{N−j}1^j: every word of that content arises from s by successive swaps of increasing adjacent pairs.
- If j ≥ 2, then sπ̄_{N−1} = 0 (letters 1,1). A map φ to C_{\{N−1\}} therefore has −φ(s) = φ(s)π̄_{N−1} = φ(sπ̄_{N−1}) = 0, so φ = 0.
- Likewise, if N−j ≥ 2 then sπ̄_1 = 0, which kills maps to C_{\{1\}}.
- Also Hom_H(W, Q·Σu) = 0 and Hom_H(W′, Q·Σv) = 0. The π̄_i act by 0 on Σu and on Σv, whereas u_{N−1}π̄_{N−1} = −u_{N−1} and v_2π̄_1 = −v_2.

(iii) **Two operator identities in A (proved by hand; machine-checked in chk.py).**
- (iii-A) f^{N−2}u_{N−1} = v_N. Moreover f^{N−2} vanishes on every V_j with j ≥ 2.
  - f_0 may only turn a 0 into a 1 strictly left of the leftmost 1. Starting from u_{N−1}, the N−2 flips must therefore hit positions N−2, N−3, …, 1, in that order. This is one path, with coefficient 1, and position N is never flippable.
  - On V_j with j ≥ 2, f raises j by one per step and reaches j+N−2 ≥ N. That is the all-1 word, which p deletes.
- (iii-B) e^{N−2}v_2 = u_1. Moreover e^{N−2} vanishes on every V_j with j ≤ N−2.
  - e_0 may only turn a 1 into a 0 strictly right of the rightmost 0. Starting from v_2, the forced path flips positions 3, 4, …, N.
  - On V_j with j ≤ N−2, e reaches j − (N−2) ≤ 0, which is the all-0 word, deleted by p.

**Step 4 (Case A).**
1. Let θ: W → X be a split H-embedding, and write θ = Σ_j θ_j with θ_j: W → V_j.
2. **θ_1 = c·ι_W with c ≠ 0.**
   - By (ii), θ_1 has zero component in Q·Σu. So θ_1 ∈ End_H W = Q·id by (i).
   - Suppose c = 0. Then θ(W) ⊂ ⊕_{j≥2} V_j, and θ(W) is a direct summand of V′. Hence θ(W) is also a summand of ⊕_{j≥2} V_j, which therefore surjects onto W and then onto top W = C_{\{N−1\}}.
   - That contradicts (ii).
3. Put x := θ(u_{N−1}) ∈ X. By (iii-A), f^{N−2}x = f^{N−2}θ_1(u_{N−1}) = c·v_N. Since f ∈ A and X is f-stable, **v_N ∈ X**.
4. Let p_X: V′ → X be an H-projection; it exists because X is a summand of V′ by Step 1. Let π_{W′}: V′ → W′ be the H-projection onto W′, along the other V_j and along Q·Σv.
5. Then ψ := π_{W′}∘p_X|_{W′} ∈ End_H W′ = Q·id. Also ψ(v_N) = π_{W′}(v_N) = v_N, because v_N ∈ X and v_N ∈ W′. So ψ = id_{W′}.
6. Hence W′ → X → W′ composes to the identity, and W′ is isomorphic to a direct summand of X. This contradicts Case A, by Krull–Schmidt.

**Step 5 (Case B).** This is symmetric to Case A.
1. Take a split embedding θ: W′ → X. Its component θ_{N−1} ∈ End_H W′ is c·id with c ≠ 0. The argument is the same as in Step 4, using (ii) with C_{\{1\}} and the summands V_j, j ≤ N−2.
2. Then e^{N−2}θ(v_2) = c·u_1 by (iii-B), so **u_1 ∈ X**.
3. Next, π_W∘p_X|_W ∈ End_H W = Q·id fixes u_1, so it is the identity. Hence W is a direct summand of X, which contradicts Case B. ∎

## Remarks

1. **What A must contain.** The proof uses only k_0, …, k_{n−1}, e_0 and f_0. The k's are used only through the support projector p. This matches the Wake 287 controls: if f_0 or e_0 is dropped, chains do appear. f_0 kills Case A and e_0 kills Case B.
2. **Relation to PROVE 287.** The argument mirrors Lemma 1 with one change. Lemma 1 needed the weight projection inside the acting algebra to turn f, g ∈ Hom_H into elements of S. Here A contains elements whose restrictions to the hooks *are* f and g:
   - f^{N−2}|_W = PROVE 287's f (u_{N−1} ↦ v_N, u_q ↦ 0 for q < N−1);
   - e^{N−2}|_{W′} = PROVE 287's g (v_2 ↦ u_1).

   The missing weight idempotent is replaced by the observation that these operators kill all *other* weight components of a top generator. The contradiction is read off on socles (v_N or u_1 ∈ X) instead of through a projection lying in A.
3. **The reduction to S = {0,1}** is legitimate because p ∈ A. The assumption flagged in PROVE.md, that the k_i do not separate weights, is true but harmless. The k_i separate *supports*, and the two hooks are separated from each other by the e/f degree count, not by k.
4. **The two U_0 conventions.** Only the shape of f_0 and e_0 on {0,1}-words is used ("flip left of the leftmost 1" and "flip right of the rightmost 0"). Another coproduct convention, e.g. one with mirrored k-factors, flips in the mirrored region. The same argument should then go through with the roles of u_1/u_{N−1} and v_2/v_N exchanged. I have not checked this; it is recorded and not claimed.

## Verification

`proofs/prove288/chk.py` uses exact integer matrices and the same operator code as the Wake 287 search. For n = 2 with N = 3…7, and for n = 3 with N = 3…5, it checks:
- p is the support-{0,1} projector;
- f^{N−2}u_{N−1} = v_N, and f^{N−2} kills u_q (q < N−1) and every {0,1}-word with ≥ 2 ones;
- e^{N−2}v_2 = u_1, and e^{N−2} kills v_q (q ≥ 3) and every {0,1}-word with ≤ N−2 ones;
- f^{N−2} and e^{N−2} commute with ρ(H).

All checks pass. The theorem agrees with the Wake 287 exact search, which found NO at (2,3), (2,4), (2,5), and with the (3,3) case (A = S_0).
