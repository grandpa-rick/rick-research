## 1. Problem

In W = D_8, decide for each of the 8 reducible constructible characters violating (★) whether a left cell of that character contains a dominated twin (x,z).

By Theorem W (proved, any finite W), (x,z) is a twin iff μ̃(y,z) ≥ μ̃(y,x) for all y ∈ W_i(x) ∪ W_ii(x). By C2 (simply laced), a twin is a candidate pair: ∅ ≠ L(z) ⊆ int L(x).

## 2. Method

**Step 1: upper bound U(x) ⊇ Γ(x), with no KL.**
- (G) Geck's induction theorem (Geck 2003, *Bull. LMS* 35, "On the induction of Kazhdan–Lusztig cells"; recalled, not re-read): for a parabolic W_I and a left cell Γ' of W_I, X_I·Γ' is a union of left cells of W.
  - So Γ(w) ⊆ {v : u_I(v) ~_L u_I(w) in W_I}, where w = x·u_I(w) with x minimal in wW_I.
  - Intersect this over all 8 maximal parabolics: D7, A7, A7, A1×A1×A5, and D_{k−1}×A_{8−k}.
  - The W_I cells are coxeter3's own native `lcells` dumps (D4–D7, A2–A7), in `prove286/lc/`.
- (S) Refine by right-star signatures, iterated (depth ≤ 40; stop after 5 stable rounds). This uses KL79 Thm 4.3: x ~_L y ⇒ x* ~_L y*. At every depth the class is still a union of left cells.
- **Validation gate (native, element-for-element).** No native cell was ever split by U, and the exactness rates are:

  | group | sample | U = cell exactly |
  |---|---|---|
  | D5 | all 126 cells | 126 |
  | D6 | all cells, full refinement | 558 / 578 |
  | D6 | 60 random cells | 58 / 60 |
  | D7 | 40 random cells | **40 / 40** |

  In D6 the non-exact classes merge cells of *different* sizes, e.g. 80 = 50 + 30.

**Step 2: certify that U is a single cell.**
- If |U| = dim χ_K and the left-descent distribution of U equals dist_K, then U is a single left cell of character K, provided dist_K is not a sum of ≥ 2 constructible distributions. This is checked by DFS for all 8 targets: none is decomposable.
- The left-descent distribution determines the constructible character: all 80 D8 constructible dists are pairwise distinct (computed).
- Sources: C3 (proved, PROVE 285) and P1 (Lusztig, recall, validated).

**Step 3: decide candidate pairs in U.**
- Enumerate all (x,z) in U with ∅ ≠ L(z) ⊆ int L(x).
- **Parity kill.** If l(x) ≢ l(z), Cor W2 applies. Concretely, take the F2 star y = rx: then l(y) − l(z) is even, so μ̃(y,z) = 0. This y is in W_i(x) by Thm W(d).
- **Witness kill.** Otherwise, take y = rx with L(y) and L(x) incomparable. By (F2), y ∈ Γ(x), and y ∈ W_i(x). If μ̃(y,z) = 0, the pair dies.
- μ comes from the coxeter3 `pol` command in a persistent session.
- No pair ever needed more than this, so nothing about Γ beyond U ⊇ Γ and F2 is used for the NO verdicts.

**Step 4: transport (§4).**
- Right stars send left cells to left cells by W-graph isomorphisms (KL79 Thm 4.3; record Lemma S, right version). These preserve L, μ̃ and l(x) − l(z) mod 2 (each length changes by ±1).
- σ, the 1↔2 diagram automorphism, preserves Bruhat order, P_{x,y} and cells, and maps L to σ(L). The candidate condition is σ-invariant, and σ fixes every non-degenerate character.
- So the verdict is constant on ⟨right stars, σ⟩-orbits of cells. The orbits are computed elementwise, with no KL.
