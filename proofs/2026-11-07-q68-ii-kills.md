## Problem

A *candidate pair* (x, z) consists of x, z in one left cell with L(x) = A and ∅ ≠ L(z) ⊆ int A.
Here int A = {s ∈ A : every Dynkin neighbour of s lies in A}; the ends of the A_{n−1} diagram count as interior.
Equivalently, the closed neighbourhood N[L(z)] is contained in L(x).
A *(ii)-element* for (x, z) is a y in the cell with L(y) ⊋ A and μ(y, x) ≠ 0.

**Conjecture Q68-ii-kills.** For every candidate pair (x, z) and every (ii)-element y, μ(y, z) = 0.

## Answer

The conjecture is **not proved in general**. What is proved:

1. **Theorem 1.** The conjecture holds for every parity-failing pair (l(x) ≢ l(z)). The proof is one line.
2. **Theorem 2.** For parity-OK pairs, the conjecture follows from an **x-free lemma**:
   - **Lemma I:** μ(x, z) = 0 for every *parity-failing* candidate pair.
   - The (ii)-instance (y, z) is itself such a pair, at A′ = L(y). The Wake-282 "tautology" (monotonicity of int) is exactly the reduction step.
3. **A Young-layer toolkit, proved modulo the usual recall-only KL basics:**
   - the Layer Lemma P_{v′u,vu} = P_{v′,v};
   - the Deletion Proposition (n in the same row ⇒ μ_λ(T, T′) = μ_{λ−□}(T∖n, T′∖n));
   - the Corner Kill, and their evacuation mirrors.
4. **Application (certificate-proved, replayed by fresh code).** The 5 UNDECIDED S_16 pairs of shape (5,5,3,3) are all **NO**.
   - The S_16 census is now **112 TWIN / 296 NO / 2 UNDECIDED**.
   - Both remaining pairs are (6,4,3,3), with A = {2,3,4,6,7,8,12,13,14} and {2,3,4,8,9,10,12,13,14}.
   - Of the 7 (ii)-instances that were undecided, 3 are now μ = 0. The other 4 belong to the two (6,4,3,3) pairs.
5. **Evidence for Lemma I (computed). Every decided instance is 0, but the undecided set is real.**
   - S_15: 20/20 parity-failing pairs proved μ = 0, both by the toolkit and by star certificates.
   - S_16: the toolkit proves 160/186 (§5).
   - The other **26 S_16 pairs are undecided**. 11 of them were checked by star search: the orbit is exhausted, so y < z in every reachable cell. The other 15 are pending.
   - These 26 are exactly the *primitive* configurations of §6. A counterexample, if one exists, lives there.
   - Lemma I has **no small-rank test**. It is vacuous in A_{n≤12}, D_4 and D_5. A_5, B_4 and H_3 have no interior configurations at all. F_4 has 32, all with even gap.

The obstruction to a general proof is stated in §6.

## 1. The parity half

**Theorem 1.** Let (x, z) be a candidate pair with l(x) ≢ l(z) (mod 2), and let y be a (ii)-element. Then μ(y, z) = 0.

*Proof.* μ(y, x) ≠ 0 forces l(y) − l(x) to be odd. Hence l(y) ≡ l(z), and μ vanishes on pairs at even length distance. ∎

So the conjecture has content only for parity-OK (x, z). In the data that is 2 instances at n = 15 and 25 at n = 16, out of the 58 total.

## 2. Reduction to an x-free lemma

**Lemma I** (conjecture). Let x, z lie in one left cell of a simply-laced W, with ∅ ≠ L(z), N[L(z)] ⊆ L(x) and L(x) ≠ S. Then μ(x, z) = 0.
In type A this says that μ(x, z) = 0 for every candidate pair. By parity the content is the parity-failing pairs.

**Theorem 2.** Lemma I restricted to parity-failing candidate pairs implies Q68-ii-kills.

*Proof.* Theorem 1 handles parity-failing (x, z). Now let (x, z) be parity-OK and y a (ii)-element.
- int is monotone: if B ⊆ B′ and s ∈ int B, then s ∈ B′ and N(s) ⊆ B ⊆ B′. So L(z) ⊆ int A ⊆ int L(y), and (y, z) is a candidate pair at A′ = L(y).
- l(y) ≢ l(x) ≡ l(z), so (y, z) is parity-failing.
- Lemma I then gives μ(y, z) = 0. ∎

Remarks.
- (a) **Lemma I is strictly stronger.** It needs no x. It is also *not* a statement about twins: for a twin (parity-OK), μ(x, z) = 0 is automatic.
- (b) **Which way the edge points.** Suppose μ(x, z) ≠ 0 in the situation of Lemma I. Then |L(x)∖L(z)| ≥ 2 forces x < z. In all 206 data pairs, |L(x)∖L(z)| ≥ 2.
  - If z < x, then every t ∈ L(x)∖L(z) gives z = tx by (F1). Two distinct t's contradict each other.
  - So any such edge runs *upward* from the element with more descents.
- (c) **A correction to my own scratch reasoning.** I first argued that a same-cell t·x can never have L(tx) ⊋ L(x), "because dual-Knuth moves drop a neighbouring descent". That is **false**.
  - Q(tx) = Q(x) does not require the swap to be an *elementary* dual-Knuth move.
  - S_15 instance: x has P_x = [[1,2,3,7,11],[4,8,12,15],[5,9,13],[6,10,14]], and y = s_1x is in the cell with L(y) = A ∪ {1}. The values 1 and 2 are not separated by 3.
  - So the l(y) = l(x)+1 (ii)-instances are genuine. They are forced by (F1) to be y = tx with |L(y)∖A| = 1.

### Why rank-2 W-graph axioms cannot see the edge (computed by hand, §6 uses it)

Take m(s,t) = 3 and expand T_sT_tT_s = T_tT_sT_t on a W-graph basis vector e_u, reading off the coefficient of e_w.
- **u of type ∅, w of type {s,t}.** μ(u, w) cancels. What remains is the polygon identities
  Σ_{v:{s}} μ(u,v)μ(v,w) = Σ_{v:{t}} μ(u,v)μ(v,w), plus the analogous 3-path identity.
- **u of type {s}, w of type {s,t}.** We get μ(u, w) = Σ_{v:{t}, v′:{s}} μ(u,v)μ(v,v′)μ(v′,w). By Stembridge's bonding rule (C1 Claim), the only terms are v = *u and v′ = u, so this collapses to μ(u, w) = μ(u, w).
- **u of type ∅, w of type {s}.** Same collapse.

So no rank-2 relation at the pair (z, x) constrains a nested edge. Lemma I needs global KL input (Bruhat order, lengths, parabolic structure). That is what §3 supplies.

## 3. The Young-layer toolkit

**Layer Lemma.** Let W be a Coxeter group, K ⊆ S, and ^K W the minimal representatives of W_K∖W. For u ∈ ^K W and v, v′ ∈ W_K,
P_{v′u, vu} = P_{v′, v}. In particular μ(v′u, vu) = μ_{W_K}(v′, v), and v′u ≤ vu ⟺ v′ ≤ v.

*Proof.* Induction on l(v). Inputs:
- (a) l(vu) = l(v) + l(u).
- (b) The projection w ↦ w^K (minimal representative of W_K w) is Bruhat order-preserving. This is Deodhar; Björner–Brenti Prop. 2.5.1, textbook.
- (c) The KL recursion. For s ∈ L(w) and w₁ = sw:
  P_{x,w} = q^{1−c}P_{sx,w₁} + q^c P_{x,w₁} − Σ_{z: sz<z} μ(z,w₁) q^{(l(w)−l(z))/2} P_{x,z}, with c = [sx < x].
  This holds for *all* x, with P_{x,w} := 0 when x ≰ w (KL79 (2.2.c); recall-only, same status as (F1)).

The steps:
1. *Base, v = e.* If v′u ≤ u then l(v′) = 0, so P_{v′u,u} = δ_{v′,e}.
2. *Inductive step.* Pick s ∈ L(v) ⊆ K. Then s ∈ L(vu) and w₁ = (sv)u is in the same layer. Put x = v′u.
   - sx = (sv′)u is in the layer, and by (a), c = [sv′ < v′].
   - A term z of the sum is nonzero only if x ≤ z ≤ w₁. By (b), u = x^K ≤ z^K ≤ w₁^K = u, so z = v″u is in the layer, with sz < z ⟺ sv″ < v″.
   - Lengths differ by l(u) uniformly.
3. By induction the right-hand side is term by term the W_K-recursion for P_{v′,v}. ∎

The right-handed version P_{uv′, uv} = P_{v′,v} (u minimal in uW_K) follows from P_{a,b} = P_{a⁻¹,b⁻¹}.

**Product rule.** Let W_K = W₁ × W₂ with the factors commuting. Then P_{(a,b),(a′,b′)} = P_{a,a′}P_{b,b′}. If a ≠ a′ and b ≠ b′, the degree is ≤ (Δl − 2)/2, so **μ = 0**.
If one coordinate agrees, μ is the μ of the other factor.

**Type-A dictionary.** Take K = S∖{s_k}, so W_K = S_k × S_{n−k}.
- For the left action, the layer of w is the set of *positions* of the values ≤ k.
- For the right action (via inverses), the layer is the set of *values* in positions ≤ k.
- k = n−1, left: the layer is the position of n, and v = w∖n.
- k = 1, left: the layer is the position of 1, and v = w∖1 shifted down.

Textbook RSK facts used:
- (R1) P(w∖n) = P(w)∖n.
- (R2) RSK(w⁻¹) = (Q(w), P(w)) (Schützenberger symmetry).
- (R3) RSK(w₀ww₀) = (evac P, evac Q).

All three were checked exhaustively for n ≤ 8 (46232 permutations, `rskfacts.py`). Also:
- conjugation by w₀ is a Bruhat automorphism preserving μ and left cells, with L ↦ n − L;
- for corners c of a tableau T, ρ_T(c) denotes the letter ejected from row 1 by reverse row-bumping T at c.

**Proposition D (deletion).** Let T, T′ ∈ SYT(λ), with n in the same row of both. Then μ_λ(T, T′) = μ_{λ∖□}(T∖n, T′∖n).
Here μ_λ(T, T′) means μ(y, z) for y = (T, Q), z = (T′, Q) in any common left cell.

*Proof.*
1. n sits in the same box c of T and T′. By (R2), the position of n in y is y⁻¹(n), the last letter of RSK⁻¹(Q, T). That letter is obtained by reverse-bumping Q at the box of n in T, i.e. at c. The same holds for z, so y and z share the position of n.
2. The Layer Lemma (K = {s_1, …, s_{n−2}}) gives P_{y,z} = P_{y∖n, z∖n}.
3. By (R1), P(y∖n) = T∖n.
4. Q(y∖n) = P((y∖n)⁻¹), and (y∖n)⁻¹ is y⁻¹ with its last letter removed and the word standardised. Its insertion tableau is Q reverse-bumped at c, then standardised. This is the same for z, so y∖n and z∖n lie in one left cell. ∎

(R5, the same-cell claim, was checked exhaustively for n ≤ 8: 2618/2618 classes. **Full-KL control** (`deltest.py`): 1428 same-row pairs at S_6 (720 with μ ≠ 0) and 25676 at S_7 (7826 with μ ≠ 0), 0 mismatches.)

**Proposition K (corner kill).** Let T, T′ ∈ SYT(λ), and suppose μ_λ(T, T′) ≠ 0 forces (T, Q) < (T′, Q) in every cell Q. By Remark 2(b) this holds whenever Des T′ ⊆ Des T and |Des T ∖ Des T′| ≥ 2.
If some corner c of λ has ρ_T(c) < ρ_{T′}(c), then μ_λ(T, T′) = 0.

*Proof.*
1. Choose Q with n at c. The last letter of x = (T, Q) is x(n) = ρ_T(c), by inverse RSK.
2. Bruhat x ≤ z implies, by the tableau criterion at k = n−1, that sorted x[1..n−1] ≤ sorted z[1..n−1] componentwise.
3. For (n−1)-subsets of [n] that differ by their omitted element, this happens iff x(n) ≥ z(n).
4. So ρ_T(c) < ρ_{T′}(c) gives x ≰ z in that cell, contradicting the hypothesis. ∎

*Control of the mechanism* (`kbru.py`, all same-shape pairs, ≤ 60 SYT per shape): at n = 6, 7, 48794 triggered (T, T′, c, Q) gave 0 Bruhat violations, and x(n) = ρ_T(c) held every time.
A full-KL control is useless here: on nested pairs in S_6, Prop K never triggers (`ktest.py`: 0 triggers). That is a test that cannot separate, so it is not counted.

**Proposition R (right deletion).** If ρ_T(c) = ρ_{T′}(c), then μ_λ(T, T′) = μ_{λ∖c}(T↤c, T′↤c), after standardisation.
Here T↤c is T reverse-bumped at c.
- The layer is the last letter, so this is the right-handed Layer Lemma in cells with n at c.
- If μ ≠ 0 forces comparability in every cell of λ, the same holds in every cell of λ∖c, because every Q′ extends by n at c.

**Evacuation mirrors.** D, K and R also apply to (evac T, evac T′), by (R3). For example, D-mirror says: if T and T′ agree on the *delete-1 slide path end*, delete 1.

More generally, there is the **Young-layer kill**: in some cell, the layer of S_k × S_{n−k} agrees for y and z but both factors differ ⇒ μ = 0. This uses only the product rule; full-KL control checked 132120 layer pairs at S_6 and 5549040 at S_7, with 0 violations.

## 6. Where the proof stops (the enemy, named)

To prove Lemma I one must show that a nested, upward, same-cell edge x → z with N[L(z)] ⊆ L(x) never carries μ ≠ 0. What I have and don't have:
- **Rank-2 relations are tautological on that edge** (§2). Lemma I is not a consequence of Stembridge admissibility at the edge.
- **Layer methods.** μ is exactly a μ in a smaller group whenever some Young layer coincides (D, R, mirrors). It is 0 whenever comparability fails at a layer (K).
  These are complete for Bruhat-type kills: x ≤ z ⟺ all Grassmannian layers compare. But they say nothing when x < z holds in *every* cell and no layer ever coincides.
- The (6,4,3,3) instances are exactly such **primitive** pairs. Deciding them needs either an actual KL computation in S_16 at gap 19/23, or a new structural reason.

What a proof would need is an invariant that sees N[L(z)] ⊆ L(x) globally. Two candidates:
- a parabolic sign-projection (ε_K for K = N[L(z)]) estimate on e_z;
- an inductive version of Proposition D/R that *creates* a coinciding layer after a μ-preserving move.

Neither is in hand. Three genuine attempts died here: rank-2 polygons, canonical-cell Bruhat, and layers. Per protocol this goes to the collaborator note.
