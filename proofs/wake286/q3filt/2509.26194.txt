                                                  the 0-Schur algebra S0 (n, r) using its geometric realization. In this paper, the simple

                                                  S0 (n, r)-mod, and U0 (gln )-mod are examined, where Hr (0) denotes the 0-Hecke algebra
                                                  and U0 (gln ) denotes the degenerate quantum group.

                                      1. Introduction
  Let n and r be nonnegative integers, and let q be an indeterminate. In 1989, Dipper and
James [7] introduced the quantum Schur algebra Sq (n, r) as the endomorphism algebra
                                              Ñ                é
                                                M
                        Sq (n, r) = EndHr (q)         xλ Hr (q) ,
                                                  λ∈Λ(n,r)

where Hr (q) is the Hecke algebra of type A over Z[q] (see Subsection 2.2). It plays a
central role in the representation theory of Hecke algebras and quantum groups, being
closely connected with Schur–Weyl duality. The specialization of Sq (n, r) at q = 0 gives
rise to the 0-Schur algebra S0 (n, r).
   Beilinson, Lusztig, and MacPherson [2] constructed certain finite-dimensional quotients
of the quantized enveloping algebra Uq (gln ) using double flag varieties. Du [9] subsequently
proved that these quotients are isomorphic to the q-Schur algebras Sq (n, ·), thereby pro-
viding a geometric realization of Sq (n, r). In parallel, Jensen and Su [12] established a
geometric realization of the 0-Schur algebra S0 (n, r).
   Let S0 (n, r) := S0 (n, r) ⊗Z[q] C and H0 (r) := H0 (r) ⊗Z[q] C. Jensen, Su, and Yang [13]
employed the realization in [12] to construct its projective indecomposable S0 (n, r)- mod-
ules. More precisely, they provided a complete set {Pλ | λ ∈ Λ• (n, r)} of representatives
of the isomorphism classes of projective indecomposable S0 (n, r)-modules, where Λ• (n, r)
is the set of weak compositions (λ1 , λ2 , . . . , λn ) such that if λi = 0 for some i, then λj = 0
for all j > i.
   In Section 3, we construct simple S0 (n, r)-modules {Sλ | λ ∈ Λ• (n, r)} by determining
the tops of the projective indecomposable modules (Theorem 3.11). We present a basis
of Sλ parametrized by column block diagonal matrices, and we show that this basis has
several notable properties (Lemma 3.9). As an immediate consequence of Theorem 3.11,
we obtain an explicit description of the radicals of projective indecomposable S0 (n, r)-
modules (Corollary 3.12).
   In Section 4, we investigate functorial relations among the module categories U0 (gln )-mod,
S0 (n, r)-mod, and Hr (0)-mod. Here, U0 (gln ) denotes the degenerate analogue of the quan-
tum group Uq (gln ) introduced by Krob and Thibon [17]. Letting V0 := Cn , the tensor space
V0 ⊗r carries the structure of a (U0 (gln ), Hr (0))-bimodule, which defines the functor
               Fn,r : Hr (0)-mod −→ U0 (gln )-mod, M 7→ V0 ⊗r ⊗Hr (0) M.

under the functors Gn,r and Ψn,r . To begin, we recall the simple and projective inde-
composable Hr (0)-modules, which are naturally indexed by the strong compositions of r.
For each strong composition α of r, we denote by Fα and Rα the simple and projective
indecomposable Hr (0)-modules corresponding to α (for details, see Subsection 4.1). As a

   In Section 5, we discuss the symmetries of the Cartan matrix of H0 (n, r) and its relation
to the Cartan matrix of S0 (n, r). By applying (anti)involution twists, we first show that
the Cartan matrix of H0 (n, r) is constant on each D4 ×Z2 -orbit (Proposition 5.4). Next, we
prove that for 0 ≤ n ≤ r, the Cartan matrix of S0 (n, r) can be obtained from that of Hr (0)
by restricting the index set from all strong compositions of r to Λ• (n, r) (Theorem 5.5).

2.3. A Z-basis and a presentation for S0 (n, r). This subsection introduces a basis for
S0 (n, r), describes the multiplication rules among its elements, and presents a defining
presentation of the algebra.
   Beilinson, Lusztig, and MacPherson [2] provided a geometric construction of certain
finite-dimensional quotients of the quantized enveloping algebra Uq (gln ) using double flag
varieties. Later, Du [9] observed that these quotients are isomorphic to the q-Schur alge-
bras Sq (n, ·), thereby giving a geometric realization of Sq (n, r).
   In a similar manner, Jensen and Su [12] provided a geometric realization of the 0-Schur
algebra S0 (n, r). Specifically, they constructed a Z-algebra that is isomorphic to S0 (n, r),
with a basis given by the set F × F/GL(V ) of GL(V )-orbits in the double flag variety
F ×F. Here, V is an r-dimensional vector space over C, and F is the variety of n-step flags
in V over C. Since there exists a natural one-to-one correspondence between F ×F/GL(V )
and the set Mn (r) of n × n matrices with nonnegative integer entries whose total sum of
entries is r, it follows that S0 (n, r) admits a basis indexed by elements of Mn (r), given by
                                      {eA | A ∈ Mn (r)}.                                   (2.2)
The precise definition of eA can be found in [12, Section 1]. In the present paper, this
basis is referred to as the standard basis for S0 (n, r).

     (1) We have an S0 (n, r)-module isomorphism
