                                                  Abstract. In this paper, we report on the τ -tilting ﬁniteness of some classes of ﬁnite-
                                                  dimensional algebras over an algebraically closed ﬁeld, including symmetric algebras of
                                                  polynomial growth, 0-Hecke algebras and 0-Schur algebras. Consequently, we ﬁnd that de-
                                                  rived equivalence preserves the τ -tilting ﬁniteness over symmetric algebras of polynomial
                                                  growth, and self-injective cellular algebras of polynomial growth are τ -tilting ﬁnite. Fur-
                                                  thermore, the representation-ﬁniteness and τ -tilting ﬁniteness over 0-Hecke algebras and
                                                  0-Schur algebras (with few exceptions) coincide.




                                                                                    1. Introduction
                                              Throughout this paper, we will use the symbol k to denote an algebraically closed field. An
                                           algebra is assumed to be an associative basic connected finite-dimensional k-algebra, unless
                                           otherwise specified. Modules are always finitely generated and right. For an algebra A, we
                                           denote by mod-A (resp. proj-A) the category of modules (resp. projective modules) over A.
                                           We also denote by Kb (proj-A) the perfect derived category of A. Finally, we use the symbols
                                           D and τ for the standard k-dual and the Auslander-Reiten translation, respectively.
                                              Recently, Adachi, Iyama and Reiten proposed τ -tilting theory in [AIR14] to classify torsion
                                           classes in mod-A. They introduced a new class of A-modules called support τ -tilting modules
                                           (see Section 2 for the definition), which are in bijective correspondence with several sets
                                           of important objects arising in representation theory, such as two-term silting complexes
                                           in Kb (proj-A) ([AIR14]), functorially finite torsion classes in mod-A ([AIR14]), left finite
                                           semibricks in mod-A ([As20]), wide subcategories of mod-A ([MS17]), some t-structures and
                                           co-t-structures in Kb (proj-A) ([KY14]), and so on. In this context, those algebras admitting
                                           only finitely many support τ -tilting modules, which are said to be τ -tilting finite, have been
                                           actively researched, for example, see [Ad16(2), AAC18, AH21, AHMW21, MW21, W22(1),
                                           Z20]. Demonet, Iyama, and Jasso originally studied τ -tilting finite algebras and characterized
                                           such algebras in [DIJ19].
                                              It is known that τ -tilting finiteness is preserved under Morita equivalence, but usually
                                           not preserved under derived equivalence. Examples are easy to find: a path algebra A of
                                           a Euclidean quiver is τ -tilting infinite; taking a tilting A-module M having both a prepro-
                                           jective and a preinjective direct summand, then the endomorphism algebra B = EndA M is
                                           representation-finite (see [ASS06, VIII, Proposition 4.4]) and hence, τ -tilting finite.

   The notion of symmetric algebras plays a prominent role in representation theory. An
algebra A is said to be symmetric if there exists a non-degenerate symmetric k-bilinear form
(−, −) : A × A → k such that (ab, c) = (a, bc), for all a, b, c ∈ A. Classical examples
of symmetric algebras include group algebras of finite groups, Brauer graph algebras, and
trivial extension algebras. Here, the trivial extension Triv(A) of an algebra A is defined by
Triv(A) := A ⋉ D(A) with multiplication (a, f )(b, g) = (ab, ag + f b) for a, b ∈ A, f, g ∈ D(A).
One may easily find that each algebra A is a certain quotient of its trivial extension Triv(A),
indicating the significance of symmetric algebras in representation theory.
   One may expect thorough research on the behavior of symmetric algebras in τ -tilting
theory. There are outcomes for some subclasses of symmetric algebras: Aihara shows in
[Ai13] that any representation-finite symmetric algebra is tilting-connected; Adachi, Aihara
and Chan provide in [AAC18] a combinatorial classification of two-term tilting complexes
for Brauer graph algebras; Asashiba, Mizuno and Nakashima in [AMN20] along with Aoki
in [Ao21] use different approaches to determine the number of two-term tilting complexes
for Brauer tree algebras; Koshio and Kozakai classify in [KK21] support τ -tilting modules
for certain block algebras of finite groups; Li and Zhang study in [LZ22] support τ -tilting
modules over trivial extension algebras; Adachi and Aoki compute in [AA23] the number of
two-term silting complexes for symmetric algebras with radical cube zero; to name a few.
   We have learned from [AAC18] that the τ -tilting finiteness of Brauer graph algebras
is preserved under derived equivalence. From this result, it is reasonable to consider the
following question:
Question 1.1. Does derived equivalence preserve the τ -tilting finiteness over symmetric
algebras?
   In this paper, the main strategy addressing Question 1.1 is given in terms of the rep-
resentation type of symmetric algebras. Due to Drozd’s Tame-Wild Dichotomy [Dr80], a
finite-dimensional algebra over an algebraically closed field is either tame or wild. We then
consider the τ -tilting finiteness of symmetric algebras in different types outlined in the fol-
lowing hierarchy (see [S06, (1.5)] for more details).

   In the case of representation-finite symmetric algebras, a positive answer to Question 1.1
is straightforward, since any representation-finite algebra is τ -tilting finite. As the first main
result in this paper, we provide an affirmative answer to Question 1.1 for representation-
infinite symmetric algebras of polynomial growth, as follows.

Theorem 1 (= Theorem 3.12). Let A and B be two representation-infinite symmetric alge-
bras of polynomial growth. If A is derived equivalent to B, then the following conditions are
equivalent.
   (1) A is τ -tilting finite.
   (2) B is τ -tilting finite.
   (3) The Cartan matrix CA (or equivalently, CB ) is non-singular.
   It is noteworthy that Question 1.1 remains unanswered for tame symmetric algebras of
non-polynomial growth and wild symmetric algebras.
   As an application of Theorem 1, one can determine the τ -tilting finiteness of self-injective
cellular algebras of polynomial growth, which are classified in [AKMW20] up to Morita
equivalence (under the assumption that the characteristic of k is not 2).
Corollary 2 (= Corollary 3.14). Suppose the characteristic of k is not 2. Then, any algebra
which is Morita equivalent to a self-injective cellular algebra of polynomial growth is τ -tilting
finite.
   Another motivation of this paper is to determine the τ -tilting finiteness of 0-Hecke algebras
and 0-Schur algebras, see Section 4 for precise definitions. This may be independent of
Question 1.1, but this is meaningful for the research related to Hecke algebras and q-Schur
algebras. In fact, the τ -tilting finiteness of Hecke algebras of type A and classical Schur
algebras has already been determined in [ALS23, AW21, W22(2)].
   Let q be an indeterminate element. For a finite Coxeter system (W, S), the Iwahori-Hecke
algebra Hk,q (W ) is the k-algebra generated by {Ts | s ∈ S} with quadratic relations and
braid relations. Then, H0 (W ) := Hk,0 (W ) is called the 0-Hecke algebra. The structure
and the representation theory of H0 (W ) have been extensively studied, for example, see
[Ca86, F05, N79]. As the second main result in this paper, we classify 0-Hecke algebras in
terms of τ -tilting finiteness as follows.
Theorem 3 (= Theorem 4.4). Let W be an irreducible finite Coxeter group. Then, the
following statements are equivalent.
   (1) The 0-Hecke algebra H0 (W ) is τ -tilting finite.
   (2) W is one of types A1 , A2 , B2 and I2 (m), for any m ≥ 5.
   We denote by Sr the symmetric group of degree r, which is actually a Coxeter group of
type Ar−1 . According to [DY12, (4.3.3)], the 0-Schur algebra S0 (n, r) is Morita equivalent to
a certain idempotent truncation of the 0-Hecke algebra H0 (Sr ), for any n, r ≥ 2. This class
of algebras has been studied by various authors, such as Donkin [Do98], Su [Su10], Deng
and Yang [DY12], etc. As the third main result in this paper, we determine the τ -tilting
finiteness of 0-Schur algebras as follows.
Theorem 4 (= Theorem 4.8). The following assertions hold.
   (1) For n ≥ 3, the 0-Schur algebra S0 (n, r) is τ -tilting finite if and only if r = 2, 3.
   (2) For n = 2 and r ≥ 2, the 0-Schur algebra S0 (2, r) is τ -tilting finite.

                                          2. Preliminaries
   Any finite-dimensional k-algebra A can be presented as a bound quiver algebra kQ/I,
where Q is a finite (connected) quiver and I is an admissible ideal of the path algebra kQ.
                                                                                          α
A quiver is said to be acyclic if it admits no oriented cycle. We will commonly write a −→b
to indicate that an arrow α has source a and target b. We refer to some textbooks, e.g.,
[ASS06], for more background materials on the representation theory of quivers.
   We recall some crucial properties of symmetric algebras. It is well-known that any algebra
that is derived equivalent to a symmetric algebra is also symmetric, see [R91, Corollary
5.3]. Moreover, the following fact is the reason why we can consider symmetric algebras in
different hierarchies of representation types.
Proposition 2.1 ([R89(2), Corollary 2.2] and [KZ00, Corollary 2]). Let A be a symmet-
ric algebra and B an algebra that is derived equivalent to A. If A is tame (respectively,
representation-finite, domestic, of polynomial growth), then B has the same property.
   We then review the basic definitions in τ -tilting theory and collect some results on τ -tilting
finite algebras, which are needed for this paper. We refer to [AIR14, DIJ19, DIRRT17,
EJR18] for more aspects related to τ -tilting theory.
Definition 2.2. Let M ∈ mod-A and |M| be the number of isomorphism classes of inde-
composable direct summands of M.
    (1) M is τ -rigid if HomA (M, τ M) = 0.
    (2) M is τ -tilting if it is τ -rigid with |M| = |A|.
    (3) M is support τ -tilting if there is an idempotent e ∈ A such that M is a τ -tilting
        module over A/AeA.
   We denote by sτ -tilt A the set of isomorphism classes of basic support τ -tilting A-modules;
it admits a poset structure, see [AIR14, Subsection 2.4] for details. We recall that A is
called τ -tilting finite if sτ -tilt A is a finite set, and otherwise, A is said to be τ -tilting infinite.
According to [DIJ19], an algebra A is τ -tilting finite if and only if A has only finitely many
isomorphism classes of bricks.
Example 2.3. We give some examples of τ -tilting finite/infinite algebras.
    (1) A local algebra A has exactly two basic support τ -tilting modules A and 0. Thus,
        any local algebra is τ -tilting finite.
    (2) A representation-finite algebra is τ -tilting finite.
    (3) Any module lying on a preprojective (or preinjective) component is a brick. Thus,
        representation-infinite algebras with preprojective components are τ -tilting infinite
        (see [Mo23, Remark 2.8]). Moreover, the path algebra kQ of an acyclic quiver Q is
        τ -tilting finite if and only if Q is a Dynkin quiver (see [Ad16(2), Theorem 2.6]).
   Let I be a two-sided ideal of A and e an idempotent of A. By considering the following
fully faithful functors

  The Cartan matrix CA of an algebra A = kQ/I is the matrix whose (i, j)-entry is
dimk HomA (Pi , Pj ), where Pi is the indecomposable projective A-module associated with
the vertex i in Q. If the determinant of CA is equal to zero, it is referred to as a singular
matrix ; otherwise, it is termed as a non-singular matrix. If A is symmetric, then CA is a
symmetric matrix and furthermore, CA is said to be positive definite if all eigenvalues of CA
are positive. The following statement is a well-known fact, e.g., see [R89(1)].

Proposition 2.4. Let A be a symmetric algebra and B an algebra which is derived equivalent

3.1. Reduction methods. We need the following two crucial lemmas.

Lemma 3.1. Let A be a symmetric algebra whose Cartan matrix CA is positive definite.
Assume that the entries of the Cartan matrix CB are bounded for any algebra B which is
derived equivalent to A, that is, there is a positive integer m such that all entries of CB are
less than m. Then, all algebras which are derived equivalent to A are τ -tilting finite.

Proof. By our assumption, it follows from [EJR18, Theorem 13] that A is τ -tilting finite.
Let B be an algebra that is derived equivalent to A. Then, by Proposition 2.4, we deduce
that CB is also positive definite. We apply [EJR18, Theorem 13] again and conclude that B
is τ -tilting finite.                                                                   

Lemma 3.2. Let A be a τ -tilting infinite algebra. Then, so is Triv(A).

Proof. Since there is a surjective algebra homomorphism Triv(A) → A, the assertion follows
immediately.                                                                            

                       4. 0-Hecke algebras and 0-Schur algebras
  In this section, we focus on 0-Hecke algebras and 0-Schur algebras. We mention that these
algebras are not necessarily connected and symmetric. In particular, a characterization of
0-Hecke algebras being symmetric is given in [F05, Proposition 4.5].

4.1. 0-Hecke algebras. We recall some background of Coxeter groups and Hecke algebras.
We refer to the textbook [BB05] for more details.
  Let W be a Coxeter group with the generating set S = {s1 , s2 , . . . , sn }, that is, the group
with a presentation of the form

                                W = hs1 , s2 , . . . , sn | (si sj )mij = 1i,

where mii = 1 and mij ∈ {2, 3, 4, . . .} for i 6= j. The pair (W, S) is called a Coxeter system.
We assign to a Coxeter system (W, S) a Coxeter diagram Γ(W, S) as follows: the vertex
set of Γ(W, S) is in bijection with S; two vertices si and sj of Γ(W, S) are joined by an
edge whenever mij ≥ 3, then this edge is labeled by mij and we omit the label on the
edge si      sj if mij = 3. A Coxeter system (W, S) is said to be irreducible if its Coxeter
diagram Γ(W, S) is connected. A Coxeter system (W, S) is said to be finite if Γ(W, S) is a
disjoint union of types An (n ≥ 1), Bn = Cn (n ≥ 2), Dn (n ≥ 4), E6 , E7 , E8 , F4 , H3 , H4
and I2 (m) (m ≥ 5); this gives a complete classification of finite Coxeter groups, see [Co91].
   Let (W, S) be a finite Coxeter system and q an indeterminate element. The Iwahori-Hecke
algebra (or briefly, Hecke algebra) Hk,q (W ) is the k-algebra generated by {Ts | s ∈ S} with
the following two kinds of relations:
     • Quadratic relations: Ts2 = (q − 1)Ts + q, for any s ∈ S.
     • Braid relations: Ts Tt Ts Tt · · · = Tt Ts Tt Ts · · ·, for any s, t ∈ S.
                        |     {z       } |        {z       }
                             mst factors       mts factors

It is well-known that if w = s1 s2 · · · sr is a reduced expression of w ∈ W , then Tw =
Ts1 Ts2 · · · Tsr is well-defined. The Hecke algebra Hk,q (W ) is a q-deformation of the group
algebra kW . Indeed, Hk,1 (W ) ≃ kW if q = 1. Another natural specialization of Hk,q (W ) is
the case q = 0. We call H0 (W ) := Hk,0 (W ) the 0-Hecke algebra.
   Our aim in this subsection is to classify H0 (W ) by τ -tilting finiteness. To do this, it
suffices to check the quiver of H0 (W ), say QW , as we shall explain below.
   Let (W, S) be a finite Coxeter system with S = {si | i ∈ I} and I = {1, 2, . . . , n}.
According to [N79, Theorem 4.21] and [F05, Theorem 5.1], the quiver QW of H0 (W ) is
obtained as follows:
     • The vertex set is {vJ | J ⊆ I}.
