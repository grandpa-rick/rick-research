## 1. Presentation being tested

We eliminate T_0 = πT_{m−1}π^{-1} from the extended affine Hecke algebra of GL_m. Its generators are then T_1..T_{m−1} and π^{±1}, with these relations:
- (a) (T_i − t)(T_i + 1) = 0.
- (b) the braid relation T_iT_{i+1}T_i = T_{i+1}T_iT_{i+1}, and far commutation T_iT_k = T_kT_i for |i−k| ≥ 2.
- (c) πT_iπ^{-1} = T_{i+1} for 1 ≤ i ≤ m−2.
- (d) π²T_{m−1}π^{-2} = T_1.
- (e) π is invertible.

The remaining affine braid relations, the ones involving T_0, follow from (b) by conjugating with π^{-1} or π^{-2} for m ≥ 3. The consequence π^m central was also checked. **The locator for this presentation is UNVERIFIED** (candidates: Macdonald CUP Tracts 157 ch. 3; Lusztig JAMS 1989 §3). This file checks only that the operators satisfy the relations. It does not check that these relations form the textbook presentation.

## 3. Hand remarks (one line each; not separately audited)

- **(d) for π_0.** π_0²F = F(X_3,…,X_m, sX_1, sX_2). So in π_0²T_{m−1}F, T_{m−1} acts on the slots (sX_1, sX_2). The divided-difference factor sX_2/(sX_1 − sX_2) is invariant under the joint scaling by s, so this equals T_1π_0²F.
- **(d) for twisted π.** π² = X_1X_2·π_0², and X_1X_2 is s_1-symmetric, so T_1 commutes with multiplication by it.
- **(e).**
  - π_0 is a ring automorphism of Pol, since s is a unit in ℚ(s,t).
  - The twisted π has image inside X_1·Pol, so it is **not surjective on Pol**: 1 ∉ im π. It is invertible on Laurent polynomials.
  - So for the twisted π, Pol carries an action only of the subalgebra generated by T_i^{±1} and π, which does not include π^{-1}. The full extended affine Hecke algebra acts on ℚ(s,t)[X^{±1}], and Pol is a submodule for that subalgebra.
  - The Y_i words use π but never π^{-1}. So any identity among the Y_i that holds in the abstract algebra holds on Laurent polynomials, and therefore restricts to Pol.
  - For π_0 there is no issue: Pol is a genuine module.

## 4. (C3) sanity: triangularity and spectrum of the untwisted Y_i (`tri_check.log`)

Scope: m=3 with d ≤ 3, and m=4 with d ≤ 2. The full matrices of all Y_i on monomials were computed symbolically.

- **Order.** Every off-diagonal term x^μ of Y_i x^λ satisfies one of two conditions (zero counterexamples):
  - μ^+ < λ^+ strictly in dominance; or
  - μ^+ = λ^+ and inv(μ) > inv(λ), where inv = #{i<j : μ_i < μ_j}. So the lower terms within an orbit are *less* decreasing.

  Counts:

  | (m, d) | dominance-lower terms | same-orbit-more-inversions terms |
  |---|---|---|
  | (3, 2) | 7 | 6 |
  | (3, 3) | 23 | 14 |
  | (4, 2) | 16 | 18 |

  The union support graph is acyclic in every case. So the Y_i are simultaneously triangular with respect to (dominance of λ^+, then fewer inversions = higher). This matches the usual Cherednik order, with the decreasing composition on top within an orbit.
- **Diagonal.** The diagonal is y_i(λ) = s^{λ_i} t^{b_i(λ)}, a pure monomial, with

  b_i(λ) = #{j : λ_j > λ_i} + #{j < i : λ_j = λ_i}.

  This b(λ) is a permutation of (0,…,m−1). The formula was fitted on the first run and then checked on all 35 compositions: 35/35. The s-exponents equal λ exactly in every case.
- **Simple joint spectrum** on each Pol_d: ✓ for all (m, d) tested. Generically this is immediate, because the s-exponents recover λ.
- **Twisted Y.** Triangularity was not tested for the twisted Y. Y^• raises degree by 1 (Y^•_1 = X_1Y_1), so it is not triangular on Pol_d. Per 216b, (C3) is needed only for the untwisted Y.

## 5. Verdict

- Both π (untwisted and twisted) satisfy all of (a)–(d) on the tested monomials. (d) holds exactly, with no scalar or q/s-shift.
- On (e):

  - Twisted π is invertible only on Laurent Pol. The abstract transfer still works through the Laurent module, because the Y-words use π but not π^{-1}.
- (C1) is reconfirmed for both π.
- (C3) for the untwisted Y_i is confirmed computationally: triangular in the (λ^+ dominance, then fewer inversions) order, with diagonal s^{λ_i}t^{b_i(λ)} and simple spectrum (m=3 d≤3, m=4 d≤2).
