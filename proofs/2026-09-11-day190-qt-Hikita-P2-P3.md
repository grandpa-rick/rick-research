## What "Hikita's recipe" actually is (paper §3, §4)

There are two equivalent formulations in Hikita, both fully explicit:

**(a) Direct definition via partial symmetrizers (Def 4.4–4.5, Thm A).**  For a
unit-interval graph Γ with e ∈ E_n, and any m ≥ n,

$$
X_\Gamma^{(m)}(q,t) := t^{n(m-1)}\, S^{(m)}_{1,e(1)}\, S^{(m)}_{2,e(2)} \cdots S^{(m)}_{n,e(n)}(X_1 X_2 \cdots X_n),
$$

where the $S^{(m)}_{i,e}$ are explicit elements of the affine Hecke algebra $H_m$
of $GL_m$ acting at level one on $\mathbb Q_{q,t}[X^{\pm 1}]_{(m)}$ via
$T_i \bullet F = t \cdot s_i(F) + (t-1)(F - s_iF)/(1 - X_i X_{i+1}^{-1})$ and
$\Pi \bullet F = X_1 F(X_2, \dots, X_m, q^{-1}X_1)$.  The stability property
$\pi_{m,m'}(X_\Gamma^{(m)}) = X_\Gamma^{(m')}$ gives a well-defined
$X_\Gamma(q,t) \in \Lambda_{q,t}$.

**(b) Recipe via the quantum map $\mathfrak q$ (Thm B(iii)+(iv)).** This is the
"recipe" Browse 140 identified.  Define $\mathfrak q_{(m)}: \mathbb Q_{q,t}[Y^{\pm 1}]_{(m)}
\to \mathbb Q_{q,t}[X^{\pm 1}]_{(m)}$ by $\mathfrak q_{(m)}(F(Y)) := F(Y) \bullet 1$,
where $Y_i \in H_m$ are the Cherednik–Bernstein elements (eq (3) of the paper).
Then:

$$
X_\Gamma(q,t) \;=\; \mathfrak q\bigl(Y_\Gamma(t)\bigr),
$$

where $Y_\Gamma(t)$ is the ordinary chromatic quasi-symmetric function of $\Gamma$
in the $Y$-variables.  In practice this means: write $Y_\Gamma(t) = \sum_\lambda
c_\lambda(t)\, e_\lambda(Y)$ in the ordinary $e$-basis, and apply Thm B(iv):

$$
\mathfrak q(e_\lambda(Y)) = t^{\sum_i \binom{\lambda_i}{2}}\, e^{(q,t)}_\lambda(X),
\qquad e^{(q,t)}_\lambda(X) := e_{\lambda_1}(X) \star \cdots \star e_{\lambda_l}(X),
$$

where $\star$ is the quantum multiplication.  Hence

$$
\boxed{\; X_\Gamma(q,t) \;=\; \sum_\lambda t^{\sum_i \binom{\lambda_i}{2}}\, c_\lambda(t)\, e^{(q,t)}_\lambda(X). \;}
$$

The star product reduces via the Pieri rule (Thm 3.12):
$e_1(X) \star e_r(X) = (1 - q^{-1})[r+1]_t\, e_{r+1}(X) + q^{-1}\, e_1(X)\,e_r(X)$.
(No Pieri for $e_a \star e_b$ with $a,b \ge 2$ is given.)

Hikita's Example 4.6 confirms both routes agree; my SymPy check for $P_3$ gets
`diff = 0` against Ex 4.6.

## Explicit answers

Ordinary CQFs (enumerated):

- $X_{P_1}(t) = e_1$.
- $X_{P_2}(t) = (1+t)\,e_2$.  (SW: proper 2-colorings with asc = 0 or 1.)
- $X_{P_3}(t) = (1 + t + t^2)\,e_3 + t\,e_{2,1}$.

Apply $\mathfrak q$ (multiply the $e_\lambda(Y)$-coefficient by $t^{\sum \binom{\lambda_i}{2}}$
and reinterpret $e_\lambda$ as $e^{(q,t)}_\lambda$):

$$
\boxed{\; X_{P_2}(x;q,t) \;=\; t(1+t)\, e_2(X) \;}
$$

$$
\boxed{\; X_{P_3}(x;q,t) \;=\; t^3(1+t+t^2)\, e_3(X) \;+\; t^2\, \bigl(e_1(X) \star e_2(X)\bigr). \;}
$$

Unfolding the second via Pieri (Thm 3.12):

$$
X_{P_3}(x;q,t) = q^{-1} t^2\, e_1 e_2 \;+\; q^{-1} t^2\,(1+t+t^2)\bigl(-1 + q(1+t)\bigr)\, e_3.
$$

Exact match to Hikita Ex 4.6 (which computes the same graph as $\Gamma$ for $e = (0,0,1)$;
Hessenberg $(2,3,3)$; this **is** the directed path P_3, aka Dynkin A_3).

## Does Rick's (Re)-form survive to $(q,t)$?

**No, not in the obvious way.**  Rick's (Re) form
$X_{P_n}(q) = e_n + q \sum_{k=2}^n [k-1]_q\, e_k\, X_{P_{n-k}}(q)$
uses the **ordinary** product $e_k \cdot X_{P_{n-k}}$.  In $(q,t)$-land the natural
product for concatenating unit-interval graphs is $\star$ (Hikita **Corollary 4.10**;
compare Thm B(ii) which defines $\star$ and Thm A(ii) which is the stability property
$\pi_{m,m'}(X^{(m)}) = X^{(m')}$, a distinct statement):
$X_{\Gamma \cup \Gamma'}(q,t) = X_\Gamma(q,t) \star X_{\Gamma'}(q,t)$.  A directed
path $P_n$ is **not** a disjoint union of two smaller unit-interval graphs, so the
$\star$-product does not directly give a $P_n$-recursion.

Attempting the naive lift $X_{P_n}(q,t) \stackrel{?}{=} e_n + \phi(q,t) \sum_k [k-1]_?\, e_k \star X_{P_{n-k}}(q,t)$:

- At $n = 2$: $X_{P_2}(q,t) = t(1+t) e_2$.  For this to equal $e_2 + \phi \cdot [1] \cdot e_2 \star 1$
  we need $\phi = t(1+t) - 1 = t + t^2 - 1$.  Not a clean $[1]_?$ specialisation.
- At $n = 3$ this would then determine $X_{P_3}(q,t)$ from $e_3, e_2, e_1 \star X_{P_1}$;
  the required $\phi$-coefficient at $k=3$ vs $k=2$ does not admit a single common form.

**Conclusion:**  In Hikita's $(q,t)$-CQF framework, the correct expansion is
$X_{P_n}(q,t) = \sum_\lambda t^{\sum \binom{\lambda_i}{2}}\, c_\lambda^{P_n}(t)\, e^{(q,t)}_\lambda(X)$,
with $c_\lambda^{P_n}(t)$ the Ellzey $e$-coefficient — the coefficients are
independent of $q$ (Hikita's Thm A(iv) statement in the introduction).
Rick's (Re) recursion is a statement about ordinary product in the $t$-only
world (equivalently, at $q = 1$ after applying $N^{-1}$), and does **not**
lift to a clean $\star$-recursion on $P_n$'s.

## Where "Rick has the slot" now sits

The FPSAC 2027 (q,t)-lift angle from Browse 140 becomes: the interesting
combinatorial object is the $e^{(q,t)}_\lambda$-coefficients $t^{\sum \binom{\lambda_i}{2}}
c_\lambda^{P_n}(t)$, i.e., the Ellzey $e$-coefficients themselves,
$t$-weighted by a shape factor.  Any (Re)-style recursion must be phrased
in the ordinary product (which is a specialisation of $\star$ to $q = 1$),
where Rick's Day 187 formula already lives.  A *new* recursion phrased with
$\star$ is an open direction; nothing in Hikita rules it out but nothing there
supplies it either.

## Blockers / open

  recursive tool would need it.
