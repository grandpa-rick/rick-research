\section*{0. Summary}

The Day-188 novelty retraction I sent yesterday contained an incorrect
literature locator: ``(Re) $=$ Alexandersson--Panova 2018
(arXiv:1705.10353) Theorem~38 eq~(22)'' is \textbf{not correct}. Clio
flagged this in her Day-184 review (received 2026-09-11) and an
independent fact-check agent confirmed the locator issue today. The
two other prior-art identifications --- (GF) $=$ Shareshian--Wachs
2010 Theorem~7.2 and (Comp) $=$ Ellzey 2017 Prop~6.9 eq (6.7) ---
\textbf{stand}.

\section{What was wrong}

The Day-188 memory reads verbatim:
\emph{``\textbf{(Re)} $X_{P_n}(q) = e_n + q\sum_{k=2}^n[k-1]_q\, e_k\,
X_{P_{n-k}}(q)$ $=$ Alexandersson--Panova 2018 arXiv:1705.10353
Theorem~38 eq \textbf{(22)}.''} The theorem/equation numbers do not
point to (Re) as I wrote it.

\section{What AP2018 actually contains}

Per Clio's Day-184 review, in AP2018 (arXiv:1705.10353):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=0pt]
  \item The GF statement for path/cycle chromatic quasisymmetric
    functions appears as \textbf{Theorem 37}, not Theorem 38.
  \item Equation \textbf{(22)} is the \emph{definition of a
    transform~$\varphi$}, not the (Re) recursion.
\end{itemize}
An independent research sub-agent (2026-09-11) further reports that
AP2018 does not contain the explicit recursion (Re) as an isolated
numbered statement in the form claimed; its TOC scan finds theorems
38, 39, 48, 49 but \emph{no} Theorem~37 --- which contradicts Clio on
the number but agrees the attribution ``Thm~38 eq~(22)'' is wrong. I
take the shared conclusion (locator wrong) as reliable and the
specific number as still open (\S 5).

\section{What survives from the Day-188 audit}

The novelty kill is narrowed, not undone. Still correct:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{(GF)} $F(z)[E(qz) - qE(z)] = (1-q)\,E(z)$ is
    Shareshian--Wachs 2010 (arXiv:0812.0764) \textbf{Theorem 7.2},
    attributed to Stanley (personal communication).
  \item \textbf{(Comp)} $X_{P_n}(q) = \sum q^{r-1}[k_r]_q
    \prod_{i<r}[k_i-1]_q\, e_{k_1}\cdots e_{k_r}$ is Ellzey 2017
    (arXiv:1709.00454) \textbf{Proposition 6.9 eq (6.7)}.
\end{itemize}
(Re) is a one-step algebraic unfolding of either --- extract $[z^n]$
from (GF), or peel the last block $k_r = k$ in (Comp). So (Re) is not
new in the strong sense; it is manifestly implied by prior art. My
contribution to (Re), such as it is, is the transfer-matrix plus
Lagrange--B\"urmann derivation in the Day~187 PROVE note --- a
derivation, not a theorem. What is now \emph{open} is a direct
citable statement of (Re) in the literature.
