## 1. Proof of T-shift identity

**Claim.** $T(e_1^a \cdot e_k) = [e_1 - k]_a \cdot e_k$ for all $a \ge 0$, $k \ge 1$.

**Method.** Exponential generating function.  Full write-up in
`code/day124/t_shift_proof.md`.  Sketch:

1. Newton: $\sum_a [x]_a t^a/a! = (1+t)^x$ (single variable).
2. $T$ acts monomial-wise and factors across variables; for any monomial
   $x^\beta$,
   $$T(e^{te_1}\cdot x^\beta) = \prod_i [x_i]_{\beta_i}(1+t)^{x_i-\beta_i}
     = (1+t)^{e_1 - |\beta|}\cdot T(x^\beta).$$
3. Extending by linearity, for *any homogeneous* $g$ of degree $d$,
   $$\boxed{\;\sum_a\frac{t^a}{a!}T(e_1^a\cdot g) = (1+t)^{e_1-d}\cdot T(g)\;}$$
4. For $g = e_k$: since $e_k$ is multilinear, $T(e_k) = e_k$; and $\deg e_k = k$.
   Extract coefficient of $t^a/a!$.  Done.

**Computational corroboration.** `code/day124/t_shift_proof.py` verifies the
master identity in Step 2 for 16 monomials $x^\beta$ (in $n=1,2,3,4$
variables, degrees up to 6 in $t$), then re-derives the $e_k$ identity for
$k=1..5$, $n=k, k+1$, $a=0..5$ — **all pass**.

## 2. Is $\Psi = T$?

**No.**  Concrete counterexample: with $n = 3$ variables,
$$\Psi(e_1) = s^*_{(1)} = x_1 + x_2 + x_3 - 3, \qquad T(e_1) = x_1+x_2+x_3.$$
Difference: $\Psi(e_1) - T(e_1) = -3 \ne 0$.

More generally, out of 20 tested partitions with $|\mu| \le 5$, $\ell(\mu) \le 3$,
**14 exhibit $\Psi(s_\mu) \ne T(s_\mu)$**.

## 3. But the structure is beautiful

Two structural facts hold in every tested case:

### (a) $T$ is the "top" of $\Psi$.

For every symmetric $f$, $\deg(\Psi(f) - T(f)) < \deg f$.  (Verified for
all $e_1^a e_k$ with $a \le 4$, $k \le 3$, 15/15 cases.)

Moreover the top-degree part of $\Psi(s_\mu)$ equals $s_\mu$ = top of
$T(s_\mu)$ = top of $s^*_\mu$ — this is a *classical* fact for shifted
Schurs and a *provable* fact for $T$ (from Step 2 above at $t=0$).

### (b) $\Psi$ and $T$ share $(1,1,2)$-weight on $e_1^a e_k$.

In the e-basis expansion, define $w_{112}(f) = \max\{a_1 + a_2 + 2a_3\}$ over
nonzero monomials $e_1^{a_1} e_2^{a_2} e_3^{a_3}$.  For $f = e_1^a e_k$
with $k \in \{1,2,3\}$, $a \in \{0,\ldots,4\}$:

| $a$ | $k$ | $w_{112}(T(e_1^a e_k))$ | $w_{112}(\Psi(e_1^a e_k))$ | equal? |
|-----|-----|-------------------------|----------------------------|--------|
| 0..4 | 1 | $a+1$ | $a+1$ | YES (5/5) |
| 0..4 | 2 | $a+1$ | $a+1$ | YES (5/5) |
| 0..4 | 3 | $a+2$ | $a+2$ | YES (5/5) |

**All 15/15 match.**

### (c) The correction $\Psi - T$ has a Bethe-like factorization.

The differences $T(e_1^a e_k) - \Psi(e_1^a e_k)$ factor as
$$c \cdot (e_1 - c_1)(e_1 - c_2)\cdots\cdot(\text{polynomial})$$
where $c_i$ appear to be shifts by consecutive integers.  E.g.
$$T(e_1^4 e_1) - \Psi(e_1^4 e_1) = 15(e_1-4)(e_1-3)(\cdots)$$
$$T(e_1^4 e_3) - \Psi(e_1^4 e_3) = 12\,e_3(e_1-6)(\cdots)$$
This is the *rho-shift* signature — strongly suggests $\Psi = T \circ \rho$
or $T \circ (\text{some substitution})$ modulo lower order.

## 4. Impact on Lemma 2

**Lemma 2 (filtration preservation) survives — the T-shift bound is a valid
proxy for the $\Psi$ bound.**

Reasoning:
- The map that matters for the E_j filtration is $\Psi$ (not $T$).
- But $\Psi(f)$ and $T(f)$ agree in $(1,1,2)$-weight for all tested
  $f = e_1^a e_k$.
- $T$-shift gives *explicit* bounds: $w_{112}(T(e_1^a e_k)) = a + w_{112}(e_k)$
  because $T(e_1^a e_k) = [e_1-k]_a e_k$ (only $e_1$ and $e_k$ appear, so
  $(1,1,2)$-weight $= a \cdot w(e_1) + w(e_k) = a + \max(1, 2\cdot[k\ge 3])$).

If we can prove the *structural* fact
"$w_{112}(\Psi(f)) \le w_{112}(T(f))$ for symmetric $f$"
then Lemma 2 reduces to the T-shift bound, which we now have in closed form.

Suggested path forward:
1. **Prove** $\deg(\Psi(f) - T(f)) < \deg f$ for all symmetric $f$.
   (This looks doable: the top of $s^*_\mu$ is $s_\mu = T(s_\mu)$-top.)
2. **Prove** $w_{112}(\Psi(f) - T(f)) \le w_{112}(T(f))$.  This is the harder
   claim — but the empirical pattern is airtight so far.
3. Combine (1)+(2) with T-shift: get Lemma 2 for $E_j$'s.

## 5. What does NOT hold

- $\Psi = T$: false, concretely.
- $\Psi(f) - T(f) = 0$ for $f$ multilinear: false ($f = e_2$ gives
  $\Psi(e_2) - T(e_2) = -e_1 + 1$).
- $\Psi = T + $ scalar shift: false (the correction depends nontrivially on $f$).
