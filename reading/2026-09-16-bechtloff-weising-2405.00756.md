## One-line summary

Milo Bechtloff Weising constructs an infinite family $\{\widetilde{W}_\lambda\}_{\lambda \in \mathbb{Y}}$ of graded cyclic $\mathcal{E}^+$-modules (positive elliptic Hall algebra), each with a distinguished basis $(\mathfrak{P}_T)_{T \in \Omega(\lambda)}$ of "generalized Macdonald functions." For $\lambda = \emptyset$ he recovers the standard $\mathcal{E}^+$-action on $\Lambda_{q,t}$, and $\mathfrak{P}_\mu = P_\mu(x; q^{-1}, t)$ up to nonzero scalar. §5 derives a Pieri rule for the action of $e_r^\bullet$ (Cor 5.10 / Thm 5.7).

## Setting (from Introduction and §4)

- **Algebra:** $\mathcal{E}^+$ = positive elliptic Hall algebra of Burban–Schiffmann. This is the subalgebra of $\mathcal{E}$ generated by $P_{r,\ell}$ with either $r \ge 1$, or ($r=0$ and $\ell \ne 0$).
- **Standard rep:** Schiffmann–Vasserot realized $\mathcal{E}^+$ as a limit of positive DAHAs in type $\mathrm{GL}_n$, giving a natural action on $\Lambda$ (recovering Cherednik). $P_{0,1} \in \mathcal{E}^+$ acts as the Macdonald operator $\Delta$.
- **New reps:** For each $\lambda \in \mathbb{Y}$, $\widetilde{W}_\lambda$ is built as a projective limit of spherical DAHA reps $W_{\lambda^{(n)}}$, using inflations $\lambda^{(n)} = (n - |\lambda|, \lambda_1, \ldots, \lambda_r)$ (Murnaghan's stabilizing sequences).
- **Underlying non-symmetric objects:** the $V_\lambda$ (Dunkl–Luque vector-valued DAHA modules) with $\mathfrak{S}_n$-invariant subspaces $W_\lambda = \epsilon^{(n)}(V_\lambda)$. The basis $F_\tau$ is indexed by $\mathrm{PSYT}_{\ge 0}(\lambda)$; $P_T$ is defined inside $W_\lambda$ for $T \in \mathrm{RSSYT}_{\ge 0}(\lambda)$ (Definition 4.4, Corollary 4.5).

## Key definitions and identifications

- **Basis $\mathfrak{P}_T$:** limit of $P_{T|_{\lambda^{(n)}}}$ (Definition 4.11). $T \in \Omega(\lambda)$: a labeling of $\lambda^{(\infty)}$ with finitely many nonzero entries, weakly decreasing right, strictly decreasing down.
- **$(q, t)$ parameters:** same $\mathbb{Q}(q, t)$ as Macdonald theory. Proposition 4.19 identifies $\widetilde{W}_\emptyset$ with $\Lambda_{q,t}$ (standard rep). **In the $\lambda=\emptyset$ case, $\Omega(\emptyset)$ is in canonical bijection with $\mathbb{Y}$ and $\mathfrak{P}_\mu = P_\mu(x; q^{-1}, t)$ up to a nonzero scalar** — the $q\to q^{-1}$ swap comes from Cherednik-operator conventions.
- **Multiplication operators (Definition 4.13):**
  - $p_r^\bullet := \lim_n (X_1^r + \cdots + X_n^r)$
  - $e_r^\bullet := \lim_n e_r(X_1, \ldots, X_n)$
  These are well-defined on $\widetilde{W}_\lambda$ by Proposition 3.14 (stability).
- **Macdonald operators:** $\widetilde{\Delta}_\ell^{(\infty)} = \lim_n(\sum_j (\theta_j^{(n)})^\ell - \sum_{\Box \in \lambda^{(n)}} t^{\ell c(\Box)})$, acts diagonally on $\mathfrak{P}_T$ (Lemma 4.14). In the $\lambda=\emptyset$ case with $T \leftrightarrow \mu$, this recovers the standard Macdonald spectrum $\sum_i (q^{\mu_i} t^{i-1} - t^{i-1})$ — up to the same $q \to q^{-1}$ swap of parameters.

## Section 5 — The Pieri rule

### Theorem 5.7 (finite version)
For $T \in \mathrm{RSSYT}_{\ge 0}(\lambda)$ and $1 \le r \le n$:
$$e_r(X_1, \ldots, X_n) P_T = \sum_S d_{S,T}^{(r)} P_S$$
where $S$ ranges over RSSYT tableaux obtained from $T$ by **increasing by 1 the labels of $T$ in $r$ distinct boxes of $\lambda$** — with $S$ RSSYT (i.e. strict-column condition preserved).

### Corollary 5.10 (limit / Pieri rule)
For $T \in \Omega(\lambda)$ and $r \ge 1$:
$$e_r^\bullet \mathfrak{P}_T = \sum_{S \in \Omega(\lambda)} \mathfrak{d}_{S,T}^{(r)} \mathfrak{P}_S,$$
with $\mathfrak{d}_{S,T}^{(r)} = d_{S|_{\lambda^{(n)}}, T|_{\lambda^{(n)}}}^{(r)}$ for $n$ large. The set of nonzero $S$ is finite; $\mathrm{rk}(S) \le \mathrm{rk}(T) + r$.

### Coefficient formula (Theorem 5.7 body)
$$\frac{d_{S,T}^{(r)}}{t^{\binom{r}{2}} e_r(1, t, \ldots, t^{n-1}) K_S(q,t)} = \sum_{\substack{\tau \in \mathrm{PSYT}_{\ge 0}(\lambda; T)\\ \Psi^r(\tau) \in \mathrm{PSYT}_{\ge 0}(\lambda; S)}} t^{c_\tau(1) + \cdots + c_\tau(r)} \prod_{(\Box_1, \Box_2) \in \mathrm{Inv}(\tau)} \left(\frac{q^{T(\Box_1)} t^{c(\Box_1)+1} - q^{T(\Box_2)} t^{c(\Box_2)}}{q^{T(\Box_1)} t^{c(\Box_1)} - q^{T(\Box_2)} t^{c(\Box_2)}}\right)$$
$$\times \prod_{(\Box_1, \Box_2) \in \mathrm{Inv}(\Psi^r(\tau))} \left(\frac{q^{S(\Box_1)} t^{c(\Box_1)} - q^{S(\Box_2)} t^{c(\Box_2)}}{q^{S(\Box_1)} t^{c(\Box_1)} - q^{S(\Box_2)} t^{c(\Box_2)+1}}\right)$$

$K_T(q,t)$ is the auxiliary constant from Cor 5.5; $\Psi$ is the "shift" operator on PSYTs (Def 2.4).

### Combinatorial content (in the $\lambda=\emptyset$ case)
$\Omega(\emptyset) \leftrightarrow \mathbb{Y}$. Applying $e_r^\bullet = e_r$-multiplication to $\mathfrak{P}_\mu = P_\mu(x; q^{-1}, t)$ gives a sum over partitions $\nu$ where $\nu/\mu$ is a **horizontal $r$-strip** — because "increasing $r$ distinct labels of $T$ by 1" while preserving RSSYT (strict columns) is exactly a horizontal-strip growth. So Cor 5.10 in the $\lambda = \emptyset$ case IS (a version of) the classical Macdonald $e_r$-Pieri rule, restated in tableau-summation form.

## §5.3 — Non-vanishing
The main downstream use in the paper (Thm 5.16, Cor 5.17): $\mathfrak{d}_{T', T}^{(1)} \ne 0$ whenever $T \uparrow T'$ (single-box raise). Used to prove $\widetilde{W}_\lambda$ is cyclic (Cor 5.18).

## Notes for our Hikita comparison

- BW's basis $\mathfrak{P}_\mu$ ($\lambda = \emptyset$ case) equals $P_\mu(x; q^{-1}, t)$ up to scalar. This is the ordinary Macdonald $P$-basis at $(q^{-1}, t)$.
- BW's multiplication $e_r^\bullet$ in the $\lambda = \emptyset$ case is just **ordinary multiplication by $e_r$ inside $\Lambda_{q,t}$** — not a Hikita-type star product.
- Number of terms in $e_r \cdot P_\mu$: number of horizontal $r$-strips one can add to $\mu$. For $\mu = (\mu_1, \ldots, \mu_\ell)$ this is typically $\binom{\ell + r}{r}$-like — many more than $\min(r, \ell) + 1$.
- The general $\lambda \ne \emptyset$ case: $\widetilde{W}_\lambda$ is *not* $\Lambda_{q,t}$; it is a genuinely new $\mathcal{E}^+$-rep. Combinatorics is richer (labels not just in $\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ on a partition but on the whole $\lambda^{(\infty)}$).

## Verdict on relevance to Rick

BW's paper is about $\mathcal{E}^+$-representations and the ordinary Pieri rule inside them. **It does NOT give a Hikita-style star product on $\Lambda_{q,t}$**, and BW's Pieri rule has no bounded-term structure (it's a horizontal-strip sum). The Day 191 formula for $e_2 \star e_r$ under Hikita's $\star$ has exactly 3 nonzero terms, corresponding to partitions of shape $(a+b-k, k)$ for $k=0,1,2$; there is no analog of this collapse in Cor 5.10 under $\lambda = \emptyset$ specialization.
